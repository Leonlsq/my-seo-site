## TP-Link Wi-Fi 8 Routers Hit an FCC Wall — Here's Your Buying Game Plan

If you've been holding out for a shiny TP-Link Wi-Fi 8 (802.11bn) router this holiday season, don't hold your breath. The FCC has once again rebuffed TP-Link's attempt to certify its next-gen Wi-Fi 8 gear for US sale — this time over routers exceeding allowable transmit power limits, not the security concerns that dogged earlier filings. The practical takeaway for you: **no TP-Link Wi-Fi 8 router is legally sellable in the US right now**, and the timeline for approval is unknown. So instead of waiting on a product that may not ship for months, this guide breaks down what actually happened, whether Wi-Fi 8 is worth waiting for at all, and the concrete Wi-Fi 7 alternatives you can buy today that won't leave you stranded.

## What the FCC Ruling Actually Means for You

The FCC's equipment authorization process (under 47 CFR Part 15) requires every radio-emitting device to pass certification before sale. TP-Link's Wi-Fi 8 routers reportedly exceeded the permitted power spectral density and EIRP limits for the 6 GHz band. That's a technical compliance failure, not a ban on the company — but it blocks retail availability until TP-Link re-engineers the radios and re-submits.

Here's the practical fallout:

- **No US launch date.** Certification can take weeks to months per revision cycle. Expect Q1–Q2 2027 at the earliest for a compliant re-submission.
- **Import risk.** Buying gray-market units from overseas voids warranties and may violate FCC rules on unauthorized RF devices.
- **Existing TP-Link Wi-Fi 7 gear is unaffected.** The Archer BE series and Deco BE mesh units already carry valid FCC IDs and remain fully legal and supported.

### Quick Reality Check: Wi-Fi 8 vs. Wi-Fi 7 at a Glance

| Spec | Wi-Fi 7 (802.11be) | Wi-Fi 8 (802.11bn) |
|---|---|---|
| Max channel width | 320 MHz | 320 MHz (unchanged) |
| Max theoretical PHY rate | ~46 Gbps | ~100 Gbps (target) |
| Primary goal | Raw throughput | Reliability & latency |
| Bands | 2.4 / 5 / 6 GHz | 2.4 / 5 / 6 GHz |
| Real-world benefit for most homes | Huge (vs. Wi-Fi 6) | Marginal without dense-device load |
| US retail availability | Widely available | Blocked (TP-Link), limited (others) |

The key insight: **Wi-Fi 8 is a reliability upgrade, not a speed revolution.** It targets multi-access-point coordination and latency under congestion. Unless you run 50+ devices or a dense mesh, Wi-Fi 7 already saturates most gigabit and multi-gig fiber connections.

## Common Issues and What You Should Know

- **"Can I still buy a TP-Link Wi-Fi 8 router overseas?"** Technically possible, but it's an uncertified RF device — no warranty, potential interference issues, and firmware region locks.
- **"Will TP-Link fix this quickly?"** Power-limit compliance usually requires hardware redesign (PA/filter changes), not just firmware. Budget for a longer wait than a simple resubmission.
- **"Is Wi-Fi 8 worth waiting for?"** For 95% of households, no. The reliability gains matter most in stadiums, offices, and ultra-dense smart homes. Wi-Fi 7 is the sweet spot through 2027.
- **Cost angle:** Wi-Fi 7 routers have dropped 20–30% in price as inventory matures. Waiting for Wi-Fi 8 means paying launch premiums later for marginal gains.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Why did the FCC reject TP-Link's Wi-Fi 8 routers?
The routers exceeded permissible transmit power limits (EIRP and power spectral density) in the 6 GHz band, failing Part 15 equipment certification. This is a technical compliance issue separate from earlier security-related scrutiny.

### Can I still buy a TP-Link Wi-Fi 8 router in the US?
No. Without FCC certification, TP-Link cannot legally sell Wi-Fi 8 routers in the US. Any units you find are gray-market imports with no warranty or regulatory protection.

### Is Wi-Fi 8 better than Wi-Fi 7?
Wi-Fi 8 (802.11bn) focuses on reliability, latency, and multi-AP coordination rather than raw speed. For typical homes, Wi-Fi 7 already delivers more bandwidth than most internet plans can use, making Wi-Fi 8 a marginal upgrade for most users.

### Should I wait for Wi-Fi 8 or buy Wi-Fi 7 now?
Buy Wi-Fi 7 now. It's certified, widely available, discounted, and future-proof for the next 3–5 years. Waiting for Wi-Fi 8 means indefinite delays with limited real-world benefit for standard home networks.

Don't wait on TP-Link's Wi-Fi 8 lineup — the FCC power-limit rejection pushes any US launch well into 2027, and the payoff for most users is small. **Buy a certified Wi-Fi 7 router today**: the TP-Link Archer BE800 if you want brand continuity and value, the Asus GT-BE98 for gaming, or Eero Max 7 for effortless mesh. Always verify the FCC ID before purchase, skip gray-market imports, and treat Wi-Fi 8 as a "nice to have in 2028," not a reason to stall your network upgrade now.
