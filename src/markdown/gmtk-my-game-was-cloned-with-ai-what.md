## Your Game Got Cloned With AI — Here's How to Actually Fight Back

If you're a solo dev or small studio whose game was scraped, cloned, and re-uploaded by an AI-generated copycat, you're not alone — and you're not powerless. The GMTK community thread that blew up this week ("My game was cloned with AI... what do I do?") captured a nightmare that's now routine in 2026: an asset-flipped, AI-generated clone appears on a storefront within days of your launch, undercutting your price and stealing your discoverability. The good news is that **storefront DMCA takedowns still work fast** — often within 24–72 hours — *if* you file them correctly with the right evidence. This guide walks you through the exact evidence package, filing sequence, and escalation path that gets clones removed instead of ignored.

## Step-by-Step Guide: Filing an Effective DMCA Takedown Against an AI Clone

The single biggest reason takedowns fail is **weak evidence of original ownership**. An AI clone rarely copies your code verbatim, so you must prove *substantial similarity* in protected expression — art, audio, level layouts, UI, and text. Follow this order:

### Step 1: Build Your Evidence Package (Do This First)
Before you touch a report form, assemble a timestamped folder:

- **Source files with creation metadata** — `.psd`, `.blend`, `.aseprite`, `.wav`, and Git commit history. Export a `git log --stat` to PDF.
- **Steam/Itch page archive** — use the Wayback Machine to snapshot your store page *before* the clone appeared.
- **Side-by-side comparison sheet** — screenshots of your assets next to the clone's, annotated with red boxes. This alone dramatically speeds up reviewer decisions.
- **Copyright registration** (if you have it) — in the US, registration is required to sue, and it makes takedowns near-automatic. If not registered, your creation timestamp still counts as *prima facie* evidence.

### Step 2: File the Storefront Takedown in the Right Order
File against the **hosting platform**, not the clone's developer (who is often anonymous):

| Platform | Report Path | Typical Response Time |
|---|---|---|
| Steam | Steam Support → Copyright/DMCA form | 24–72 hrs |
| Itch.io | "Report" button → Copyright Infringement | 1–3 days |
| Google Play | Copyright removal web form | 2–5 days |
| Apple App Store | App Store "Report a Problem" + legal notice | 3–7 days |
| Web host (clone site) | Host's abuse@ email + DMCA notice | 24–48 hrs |

### Step 3: Write the DMCA Notice Correctly
Include all six statutory elements (17 U.S.C. § 512(c)(3)):
1. Your physical/electronic signature.
2. Identification of the **infringed work** (your game, with URLs).
3. Identification of the **infringing material** (clone URLs, exact pages).
4. Your contact info.
5. A good-faith statement that use is unauthorized.
6. A statement, **under penalty of perjury**, that the info is accurate and you're the rights holder.

> **Pro tip:** File against the *specific clone listing URL*, not the whole store. Broad notices get rejected; surgical ones get actioned.

### Step 4: Escalate if Ignored
If the storefront stalls past 5 business days, send a follow-up citing **§ 512** and CC the platform's designated copyright agent (published in the US Copyright Office DMCA directory). Document every timestamp — this becomes your paper trail for a counter-notice dispute or, in egregious cases, a DMCA § 512(f) claim.

## Common Issues, Alternatives, and What You Should Know

**"The clone changed the art slightly — is it still infringing?"**
Yes, if it's *substantially similar* in protected expression (character design, level flow, distinctive UI). AI "re-skins" often still copy layout and structure, which counts.

**"Can I DMCA an AI-generated asset directly?"**
Tricky. Purely AI-generated output may lack human authorship protection in the US. But if the *clone* reproduces **your** human-created art, music, or text, that's your protected work being infringed — file on that basis.

**Counter-notice risk:** The clone owner can file a counter-notice, forcing you to either sue within 10–14 days or let the content return. For small devs, **registration before launch** is the cheapest insurance — it turns a bluff into a real legal threat.

**Alternatives to DMCA:**
- **Storefront "impersonation" reports** — faster than DMCA on some platforms for brand-name clones.
- **Trademark takedown** — if the clone uses your game's *name* or logo, this is often quicker than copyright.
- **Public pressure** — a documented thread (like the GMTK one) frequently triggers platform review faster than a form.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### How long does a DMCA takedown take for a cloned game?
On Steam and Itch.io, expect **24–72 hours** for a complete, well-evidenced notice. Mobile stores take **2–7 days**. Incomplete notices can stall indefinitely, so always include side-by-side comparisons.

### Can I DMCA a game cloned with AI if I never registered copyright?
Yes. **Registration is not required to file a DMCA takedown** — only to sue in US federal court. Your creation timestamps and Git history serve as evidence of ownership for the takedown itself.

### What if the AI clone is on a platform in another country?
File with the **hosting provider** (many use US-based CDNs and comply with DMCA) and the platform's global copyright agent. Cross-border enforcement is slower, but major storefronts honor DMCA regardless of the uploader's location.

Going forward, **register your copyright before your next launch** and archive your store pages; it's the difference between a 48-hour takedown and a months-long fight. The clone economy is real, but the takedown tools are still on your side — use them fast, use them precisely.
