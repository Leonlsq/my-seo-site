## Spotify Family Plan QR Code Verification: What It Is and How to Pass It

Spotify has started enforcing its Family plan household rules more aggressively, and the new friction point is a **QR code address check**. If you share a Premium Family or Duo subscription with people who don't actually live at the same address, Spotify may now prompt every member to scan a QR code and confirm their location. The direct takeaway: this isn't a bug and there's no "skip" button — the only reliable way through is to have every member verify from the same home address, or move to individual/student plans. This guide walks through exactly how the check works, how to complete it, and what to do if it fails.

## How the Spotify Household QR Verification Works

Spotify's Family plan has always required all members to live at the **same address**. What changed is the enforcement: instead of a one-time address entry, Spotify can now trigger a re-verification that uses your phone's location to confirm you're at the registered home address.

Here's the typical flow when you're flagged:

1. **You get a prompt in the Spotify app** — usually a banner or modal saying your family plan membership needs verification.
2. **You tap "Verify"** and Spotify displays a QR code (or asks you to scan one shown on another device).
3. **You scan the code with your phone camera** while physically at the home address.
4. **Spotify checks your device location** against the address on the account.
5. **You get a confirmation** (or a failure message) within the app.

A few technical realities worth knowing:

- **Location is the deciding factor.** The QR code is essentially a handshake that ties the scan to your current GPS position. Scanning from a different city will fail even if the code is valid.
- **You need location permissions enabled** for the Spotify app (Settings → Spotify → Location → "While Using the App" on iOS; App permissions on Android).
- **The check is periodic, not constant.** Spotify doesn't track you continuously; it samples location at verification moments.
- **Duo plans face the same rule** — two people, one address.

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Same address | All members must share the registered home address |
| Location permission | Must be granted to the Spotify app |
| QR scan | Must be done on-site at the home address |
| Re-check frequency | Periodic; triggered by Spotify, not user-controlled |
| Plan types affected | Premium Family (up to 6) and Premium Duo (2) |

## Step-by-Step: How to Complete the QR Verification Successfully

If you legitimately live together, this should take under two minutes. Do it in this order:

1. **Update the Spotify app** to the latest version on every member's phone (App Store / Google Play). Older builds may not render the verification flow correctly.
2. **Confirm the home address** on the plan manager's account: Account → Premium Family → address settings. Make sure it's accurate and matches where members actually live.
3. **Enable location permissions** for Spotify on each phone before starting.
4. **Be physically at the home address** when you scan — not "nearby," not on the way. GPS accuracy indoors can drift, so stand near a window if the first attempt fails.
5. **Turn off VPNs.** A VPN routing your traffic through another region can conflict with the location check. Disable it, then retry.
6. **Scan the QR code** shown in the app, approve the location prompt, and wait for the confirmation screen.
7. **Repeat for each member** — verification is per-person, so a single successful scan doesn't cover the whole household.

## Common Issues and What to Do If Verification Fails

### "We couldn't verify your location"
This is the most common failure. Causes and fixes:
- **GPS drift indoors** → move near a window or step outside, then retry.
- **VPN or proxy active** → disable it fully (including any always-on VPN profile).
- **Location permission set to "Never"** → re-enable it in system settings, then reopen Spotify.
- **Stale app version** → update and force-quit the app.

### The QR code won't scan
Clean your camera lens, increase screen brightness on the device showing the code, and hold steady about 6–10 inches away. If the in-app scanner keeps failing, use your phone's native camera app to scan, which usually opens the verification link directly.

### You genuinely live apart (shared-account scenario)
There's no legitimate workaround — faking location violates Spotify's terms and can get the whole plan suspended. Your realistic options:

- **Premium Individual** ($11.99/mo) for each person who lives elsewhere.
- **Premium Student** (~$5.99/mo) if eligible — the cheapest legitimate route for one person.
- **Premium Duo** for two people at one address.

### Cost reality check
A 6-person Family plan at $19.99/mo works out to ~$3.33 per person. Splitting into individual plans costs far more — which is exactly why Spotify is tightening enforcement. Budget accordingly rather than risk a suspension mid-billing cycle.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Why is Spotify asking me to scan a QR code for my family plan?
Spotify is enforcing its household rule, which requires all Family and Duo members to live at the same address. The QR code plus location check is how it confirms members are actually at that address.

### Can I verify my Spotify family plan without being at the home address?
No. The verification ties the QR scan to your device's GPS location. Scanning remotely or with a VPN will fail the check, and repeated attempts can flag the account.

### What happens if I fail the Spotify family verification?
You'll typically get a retry prompt and a grace period. If verification isn't completed, your account can be downgraded to the free tier or removed from the plan. Fix location permissions and VPN settings, then retry from the home address.

## Final Verdict

The QR code check is here to stay, and it's a location test, not a puzzle to outsmart. If you share a household, update every phone, grant location access, and verify on-site — you'll pass in minutes. If you've been splitting a Family plan across addresses, stop gambling on workarounds: move the out-of-house members to Individual or Student plans before Spotify forces the downgrade for you. The savings from sharing aren't worth losing your playlists and offline library mid-month.
