## Moderna's mRNA Cancer Vaccine Just Hit 42%: What the Data Actually Means for You

On October 25, 2026, Moderna and Merck released top-line results from the Phase 3 INTerpath-001 trial of **mRNA-4157 (V940)**, an individualized neoantigen therapy combined with Keytruda (pembrolizumab). The headline number: a **42% reduction in recurrence or death** versus Keytruda alone in high-risk resected melanoma. If you or a family member has been diagnosed with stage IIB–IV melanoma, this is the most consequential oncology news since checkpoint inhibitors — but the headlines skip the operational details. This guide breaks down exactly what the 42% figure means, who qualifies, the timeline to approval, and the questions to bring to your oncologist.

## mRNA-4157 by the Numbers: Trial Specs and What 42% Really Means

The 42% is a **hazard ratio of 0.58** for the primary endpoint of recurrence-free survival (RFS). That is not "42% of patients were cured" — a critical distinction. Here is the breakdown:

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| Trial name | INTerpath-001 (KEYNOTE-942 successor) |
| Phase | 3, randomized, double-blind |
| Population | Resected high-risk melanoma (stage IIB–IV) |
| Arms | mRNA-4157 + pembrolizumab vs. pembrolizumab + placebo |
| Primary endpoint | Recurrence-free survival (RFS) |
| Reported effect | 42% relative reduction in recurrence or death (HR ≈ 0.58) |
| Mechanism | Up to 34 patient-specific neoantigens encoded in a single mRNA |
| Dosing | 9 doses over ~1 year, administered with Keytruda every 3 weeks |
| Manufacturer | Moderna (mRNA) + Merck (Keytruda, commercialization) |

**How the vaccine works, in plain terms:** Your tumor is sequenced, an algorithm picks up to 34 mutated proteins unique to your cancer, and Moderna manufactures a bespoke mRNA shot that trains your T-cells to hunt those exact mutations. It is *not* off-the-shelf — every dose is patient-specific, which is why manufacturing logistics matter as much as efficacy.

## Who Qualifies — and the Step-by-Step Path to Access

Right now, mRNA-4157 is **not FDA-approved**. Top-line results are the first of several regulatory gates. Here is the realistic timeline and what you can do today:

1. **Confirm your eligibility profile.** The trial enrolled resected stage IIB, IIC, III, and IV cutaneous melanoma patients who had complete surgical resection within the prior 13 weeks. If you are mid-treatment for metastatic (unresected) disease, this specific regimen does not apply.
2. **Ask your oncologist about expanded access / compassionate use.** Moderna has signaled it will open an expanded access program (EAP) for eligible patients ahead of approval. Request the specific protocol number from your care team.
3. **Check ClinicalTrials.gov for open INTerpath sub-studies.** Adjacent cohorts (e.g., adjuvant head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, non-small cell lung cancer) are recruiting and may match your diagnosis better than melanoma.
4. **Get tumor sequencing on file now.** Neoantigen selection requires fresh or banked tumor tissue plus germline DNA. Ask for whole-exome sequencing to be ordered so you are ready the moment a slot opens.
5. **Prepare a BLA-timeline question.** Ask: "When is the rolling FDA submission expected, and what is the PDUFA date?" Moderna has guided toward a 2027 filing; approval realistically lands **late 2027 to mid-2028**.

## Common Questions, Cost Reality, and What You Should Know

**This is not a replacement for Keytruda.** Every patient in the trial received pembrolizumab. The 42% is the *incremental* benefit of layering the personalized vaccine on top of standard immunotherapy.

**Cost will be the elephant in the room.** Personalized mRNA manufacturing is expensive. Analysts project a per-patient price in the **$100,000–$250,000 range** for the vaccine course alone, on top of Keytruda. Insurance coverage and CMS reimbursement pathways are unresolved and will determine real-world access more than efficacy does.

**Watch the subgroup data.** Top-line results do not break out stage IIB vs. stage IV or PD-L1 status. Those subgroup analyses — expected at the next major oncology congress (ASCO 2027) — will tell you whether the benefit is uniform or concentrated in specific patients.

**Competitors are close behind.** BioNTech's autogene cevumeran (with Roche) and Transgene's TG4050 are in late-stage trials. If you are weighing trial enrollment, ask about all personalized neoantigen options, not just Moderna's.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Is the Moderna mRNA cancer vaccine FDA approved yet?
No. As of October 2026, mRNA-4157 has only reported top-line Phase 3 results. Moderna and Merck have not yet completed a Biologics License Application (BLA) submission. Approval is realistically expected in **2027–2028**, pending full data and regulatory review.

### Does the 42% reduction mean the vaccine cures melanoma?
No. A 42% relative reduction in recurrence or death means the risk of the cancer coming back or death was 42% lower in the vaccine-plus-Keytruda group compared to Keytruda alone. It is a relative risk reduction, not a cure rate. Absolute survival gains will be published with the full dataset.

### Can I get mRNA-4157 outside a clinical trial right now?
Only through an expanded access (compassionate use) program or an open clinical trial. Ask your oncologist to check ClinicalTrials.gov for INTerpath sub-studies and to request Moderna's EAP protocol. Off-label access is not possible because the therapy is patient-specific and unapproved.

### How long does it take to manufacture a personalized mRNA cancer vaccine?
The manufacturing cycle — from tumor sequencing to neoantigen selection to mRNA production — typically runs **6 to 8 weeks** per patient. That turnaround is one of the biggest logistical hurdles to scaling the therapy after approval.

## Final Verdict

The 42% figure is genuinely practice-changing for high-risk resected melanoma — but it is a **2027–2028 story, not a today story**. If you are a patient, the single highest-value action is to get tumor sequencing on file and ask your oncologist about expanded access and adjacent INTerpath trials now. If you are an investor or industry watcher, focus on three numbers over the next 18 months: the full subgroup analysis at ASCO 2027, the BLA filing date, and the reimbursement price. Efficacy is no longer the question — **access and cost are**.
