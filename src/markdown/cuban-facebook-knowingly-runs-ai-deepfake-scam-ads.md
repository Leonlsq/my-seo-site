## How to Spot a Deepfake Scam Ad: 7 Red Flags

Before you tap anything, run this checklist. Deepfake scam ads almost always fail at least two of these:

1. **Unnatural mouth and eye movement.** AI lip-sync often lags by a fraction of a second. Watch the mouth for 10 seconds — if audio and lips drift, it's synthetic.
2. **"Guaranteed returns" or "free crypto."** Real Musk, Zuckerberg, or Bezos will never ask you to send crypto to double it. This is the #1 deepfake script.
3. **Urgency countdowns.** "Offer expires in 4:59" timers are engineered to stop you from thinking.
4. **Fake news-site landing pages.** Scammers clone CNN, Forbes, or BBC layouts with a fabricated interview. Check the URL — it's usually a random domain like `finance-daily-news[.]xyz`.
5. **Comments disabled or scrubbed.** Legit advertisers allow comments. Scam ads often hide them or delete skeptical replies.
6. **Brand-new Page with no history.** Tap the advertiser name → "Page transparency" → check creation date. Pages created within days are a red flag.
7. **Weird payment rails.** If the "investment" requires a crypto wallet, gift cards, or a wire transfer, stop immediately.

> **Rule of thumb:** If a celebrity is personally asking *you* to invest via a social ad, it is a scam. Full stop.

## Step-by-Step: How to Block and Report Deepfake Scam Ads on Facebook

Do this in order — it takes about 5 minutes and meaningfully reduces your exposure.

### Step 1: Report the ad the right way
- Tap the **three dots (⋯)** in the top-right of the ad.
- Select **"Report ad"** → **"Scam, fraud, or deceptive"** → **"It's a scam or fraud."**
- Do **not** just hide it. Reporting feeds Meta's ad review system; hiding only trains your personal feed.

### Step 2: Block the advertiser
- Tap the ad → **three dots** → **"Block advertiser."**
- This stops the Page from serving you ads again.

### Step 3: Audit your Ad Preferences
- Go to **Settings & Privacy → Settings → Ads**.
- Review **"Ad topics"** and remove financial/crypto topics.
- Under **"Advertisers you've used,"** remove any you don't recognize.
- Turn on **"Ad review"** if available in your region to see fewer sensitive-category ads.

### Step 4: Lock down who can target you
- In **Settings → Ads → Data about your activity from partners**, toggle it **off**.
- Turn off **"Ads based on your activity on websites and apps off Meta."** This shrinks the data pool scammers use to match you with high-value fraud ads.

### Step 5: Harden your account
- Enable **two-factor authentication** (Settings → Password and security).
- Add **login alerts** and review **"Where you're logged in."**
- Remove any third-party apps you don't recognize under **Apps and Websites**.

## What You Should Know: The Cuban Claim, the $16B Figure, and What's Actually Verified

Cuban's allegation — that Meta's internal documents show roughly **$16 billion in revenue tied to fraudulent advertising** — is a serious claim and should be treated as an **allegation**, not a proven fact. Meta has publicly disputed characterizations that it knowingly profits from scam ads and points to its ad review systems and takedown volumes. Here's how to read the story critically:

| Claim | Status | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Deepfake scam ads run on Meta platforms | **Widely documented** | Report and block (see above) |
| Cuban says Meta "knowingly" runs them | **Allegation** | Follow the court/regulatory filings for confirmation |
| ~$16B fraud-linked revenue figure | **Disputed / unverified** | Don't cite as fact without the source doc |
| Meta removes scam ads at scale | **Stated by Meta** | Still verify ads yourself — review isn't perfect |

### Alternatives if Meta's reporting fails you
- **Use ad blockers** (uBlock Origin on desktop browsers) to cut exposure.
- **Report to regulators:** In the US, file with the **FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov**; in the UK, **Action Fraud**; in the EU, your national consumer authority.
- **Check before you click:** Reverse-image search the "celebrity" and search the domain on **ScamAdviser** or **VirusTotal**.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Are the Elon Musk crypto ads on Facebook real?
No. Any ad showing Elon Musk, Mark Zuckerberg, or another billionaire promising to double your crypto or offering a "free giveaway" is a deepfake scam. These are AI-generated videos and cloned voices. Report them via the ad's three-dot menu.

### How do I stop seeing scam ads on Facebook?
You can't eliminate them entirely, but you can reduce them: report each scam ad as fraud, block the advertiser, disable off-Meta ad tracking in Settings → Ads, and remove financial/crypto ad topics. Combine this with a browser ad blocker on desktop.

### Is Mark Cuban's $16 billion Facebook fraud claim confirmed?
No. It remains an allegation based on documents Cuban says he reviewed. Meta disputes the framing. Treat the figure as unverified until regulators or courts confirm it.

### What should I do if I already clicked a deepfake ad?
Immediately change your Facebook password, revoke unfamiliar third-party apps, contact your bank if you entered payment details, and report the incident to the FTC (US) or Action Fraud (UK). If you sent crypto, report to the wallet/exchange and file a police report.
