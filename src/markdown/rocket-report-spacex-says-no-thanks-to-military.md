## SpaceX Said No to a Military Launch—Here's How to Pick a Rocket Provider Anyway

If you're tracking the "Rocket Report" news cycle, the headline is simple: SpaceX reportedly declined a military launch contract, while Spain's PLD Space says its Miura 5 rocket is nearly ready for its debut flight. The quote making the rounds—"We believe Canada should be able to reach orbit on its own terms"—signals a broader shift: launch customers are rethinking who they fly with.

But here's the practical question nobody is answering for you: **if you're a satellite operator, a university lab, or a defense procurement officer, how do you actually choose a launch provider when the biggest name in the industry starts turning down work?** This guide breaks down the decision framework, real cost comparisons, and the step-by-step process for booking a ride to orbit in 2026.

## Step-by-Step: How to Evaluate and Book a Launch Provider

Whether you're flying a 3U CubeSat or a 500 kg defense payload, the process is the same. Follow these steps in order.

### Step 1: Define Your Payload and Orbit Requirements
Before you talk to any provider, lock down these parameters:

- **Mass and volume**: A 1U CubeSat (1 kg) vs. a 500 kg ESPA-class payload changes everything.
- **Target orbit**: LEO (400–600 km), SSO (sun-synchronous), MEO, or GTO. SSO is the most in-demand and most constrained.
- **Injection accuracy**: Defense payloads often need tighter insertion tolerances than commercial rideshares.
- **Timeline**: Rideshare slots can be 18–36 months out; dedicated launches are faster but costlier.

### Step 2: Shortlist Providers by Capability
Use this comparison table to filter candidates:

| Provider | Rocket | Payload (LEO) | Rideshare? | Est. Cost/kg | Status (2026) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SpaceX | Falcon 9 | 22,800 kg | Yes | ~$3,000 | Operational, selective |
| Rocket Lab | Electron | 300 kg | Yes | ~$25,000 | Operational |
| PLD Space | Miura 5 | ~540 kg | Planned | ~$15,000 (est.) | Debut imminent |
| Arianespace | Vega-C | 1,500 kg | Yes | ~$20,000 | Operational |
| Firefly | Alpha | 1,000 kg | Limited | ~$15,000 | Operational |

**Key takeaway**: If SpaceX declines your contract, you are not out of options—but your cost per kilogram roughly doubles to quintuples.

### Step 3: Request Quotes and Compare Total Mission Cost
Never compare sticker price alone. Ask each provider for a **total mission cost breakdown**:

1. Base launch price
2. Payload integration and adapter fees
3. Range and regulatory fees
4. Insurance (typically 3–8% of payload value)
5. Schedule assurance / rebooking penalties

### Step 4: Verify Regulatory and ITAR/EAR Compliance
Military and dual-use payloads trigger export controls. Confirm your provider handles:
- **ITAR** (US Munitions List) clearances
- **EAR** export licensing
- Host-nation launch licenses (e.g., FAA, CNES, or Spain's AEE for Miura 5)

### Step 5: Lock the Contract and Build Schedule Slack
Book 6–12 months of buffer. Debut rockets (like Miura 5's first flight) carry inherent delay risk—budget for a slip.

## Common Issues, Alternatives, and What You Should Know

**Why did SpaceX decline?** Reports suggest capacity prioritization and contract terms, not a blanket refusal of defense work. SpaceX still flies national security missions for the US government—the decline appears contract-specific. Don't assume the door is closed; always request a quote.

**The Miura 5 wildcard**: PLD Space's Miura 5 is a two-stage, partially reusable rocket targeting ~540 kg to LEO. Its first flight is the make-or-break moment. If you book a debut flight, negotiate a **free reflight clause** in case of failure—most providers offer this for maiden launches.

**Canada's "on its own terms" angle**: The quote points to sovereign launch ambitions. If you're a Canadian operator, watch for domestic launch corridor developments—but for now, you'll still fly foreign rockets.

**Hidden cost traps**:
- Rideshare "slot" fees if your payload misses the integration deadline
- Late payload delivery surcharges (often 10–20% of contract value)
- Deorbit compliance costs for LEO constellations

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Did SpaceX actually refuse all military launches?
No. SpaceX continues to fly US national security missions. The reported decline was for a specific contract, likely due to capacity or terms. Always request a direct quote before assuming ineligibility.

### When will Spain's Miura 5 rocket launch?
PLD Space has stated the Miura 5 is "nearly ready," with a debut flight targeted in the near term. As with any maiden launch, expect schedule slips of 3–12 months and negotiate reflight protections.

### How much does it cost to launch a small satellite in 2026?
Rideshare on Falcon 9 runs roughly $3,000/kg; Electron about $25,000/kg; Miura 5 is estimated near $15,000/kg. A 10 kg CubeSat typically costs $30,000–$250,000 depending on provider and orbit.

### Can I launch a military payload on a European rocket?
Yes, provided you clear export controls and the host nation's launch license. Vega-C and the upcoming Miura 5 are viable options, but confirm ITAR/EAR compliance early.

## Final Verdict

**Don't panic over the SpaceX headline—diversify instead.** If you're planning a launch in 2026–2027, request quotes from at least three providers: SpaceX (still ask), Rocket Lab for small dedicated missions, and PLD Space if you want to hedge on cost and can tolerate debut-flight risk. For defense payloads, prioritize providers with proven ITAR handling and negotiate reflight clauses on any maiden rocket. The launch market is finally competitive—use that leverage.
