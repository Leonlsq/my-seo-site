## Waymo and Uber Are Breaking Up in 2028: Here's Your Rider Prep Plan

Waymo has confirmed that its robotaxi integration inside the Uber app will end in **January 2028**, converting a quiet partnership into direct head-to-head competition in Phoenix, Austin, Atlanta, and any market either company enters before then. If you're one of the riders who has been hailing a Waymo through Uber—sometimes without even realizing it—this guide is your practical migration checklist: how to tell which app you're actually using, how to set up the Waymo app before the cutoff, and how the two services will differ once they split.

The short version: nothing changes today, but riders who wait until December 2027 to set up a direct Waymo account will be competing for onboarding slots and promo credits that early movers usually lock in. Below is the full breakdown.

## Step-by-Step: How to Prepare for the January 2028 Split

### Step 1: Check whether you've been riding Waymo via Uber
1. Open the **Uber app** and go to **Activity** (or **Your rides**).
2. Tap any ride receipt from Phoenix, Austin, or Atlanta.
3. Look for a **"Waymo"** or **"Autonomous"** vehicle label and the vehicle type (Jaguar I-Pace in most current Waymo fleets).
4. Screenshot receipts for any rides you may want to reference for loyalty credits or support tickets later.

If you've never seen a Waymo label, you've been riding with human drivers—but the split still matters to you, because Uber's AV partner lineup (and pricing) will shift.

### Step 2: Set up a direct Waymo account now, not in 2028
1. Download the **Waymo One app** (iOS/Android) or bookmark **waymo.com/waymo-one**.
2. Create an account with the same phone number and payment method you use on Uber—this simplifies any future account-linking or credit transfer offers.
3. Add your home and work addresses to **Saved places** so the app can pre-qualify service availability in your area.
4. Enable notifications. Waymo historically rolls out new service areas and waitlist invitations in waves, and app users get first notice.

### Step 3: Keep Uber as your multi-partner fallback
Uber's network will still include other autonomous vehicle partners after the Waymo exit. Practically, that means:
- **Keep your Uber account active**—it remains the broadest single app for mixed human/AV coverage.
- **Expect AV supply to tighten in AV-heavy cities** during the transition quarter (Q1 2028), with possible longer wait times or surge in Phoenix and Austin.
- **Compare per-ride pricing** in both apps for your two or three most common routes before January 2028, so you know which app wins on cost for your commute.

## Common Issues, Alternatives, and What You Should Know

**Your Uber promo credits won't transfer.** Uber credits, Uber Cash, and Uber One benefits are tied to the Uber platform. If you've been stacking Uber One membership perks on Waymo rides, budget for that discount disappearing when you book direct.

**Accessibility features differ by app.** Waymo vehicles support in-car accessibility controls (climate, music, pull-over requests) through the Waymo app and in-car screens. Riders who rely on Uber's accessibility hotline should confirm Waymo's equivalent support channel before switching.

**Service-area mismatch is the biggest gotcha.** Waymo's geofenced service areas are narrower than Uber's coverage. Before you commit to Waymo One as your primary app, test-book a ride to your workplace, your gym, and your most frequent airport route. If any of those fall outside the geofence, keep Uber as your default.

**Alternatives worth watching.** Uber's other AV partners and competing robotaxi networks will expand through 2027–2028. Riders in Phoenix and Austin should keep an eye on which partners Uber prioritizes post-split—those will determine whether Uber's AV supply actually shrinks or just swaps brands.

**Watch for migration incentives.** Platform splits historically trigger retention offers. Expect Waymo to push sign-up credits to Uber-originated riders in late 2027, and Uber to counter with discounts to keep AV riders in-app. Don't switch permanently until you've seen both offers.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Will Waymo still be available in the Uber app after January 2028?
No. Waymo has confirmed the integration ends in January 2028, after which Waymo rides must be booked directly through the Waymo One app. Uber will continue offering rides through its other autonomous vehicle partners and human drivers.

### Do I need to do anything right now to keep riding Waymo?
Not immediately—the split is over a year away. But setting up a Waymo One account, saving your frequent destinations, and comparing prices on your regular routes now means you'll have a working fallback the day the Uber integration ends.

### Will Waymo rides get cheaper or more expensive after the split?
Pricing is unconfirmed, but removing the Uber middleman typically gives the fleet operator more pricing flexibility. Historically, direct-booking AV rides have been priced competitively in core service zones. Compare both apps on your own routes in late 2027 rather than assuming.

### Which cities are affected by the Waymo–Uber split?
The split affects every market where Waymo vehicles are dispatched through Uber, currently including Phoenix, Austin, and Atlanta, plus any additional markets either company launches before January 2028.

## Final Verdict

Don't panic-switch apps today—but don't sleepwalk into 2028 either. **Set up your Waymo One account this month, save your top three destinations, and benchmark prices on both apps for your regular routes.** If Waymo's geofence covers your commute, plan on making Waymo One your primary app and Uber your fallback. If it doesn't, Uber remains your default—just expect a different mix of vehicles behind it. The riders who win this transition are the ones who test both apps before the deadline forces the choice.
