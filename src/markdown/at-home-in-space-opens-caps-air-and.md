## Planning Your Visit to the Reopened "At Home in Space" Gallery

The Smithsonian's National Air and Space Museum on the National Mall has finally reopened its last remaining wing, meaning all of its "space" is back open to the public. The centerpiece of this milestone is the new "At Home in Space" exhibition, which explores how astronauts live, work, eat, and sleep in orbit. If you're searching for how to actually see it without wasting a day in line, this guide gives you the direct answer: **book free timed-entry passes online the moment they release, arrive at opening, and head straight to the second floor's space wing before the midday crowd.** Here's the full breakdown of what to expect and how to plan around it.

## Step-by-Step: How to See "At Home in Space" Without the Crowds

The museum uses free timed-entry passes, and the newly reopened galleries are drawing heavy traffic. Follow this sequence:

1. **Reserve timed-entry passes early.** Passes are free but required and released on a rolling schedule (typically weeks in advance) via the museum's official site. Set a calendar reminder—weekend slots vanish first.
2. **Pick the 10:00 a.m. opening slot.** The space wing is quietest in the first 45–60 minutes. Later afternoon slots are second-best.
3. **Enter via the Independence Avenue entrance** if the Mall-side line is long; it often moves faster.
4. **Head to the second floor space galleries first.** "At Home in Space" sits within the renovated space wing. Do this before the IMAX and planetarium crowds build.
5. **Budget 45–60 minutes** for the gallery itself, plus 2–3 hours for the rest of the museum.
6. **Check the planetarium and IMAX separately.** Those require their own paid tickets and sell out—reserve them at the same time as your entry pass.

## Common Issues, Alternatives, and What You Should Know

**Passes sell out—have a backup plan.** If your date is gone, check back for released batches and consider weekday mornings. The museum also offers same-day options on quieter days, but don't count on it during peak season.

**Don't confuse the two locations.** The National Air and Space Museum on the National Mall is separate from the Steven F. Udvar-Hazy Center in Chantilly, Virginia. The Udvar-Hazy Center houses the space shuttle Discovery and is far less crowded—a great alternative if Mall passes are unavailable.

**Accessibility and logistics:**
- The museum is fully accessible with elevators to all galleries.
- Security screening is mandatory; leave large bags and prohibited items behind to speed entry.
- Nearby Metro stops (L'Enfant Plaza, Smithsonian) make car-free visits easy.

**Cost reality check:** Entry is free, but planetarium and IMAX tickets run roughly $10–$15 per person, and food on-site is pricey—budget accordingly or eat nearby.

**Timing tip:** The renovation completion means the full museum is now open for the first time in years. Expect elevated crowds through the opening season; go on a Tuesday–Thursday if you can.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Do I need tickets to see "At Home in Space"?
Yes—free timed-entry passes are required for the museum, and the gallery is included with general admission. Planetarium and IMAX shows cost extra.

### How long does it take to see the whole Air and Space Museum now that it's fully reopened?
Plan for at least 3–4 hours to cover all galleries comfortably, including "At Home in Space." Add 45–60 minutes if you book a planetarium or IMAX show.

### Is "At Home in Space" good for kids?
Yes. The interactive microgravity and habitation displays are designed to engage younger visitors, and the gallery is compact enough to hold their attention.

### What's the best day and time to visit?
Weekday mornings at opening are the quietest. Avoid weekends and school holidays if possible, and reserve passes as early as they're released.

## Final Verdict

The fully reopened National Air and Space Museum is worth the trip, and "At Home in Space" is a genuinely engaging look at the human side of spaceflight. **Book your free timed-entry pass the day it releases, choose a weekday opening slot, and hit the second-floor space wing first.** If Mall passes are gone, pivot to the Udvar-Hazy Center—you'll still get a world-class space experience with far shorter lines.
