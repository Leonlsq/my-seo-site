## What Actually Happened — and Why "One Day Before Launch" Matters

A last-minute delay is almost never a marketing decision. When a studio pulls a launch within 24 hours, it usually means one of three things:

- **A certification blocker** — a platform holder (Steam, PlayStation, Xbox) flagged a crash or compliance issue in the final build.
- **A save-corruption or progression bug** discovered in preload or review builds.
- **A server/backend failure** for any online or cloud-save component.

The practical takeaway for you: a 72-day window is long enough to fix systemic issues but short enough that the game is likely feature-complete. That's a *good* sign for final quality — but it doesn't change the fact that your plans for this week are now open.

## Step-by-Step: What to Do With Your Preorder Right Now

Don't just sit on it. Work through this checklist today:

1. **Check your storefront's refund window.** Steam's standard policy allows refunds on unreleased titles at any time before release, and within 14 days / 2 hours of play after launch. PlayStation and Xbox store policies differ — check your order confirmation email for the exact terms.
2. **Decide: refund or hold.** If you preordered for a preorder bonus (cosmetics, early access, in-game currency), confirm in writing that the bonus still applies to the January 7, 2027 date. Most stores honor it, but verify.
3. **Screenshot your order.** A timestamped screenshot of your preorder confirmation is your best friend if billing or bonus issues come up in January.
4. **Cancel any booked time off or stream slots.** If you scheduled PTO or a launch stream, move it now — January 7, 2027 is a Thursday, so a Friday-the-8th stream or a long weekend works well.
5. **Turn off preload reminders.** If you had auto-download enabled, disable it so you don't burn bandwidth on a build that may be replaced by a day-one patch anyway.
