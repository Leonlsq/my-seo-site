## Should You Buy the M6 iMac or Entry-Level MacBook Pro? The Honest Answer

Apple just refreshed two of its most popular machines: the M6 iMac and a new entry-level 14-inch MacBook Pro built on the M6 chip. Both now ship with the N1 wireless chip, bringing **Wi-Fi 7 and Bluetooth 6** to Apple's mainstream lineup for the first time. That sounds like a no-brainer upgrade — but the real question most buyers are typing into Google is simpler: *is this generation actually worth my money, or am I paying a premium for connectivity I can't use yet?*

Short answer: **buy if you're coming from an Intel Mac or an M1 machine, wait if you own an M2/M3.** The M6 is a meaningful generational jump in efficiency and wireless future-proofing, but the N1 chip's headline features (Wi-Fi 7, Bluetooth 6) only pay off if your router and accessories support them. This guide breaks down the specs, the real-world upgrade math, and the setup steps you need to actually unlock Wi-Fi 7 speeds.

Before you spend a dollar, here's how the two refreshed machines stack up on the specs that actually matter for daily use.

The takeaway: **the internal silicon is identical.** Your decision comes down to form factor and display, not raw performance. The iMac wins on screen real estate and clean desk setup; the MacBook Pro wins on portability, port selection, and that XDR display for HDR work.

## How to Actually Unlock Wi-Fi 7 and Bluetooth 6 on Your New Mac

Buying the hardware is only half the job. The N1 chip is capable, but your network has to meet it halfway. Follow these steps to confirm you're getting the new wireless standard — and fix it if you're not.

1. **Check your router spec.** Wi-Fi 7 requires a Wi-Fi 7 (802.11be) router. If you're on Wi-Fi 6E, your Mac will fall back to 6E speeds — no error, just a silent downgrade. Look for "802.11be" in your router's admin panel.
2. **Enable 6 GHz band and 320 MHz channels.** Wi-Fi 7's biggest gain is 320 MHz channel width on the 6 GHz band. In your router settings, enable the 6 GHz radio and set channel width to 320 MHz (or "auto" if your firmware manages it).
3. **Verify the connection on macOS.** Hold **Option** and click the Wi-Fi menu bar icon. Look for the "PHY Mode" line — it should read **802.11be**. If it says 802.11ax, you're still on Wi-Fi 6/6E.
4. **Update to the latest macOS.** Early N1 firmware ships with the OS; check **System Settings > General > Software Update** before benchmarking.
5. **Pair Bluetooth 6 accessories deliberately.** Bluetooth 6's headline feature is "Channel Sounding" for precise distance sensing — but only supported accessories benefit. Older Bluetooth 5.x devices still work; they just won't use the new ranging features.

> **Pro tip:** Run a speed test while standing 3 feet from the router, then again from your normal desk position. Wi-Fi 7's multi-link operation (MLO) should keep throughput far more stable through walls than Wi-Fi 6E did — if it doesn't, your router firmware is likely the culprit.

## Common Issues, Alternatives, and What You Should Know

**Issue 1: "My Wi-Fi 7 speeds are slower than expected."** This is almost always a router-side problem — outdated firmware, a 160 MHz cap, or MLO disabled. Update router firmware first, then re-test.

**Issue 2: "Bluetooth 6 accessories won't pair."** Bluetooth 6 is backward compatible, so pairing failures usually trace to a stale Bluetooth cache. Toggle Bluetooth off/on, or reset the module via **System Settings > Bluetooth > Advanced**.

**Alternatives worth weighing before you buy:**

- **Refurbished M3 iMac** — saves roughly 20–25% if you don't need Wi-Fi 7.
- **M4 MacBook Air** — lighter and cheaper than the entry MacBook Pro, but lacks the XDR display and fan for sustained loads.
- **Mac mini M6 + your own monitor** — often the best value if you already own a good display.

**Cost reality check:** The N1 chip's benefits are *future-proofing*, not day-one gains. If your home network is still Wi-Fi 5 or 6, budget for a Wi-Fi 7 router ($150–$300) to actually see the upgrade you paid for.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Is the M6 iMac worth upgrading from an M1 iMac?
Yes — the jump from M1 to M6 delivers roughly 2x multi-core performance, double the base RAM (16GB vs 8GB), and Wi-Fi 7 via the N1 chip. From an M2 or M3 iMac, the upgrade is harder to justify unless wireless speed is critical.

### Does the entry-level MacBook Pro M6 support Wi-Fi 7?
Yes. Both the M6 iMac and the entry-level 14-inch MacBook Pro include the N1 chip with Wi-Fi 7 (802.11be) and Bluetooth 6. You'll need a compatible Wi-Fi 7 router to use the faster standard.

### What is the N1 chip in the new iMac and MacBook Pro?
The N1 is Apple's own wireless connectivity chip, replacing third-party Wi-Fi/Bluetooth modules. It enables Wi-Fi 7, Bluetooth 6, and improved power efficiency for longer battery life on the MacBook Pro.

### Should I wait for the M7 instead?
If you're on an M2 or M3 machine and everything works, waiting another cycle is reasonable. If you're on Intel or M1, the M6's performance and connectivity gains are large enough to buy now.

## Final Verdict

**Buy the M6 iMac** if you want a clean all-in-one desk setup and you're upgrading from Intel or M1 — the 4.5K display plus N1 wireless makes it a genuinely future-proof purchase. **Buy the entry-level MacBook Pro M6** if you need portability, ports, and the XDR screen on a budget; it's the smarter pick for students and commuters. **Wait** if you already own an M2 or M3 Mac — the N1 chip is excellent, but it's not worth a full machine upgrade on its own. Whichever you choose, budget for a Wi-Fi 7 router so the N1 chip can actually stretch its legs.
