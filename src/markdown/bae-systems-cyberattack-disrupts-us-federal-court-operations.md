## Federal Court Notice System Down? Here's Your Bankruptcy Filing Workaround

The Administrative Office of the U.S. Courts has confirmed that a cyberattack on defense contractor BAE Systems took down the electronic notice system used by federal bankruptcy courts — meaning automated Notice of Electronic Filing (NEF) emails, creditor notifications, and certain CM/ECF notice triggers may be delayed or unavailable. If you're an attorney, paralegal, trustee, or pro se filer with a deadline this week, **do not assume the outage pauses your obligations**. This guide gives you the exact manual workarounds to keep filings compliant, serve creditors, and preserve your record while the system recovers.

## Step-by-Step: Filing and Serving Notices During the Outage

The core risk during a notice-system outage is **missing a service deadline or failing to prove you served a party**. Courts generally do not excuse missed deadlines just because a third-party vendor was attacked. Work through these steps in order.

### Step 1: Confirm Your Court's Local Status
- Check your specific bankruptcy court's website (e.g., `[district].uscourts.gov`) for a posted notice about the BAE Systems outage.
- Log into **CM/ECF** directly — filing often still works even when automated *notice* delivery is degraded.
- Call the clerk's office and ask for the outage reference number; note the date, time, and the name of the person you spoke to.

### Step 2: File Manually If ECF Rejects You
If CM/ECF is fully unavailable, most districts allow **emergency paper filing** with the clerk. Prepare:
1. A cover sheet noting "FILED DUE TO CM/ECF OUTAGE — BAE SYSTEMS INCIDENT."
2. Two copies of every document (one for the clerk's stamp, one for your records).
3. A signed declaration stating the electronic system was unavailable at the time of filing.

### Step 3: Serve Creditors and Parties Manually
Since automated NEF emails may not reach creditors, you must serve them yourself:

| Method | When to Use | Proof You Keep |
|---|---|---|
| Email (if address on file) | Fastest; most courts accept with consent | Sent message + read receipt |
| First-class mail | Required for many statutory notices | USPS tracking / certificate of mailing |
| Certified mail | High-stakes adversary proceedings | Green card + tracking number |
| Process server | Contested matters | Affidavit of service |

### Step 4: Document Everything for the Record
Create a running log with: document name, date/time filed, method used, parties served, and confirmation numbers. If a deadline is later challenged, this log is your defense. File a **Notice of Manual Service** in CM/ECF as soon as the system allows.

### Step 5: Monitor PACER and Re-File Electronically
Once the notice system is restored, re-docket any paper filings electronically and check that all parties received notice. Watch for court-issued **administrative orders** extending deadlines retroactively — many districts issue these after confirmed outages.

## Common Issues, Alternatives, and What You Should Know

**Is PACER down too?** Not necessarily. PACER (the public access portal) and CM/ECF (the filing system) are separate from the third-party notice delivery system. You may be able to *read* dockets even when automated notices fail — so check PACER before assuming everything is offline.

**Does the outage excuse my deadline?** Generally no, unless your court issues a specific order. Federal Rule of Bankruptcy Procedure 9006 and local rules control extensions; a vendor cyberattack is rarely automatic grounds. File a motion to extend *before* the deadline passes if you're at risk.

**What about trustees and the U.S. Trustee Program?** Trustees rely heavily on automated notices. If you're a debtor or creditor, proactively email the trustee's office with case numbers and document copies rather than waiting for the system.

**Is my data at risk?** The reported attack targeted BAE Systems' systems, not the courts' case data directly. Still, treat any unexpected email claiming to be a court notice during this window as **phishing** — verify through PACER, not through links in emails.

**Alternatives to automated NEF:** Set up calendar reminders for every deadline, use your firm's case management software to track service manually, and subscribe to docket-monitoring services (e.g., PACER's RSS or third-party docket alerts) as a backup notification layer.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Is the federal bankruptcy court notice system still down after the BAE Systems cyberattack?
Status changes daily. Check your specific court's website and the Administrative Office of the U.S. Courts announcements page for the latest restoration update before relying on any workaround.

### Can I still file documents in bankruptcy court during the outage?
In most districts, yes — CM/ECF filing often remains available even when automated notices fail. If ECF is down, ask the clerk about emergency paper filing procedures.

### Does the BAE Systems cyberattack excuse missed bankruptcy deadlines?
Not automatically. Unless your court issues an administrative order extending deadlines, you should file a motion to extend before the deadline passes and document the outage as cause.

### How do I prove I served a creditor if NEF emails aren't working?
Use certified mail, email with read receipts, or a process server, and keep all confirmation numbers. File a Notice of Manual Service once CM/ECF is fully operational.

## Final Verdict

Don't wait for the system to fix itself while your deadlines tick down. **Treat this outage as a manual-service event**: confirm your court's status, file through CM/ECF if possible, serve every party yourself with provable methods, and keep a timestamped log. The filers who get burned in vendor-outage scenarios are the ones who assumed "the system will handle it." Verify on PACER, document everything, and file a motion to extend the moment a deadline looks threatened.
