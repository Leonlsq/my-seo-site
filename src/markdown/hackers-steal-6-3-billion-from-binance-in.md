## Binance Hack 2026: How to Check If Your Crypto Is Safe (6-Step Audit)

The record-breaking **$6.3 billion Binance hack** has one question on every holder's mind: *is my crypto actually safe?* The short answer: your coins are only as safe as the steps you take **right now**. This guide walks you through a 6-step security audit you can complete in under 30 minutes — checking exchange exposure, revoking risky permissions, moving funds to self-custody, and locking down your accounts so a headline like this never costs you money again. No panic, no vague advice — just a checklist you can run today.

## Step-by-Step Guide: Run This 6-Step Crypto Safety Audit

Most people lose funds not because of the hack itself, but because they leave **dormant approvals, reused passwords, and exchange-held balances** exposed for months afterward. Work through these steps in order.

### Step 1: Confirm Your Exchange Exposure (5 minutes)

- Log into every exchange you use (Binance, Coinbase, Kraken, etc.) and check **withdrawal status** and any banner notices about frozen assets.
- Verify your balances match your own records. If something is missing, screenshot it immediately — exchanges require timestamped proof for reimbursement claims.
- **Do not** click links in emails or DMs claiming to be "Binance recovery support." Phishing spikes 300–500% within 48 hours of major hacks. Always navigate to the exchange manually.

### Step 2: Revoke Dangerous Token Approvals (10 minutes)

This is the single most overlooked step. Smart-contract approvals let dApps spend your tokens — and hackers drain wallets through old approvals.

1. Go to a reputable approval checker (Revoke.cash or Etherscan's Token Approval Checker).
2. Connect your wallet **read-only** — never enter your seed phrase.
3. Sort approvals by **unlimited allowance** and revoke anything you don't actively use.
4. Repeat for every chain you hold assets on (Ethereum, BSC, Polygon, Arbitrum).

### Step 3: Move Long-Term Holdings to Self-Custody (10 minutes)

| Storage Type | Security Level | Best For | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exchange account | Low–Medium | Active trading | Counterparty/hack risk |
| Hot wallet (MetaMask, Phantom) | Medium | Daily use, small amounts | Malware, phishing |
| Hardware wallet (Ledger, Trezor) | High | Long-term savings | Physical loss, supply chain |
| Multisig (Safe) | Very High | Large holdings, teams | Setup complexity |

**Rule of thumb:** keep only what you're actively trading on exchanges. Move the rest to a hardware wallet. A $79 Ledger is cheaper than losing 100% of your stack.

### Step 4: Harden Your Account Security (5 minutes)

- Enable **app-based 2FA** (Google Authenticator, Authy) — never SMS, which is vulnerable to SIM-swap attacks.
- Set **withdrawal whitelists** so funds can only leave to pre-approved addresses.
- Use a **unique password** per exchange via a password manager (Bitwarden, 1Password).
- Enable **anti-phishing codes** where offered, so you can verify genuine emails.

### Step 5: Check for Malware and Clipboard Hijackers

Clipboard hijackers silently swap crypto addresses when you copy-paste. Run a full scan with Malwarebytes or Windows Defender, and **always verify the first and last 6 characters** of any address before sending. Better yet, send a small test transaction first.

### Step 6: Monitor and Set Alerts

- Enable **login and withdrawal notifications** via email and app push.
- Set price/balance alerts so unusual activity is visible fast.
- Re-run Step 2 **monthly** — approvals accumulate every time you use a new dApp.

## Common Issues, Alternatives, and What You Should Know

**"My exchange froze my withdrawals — is my money gone?"** Not necessarily. During major hacks, exchanges freeze withdrawals as a precaution. Historically, most users regain access within days to weeks. Keep documentation and avoid paying anyone who claims they can "unfreeze" your account.

**"Should I switch exchanges?"** Diversification helps, but the bigger win is **not storing assets on any exchange long-term**. If you must, split holdings across 2–3 regulated platforms with proof-of-reserves.

**"Is a hardware wallet overkill for small amounts?"** Under ~$500, a well-secured hot wallet is reasonable. Above that, hardware wallets pay for themselves the first time you avoid a single phishing drain.

**Watch for these red flags post-hack:**
- "Recovery service" DMs and fake support accounts
- Airdrops and tokens you didn't request (never interact — they're drainer bait)
- Urgent emails demanding you "verify your wallet" to claim compensation

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Is my crypto safe on Binance after the $6.3 billion hack?
Binance has stated it is freezing affected funds and working with authorities. Your individual balance safety depends on whether your assets were in affected wallets and Binance's reimbursement policy. Check your account directly, enable 2FA and withdrawal whitelists, and move long-term holdings to self-custody as a precaution.

### How do I check if my wallet was drained or compromised?
Connect your wallet read-only to a block explorer or approval checker like Revoke.cash, review recent outgoing transactions, and confirm your token approvals. If you see transfers you didn't authorize, revoke all approvals immediately and move remaining funds to a fresh wallet.

### Can hackers steal crypto if I use a hardware wallet?
Not remotely — a hardware wallet keeps private keys offline, so hackers can't drain it without physical access or you approving a malicious transaction. The main risks are phishing (signing a bad transaction) and losing your recovery phrase. Never enter your seed phrase online.

### What should I do immediately after a major exchange hack?
Revoke token approvals, enable app-based 2FA and withdrawal whitelists, move non-trading funds to a hardware wallet, run a malware scan, and ignore all "recovery" outreach. Treat every unsolicited message as a scam.
