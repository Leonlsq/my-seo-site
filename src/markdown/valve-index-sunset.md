## Valve Index Sunset: What It Actually Means for Your Headset

Valve has officially wound down the Index, and the "Valve Index sunset" news has left a lot of owners asking the same question: *is my $999 headset now a paperweight?* The short answer is no — but the long answer matters. A hardware sunset means **end of production and end of first-party support**, not end of function. Your Index will keep working with SteamVR, your base stations will keep tracking, and your Knuckles controllers will keep finger-tracking for years. What changes is the supply chain: replacement parts, cables, and controllers get harder and pricier to source, and Valve's warranty pipeline dries up.

This guide is for the roughly 1.5 million Index owners who now need a plan. Below you'll find a maintenance checklist to extend your Index's life, a comparison of the best PCVR upgrade paths in 2026, and the exact SteamVR settings to squeeze more performance out of aging hardware.

## Keep Your Index Alive: Maintenance & Replacement Checklist

The Index's known failure points are well documented in the community: the **tether cable** (the most common failure), the **left controller thumbstick drift**, and **base station whine or motor death**. Here's how to prioritize.

### 1. Buy a Spare Tether Cable Now
The Index tether is the #1 failure point, and third-party replacements are hit-or-miss. If you can still find a genuine Valve tether, buy one as a spare. Expect prices to climb 30–60% as stock dries up.

### 2. Fix Thumbstick Drift Before It Gets Worse
Knuckles controllers use potentiometer-based sticks that drift over time. Options:
- **Contact cleaner + calibration**: temporary fix, works for mild drift.
- **Third-party Hall-effect stick replacements**: permanent fix, requires soldering.
- **Used controller market**: prices for individual Knuckles controllers have risen sharply post-sunset.

### 3. Protect Your Base Stations
SteamVR 2.0 base stations (the ones the Index uses) are shared with the Vive Pro 2 and Bigscreen Beyond, so they'll remain available longer than Index-specific parts. Still, avoid running them 24/7 — the motors wear out. Use **SteamVR's power management** to spin them down when idle.

### 4. Set Up a SteamVR Fallback Profile
Document your working SteamVR config (resolution, refresh rate, per-app supersampling) so you can restore it if a driver update breaks things. Valve's software support will taper off, and community fixes will become the norm.

## SteamVR Settings to Extend Index Performance in 2026

Older GPUs paired with the Index's 144Hz panels need tuning. Use these settings:

1. **Refresh rate**: Drop from 144Hz to **120Hz or 90Hz** if you're GPU-bound. The Index's LCD looks best at native 90–120Hz anyway.
2. **Render resolution**: Set SteamVR to **Custom** and target 100–120% per-eye. Above 150% yields diminishing returns on the Index's panels.
3. **Motion smoothing**: Enable **Motion Smoothing** (not reprojection) for CPU-bound sim titles like *Assetto Corsa*.
4. **Per-app supersampling**: Push it higher only for lightweight titles (*Beat Saber*, *Superhot*).
5. **Disable the camera**: The Index's passthrough camera eats USB bandwidth — turn it off if you get tracking dropouts.

## Common Issues, Alternatives, and What You Should Know

- **Driver rot is real.** As SteamVR updates, older Index firmware occasionally breaks. Keep a known-good SteamVR version pinned if you rely on the Index for work.
- **Base station scarcity is the real risk.** 2.0 base stations are cross-compatible, so they'll survive longer than the headset itself. Buy a spare if you see one at retail.
- **Don't panic-sell.** Used Index prices dipped on the sunset news but stabilized as buyers realized base stations + Knuckles remain valuable.
- **Warranty is over.** Out-of-warranty repairs now mean third-party shops or DIY. Budget accordingly.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Is the Valve Index discontinued?
Yes. Valve has ended production of the Index headset, though SteamVR software support and the broader lighthouse ecosystem continue. Existing headsets keep working.

### Can I still get Valve Index replacement parts in 2026?
Genuine parts are increasingly scarce. Tether cables and controllers are the hardest to find; base stations remain available because they're shared with other SteamVR headsets. Third-party and used markets are your main options.

### What's the best headset to replace a Valve Index?
For lighthouse owners, the Bigscreen Beyond reuses your base stations and controllers. For everyone else, the Meta Quest 3 offers the best value for wireless PCVR.

### Will my Valve Index stop working after the sunset?
No. Sunset means no new production and limited first-party support. Your hardware runs on SteamVR, which remains active, so the Index will function indefinitely as long as your PC and SteamVR support it.

## Final Verdict

The Valve Index sunset is a **supply problem, not a bricking event**. If your Index still works, don't rush to replace it — buy a spare tether cable, fix controller drift now, and tune SteamVR to 120Hz. When you're ready to upgrade, the **Bigscreen Beyond** is the smartest path for lighthouse owners because it reuses everything you already own. If you want out of the base-station ecosystem entirely, get a **Quest 3** and never look back. Either way, act on replacement parts *before* they vanish — that's the real deadline the sunset created.
