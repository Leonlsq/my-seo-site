## Who Is Speaking at TechCrunch Founder Summit 2026 — and Which Sessions Actually Matter?

The TechCrunch Founder Summit 2026, hosted in partnership with Fidelity Private Shares, is a full-day event that deliberately steps past the fundraising-and-AI echo chamber. The agenda covers finding customers, building teams, sharpening a pitch, developing founder leadership, and scaling inside and outside traditional startup hubs. If you're attending (or watching remotely), the practical question isn't "who's on stage" — it's **which sessions map to the bottleneck your company has right now**. This guide breaks down the speaker archetypes, the session tracks, and a prep checklist so you leave with action items instead of business cards.

## The Speaker Lineup by Track: What to Expect and How to Prep

TechCrunch structures the day around founder realities rather than keynote spectacle. Here's how the speaker types typically map to each track, and what you should bring to each session:

| Track | Speaker Archetype | What You'll Hear | Prep Before You Go |
|---|---|---|---|
| Finding Customers | Early-stage founders who hit $1M ARR without paid ads | Outbound playbooks, community-led growth, niche wedge strategies | Write your current CAC and top 3 failed channels |
| Building Teams | Operators scaling from 5 to 50 | First 10 hires, comp bands, firing fast, async culture | Draft your org chart for the next 12 months |
| Sharpening the Pitch | Seed/Series A investors + pitch coaches | Narrative structure, traction framing, objection handling | Record a 60-second pitch on your phone |
| Founder Leadership | Second-time founders and executive coaches | Burnout management, decision fatigue, board dynamics | List your 3 hardest open decisions |
| Scaling Outside Hubs | Founders building in secondary markets | Talent sourcing, remote-first ops, local incentives | Map your hiring radius and cost-of-living arbitrage |

**Concrete prep steps (do these 48 hours before the event):**

1. **Audit your funnel.** Pull your last 90 days of conversion data. Sessions on customer acquisition are useless without your own baseline numbers.
2. **Pre-write three questions per track.** Speakers answer audience questions more candidly than scripted panels — arrive with specifics like "What was your first sales hire's quota in month one?"
3. **Book 1:1s in advance.** Most summits use matchmaking apps; slots fill within hours of opening. Target founders one stage ahead of you, not celebrities.
4. **Bring a one-pager.** A single PDF with your metrics, ask, and contact info converts hallway chats into follow-ups.
5. **Assign a note-taker per session.** Splitting tracks across your team beats everyone attending the same keynote.

## What You Should Know: Costs, Access, and the Fidelity Partnership Angle

The Fidelity Private Shares partnership signals a heavier emphasis on **cap table management, 409A valuations, and secondary liquidity** than a typical founder confab. If you're approaching a priced round or an employee tender offer, prioritize any session touching equity administration — this is where the sponsor's domain expertise shows up in the content.

**Practical considerations:**

- **Cost analysis:** Full-day in-person tickets at comparable TechCrunch events historically run in the low-to-mid hundreds of dollars; virtual or livestream access is often free or heavily discounted. If budget is tight, send one founder in person and stream the rest.
- **Recordings:** TechCrunch typically posts session recaps and select video afterward. Don't skip a track you need just because it conflicts — check the post-event content library.
- **Networking ROI:** The unstructured breaks and lunch blocks are where most founder-to-founder value happens. Block 30 minutes after each session for follow-ups rather than rushing to the next room.
- **Alternatives if you can't attend:** Y Combinator's Startup School (free), local founder dinners, and accelerator demo days deliver similar tactical content at lower cost. The Summit's edge is density — dozens of operators in one day.

**Common pitfalls to avoid:**

- Attending only the AI and fundraising sessions — the event's stated differentiator is the unglamorous operational tracks.
- Collecting business cards without a follow-up system; use a CRM or a simple spreadsheet with a 48-hour follow-up rule.
- Treating it as a pitch opportunity. Investors on panels are working, not scouting.
