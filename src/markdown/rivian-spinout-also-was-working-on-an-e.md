## Also's Kenya E-Moto Pivot: What Actually Happened and Why It Matters

If you were tracking Also — the Rivian spinout formerly known as "Rivian Micromobility" — for a cheap, rugged electric moto built for African roads, the news stings: the Kenya e-moto program is dead. Also co-founder and president Chris Yu confirmed to TechCrunch that the company walked away from the project to prioritize autonomous vehicles. The practical takeaway: **do not wait for an Also-branded e-moto, and do not build a business plan around one.** Instead, use this guide to understand what got shelved, what hardware and specs were implied by the project, and which alternatives and workarounds you should evaluate right now — whether you're a rider in Nairobi, a fleet operator, or an investor tracking Rivian's autonomy roadmap.

## What We Know About the Shelved Kenya E-Moto — and the Specs That Mattered

Also's original thesis was a low-cost, high-durability electric two-wheeler optimized for emerging markets, with Kenya as the pilot market. The pivot to autonomous vehicles means the engineering bandwidth, supply chain partnerships, and capital that would have gone into the e-moto now flow into autonomy stacks. Here's how to read the decision practically:

| Factor | E-Moto Kenya Project (shelved) | Autonomous Vehicle Focus (active) |
|---|---|---|
| Primary market | Kenya, East Africa boda-boda and commuter riders | US/EU robotaxi and fleet pilots |
| Core hardware | Lightweight e-moto chassis, swappable battery | Sensor suites, compute, redundant drive-by-wire |
| Business model | Unit sales + battery-as-a-service | Fleet software, autonomy licensing |
| Capital intensity | Moderate, hardware-heavy | Very high, R&D-heavy |
| Timeline risk | Supply chain + import duties | Regulatory approval |

**Why the pivot happened:** Yu's stated rationale — prioritizing autonomous vehicles — reflects a classic startup triage. Autonomy has a larger addressable market and stronger investor narrative in 2026 than a low-margin hardware play in a market with currency and import-tariff friction. If you were modeling Also's e-moto as a competitor to Spiro, Roam, or Ampersand in Kenya, that model is now invalid.

## Step-by-Step: What to Do If You Were Waiting on the Also E-Moto

1. **Audit your dependency.** If you're a fleet operator who pre-committed budget to Also e-motos, reallocate to proven Kenyan-market platforms (Spiro, Roam Air, Ampersand) and re-run your total cost of ownership with current import duty and VAT assumptions.
2. **Re-benchmark your specs.** The shelved project implied targets like swappable packs and sub-$1,500 unit pricing. Compare any alternative against those targets before signing.
3. **Check the warranty and parts pipeline.** Emerging-market e-moto failures are usually battery and controller failures, not motors. Confirm local parts stock before purchase.
4. **For investors:** treat Also as an autonomy bet, not a micromobility bet. Re-weight your thesis toward sensor/compute supply chain exposure.
5. **For riders:** don't delay a purchase waiting for vaporware. Buy a platform with an existing service network today.

## Common Issues, Alternatives, and What You Should Know

**The real risk isn't the cancellation — it's the support vacuum.** Any pilot units or dealer commitments tied to the Kenya project may face parts and firmware uncertainty. If you hold one, document firmware versions now.

**Practical alternatives to evaluate in Kenya (2026):**

- **Spiro** — established swap network, strong boda-boda presence.
- **Roam Air** — locally assembled, designed for African road conditions.
- **Ampersand** — mature battery-swap model with fleet telematics.

**Hidden cost checklist before buying any e-moto:**
- Battery swap subscription vs. owned pack
- Import duty + VAT on landed cost
- Controller and BMS replacement lead time
- Local technician availability

**What the pivot signals for the sector:** Also's exit doesn't kill Kenya's e-moto market — it concentrates it around operators with local assembly and swap infrastructure. That's arguably better for riders.
