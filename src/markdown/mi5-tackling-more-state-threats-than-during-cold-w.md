## What is the latest on MI5 tackling more state threats than during Cold War, warns intelligence chief?

MI5 is currently confronting a volume and complexity of state-backed threats that surpass anything seen during the Cold War, according to a stark warning from its Director General, Ken McCallum. In his annual threat update, the head of the UK’s domestic intelligence agency revealed that MI5 is now running approximately 200 investigations into state threat actors—a figure that dwarfs the Cold War average of 100. The rise is driven by an increasingly aggressive Russia, a sophisticated Chinese espionage network, and disruptive operations from Iran. This topic is trending because it marks a historic pivot in national security, signaling that espionage and sabotage are no longer relics of the past but active, daily realities.

## Key Details

- **Russia’s Escalation:** Since the invasion of Ukraine, MI5 has expelled over 750 Russian diplomats suspected of being intelligence officers, describing Russia as the primary state threat.
- **China’s Long Game:** The agency is actively countering Chinese espionage efforts, including attempts to steal intellectual property and interfere in UK politics through covert networks.
- **Iran’s Growing Aggression:** McCallum highlighted a sharp increase in Iranian plots to kidnap or assassinate dissidents on UK soil since 2022, forcing MI5 to divert significant resources to counter them.

## Conclusion

The warning from MI5’s chief is a clear signal that the UK faces a new era of hybrid warfare. With state threats now exceeding Cold War levels, intelligence services are under immense pressure to adapt. This trend underscores the importance of public vigilance and robust counter-intelligence measures in a rapidly changing global landscape.
