## How to Spot a Restaurant Delivery Payment-Skimming Scam Before You Pay

The "Scamwich" delivery skimming campaign reported in late 2026 is not a single hacked restaurant — it is a coordinated pattern of fake storefronts, cloned delivery menus, and tampered checkout pages designed to harvest card data. If you have ordered delivery recently and noticed a charge you don't recognize, or a "restaurant" that exists only on a delivery app, this guide gives you a concrete checklist to detect the fraud, lock down your payment method, and dispute charges before they multiply. The short version: verify the merchant, never pay through a link texted to you, and treat any checkout page that asks for more than card number, expiry, and CVV as a red flag.

## Step-by-Step: How to Detect and Shut Down a Skimming Attempt

The scam works in three stages: a fake or compromised listing appears on a delivery platform, the victim pays through a tampered checkout, and the card is drained through micro-charges before a large hit. Here is how to break that chain at each stage.

### Step 1 — Verify the merchant is real (2 minutes)

- **Cross-check the address.** Search the restaurant name plus its street address in Google Maps. A real restaurant has photos, reviews older than a few weeks, and a phone number that resolves to a business line.
- **Check the domain.** Legitimate delivery pages live on the platform's own domain (e.g., `order.platform.com/restaurant-name`). A checkout hosted on a lookalike domain, or a URL with random characters, is a skimming page.
- **Look for the "new listing" tell.** Scam listings are typically days old with a handful of five-star reviews posted within hours of each other. Sort reviews by "newest" and check timestamps.

### Step 2 — Audit the checkout page before entering card data

| Red Flag | What It Means | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Asks for card PIN or online banking login | No delivery platform needs this | Close the tab immediately |
| Requests a "verification" photo of your card | Classic skimming tactic | Never comply; report the listing |
| Payment link sent via SMS/WhatsApp | Bypasses platform payment protection | Pay only inside the official app |
| Checkout URL differs from the app's domain | Cloned page harvesting data | Abandon cart, screenshot, report |
| "Tip" field pre-filled at 40%+ with no option to edit | Pressure tactic to rush you | Manually set the tip or leave |

### Step 3 — Contain the damage if you already paid

1. **Freeze the card** in your banking app (most major banks support instant freeze in-app).
2. **Screenshot everything** — the listing, the checkout URL, the confirmation email, and the charge line on your statement.
3. **Dispute the charge** as "fraudulent / unauthorized" rather than "item not received." The former triggers a card-network fraud investigation.
4. **Request a new card number** rather than just a freeze; skimming campaigns often retry stolen numbers weeks later.
5. **Report the listing** to the delivery platform and to your local consumer protection agency.

## Common Issues, Alternatives, and What You Should Know

**Micro-charge testing.** Skimmers often run $0.50–$2.00 test charges first. If you see small unfamiliar charges from a delivery app, treat them as a probe and act immediately — the large charge usually follows within 72 hours.

**Virtual card numbers are your best defense.** Most major banks and fintech apps let you generate a single-use virtual card number. Use one per order; a skimmed virtual number is worthless after the transaction. This is the single highest-impact habit change from this scam wave.

**Platform payment vs. restaurant-direct payment.** Paying inside the delivery app keeps you under the platform's dispute and refund policy. Paying a restaurant directly via a texted link removes that protection entirely — which is exactly why the campaign targets it.

**Cash-on-delivery is not a fix.** It avoids card skimming but exposes you to fake-driver scams where the "restaurant" never existed. Stick to in-app card payment with a virtual number.

**Check your statement cadence.** Set a weekly calendar reminder to scan your card statement for delivery-app charges you don't recognize. Skimming campaigns rely on victims not noticing small charges for weeks.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### How do I know if a restaurant delivery listing is a scam?
Cross-check the restaurant's physical address and phone number in Google Maps, confirm the checkout URL matches the delivery platform's official domain, and sort reviews by newest to spot clusters of fake five-star reviews posted within hours. A listing with no verifiable address or a checkout on an unfamiliar domain is a scam.

### What should I do if I entered my card details on a fake delivery checkout?
Freeze the card in your banking app immediately, request a new card number (not just a freeze), screenshot the fake listing and checkout URL, and file a fraud dispute with your bank marked "unauthorized charge." Then report the listing to the delivery platform.

### Are virtual card numbers safe for food delivery orders?
Yes — single-use virtual card numbers are the strongest practical defense against payment skimming. A stolen virtual number is typically useless after one transaction, so even if a checkout page is compromised, your real card details are never exposed.

### Can a restaurant delivery app charge me without my permission?
Legitimate platforms require your authorization for each order. Unrecognized charges from a delivery app are a fraud indicator, not a billing error — dispute them as unauthorized and request a new card number rather than waiting for a refund.

## Final Verdict

The Scamwich skimming campaign is a reminder that the weakest link in food delivery is the checkout page, not the food. My recommendation: switch to single-use virtual card numbers for every delivery order, pay only inside official apps, and check your statement weekly. That three-step routine costs nothing, takes under five minutes a week, and neutralizes the entire attack pattern this campaign relies on. If you have already been hit, freeze the card today and dispute as fraud — do not wait for a second charge to confirm the pattern.
