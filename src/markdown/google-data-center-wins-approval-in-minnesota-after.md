## Google's Hermantown Data Center Just Cleared Its Final Hurdle — Here's Your Action Plan

After two delayed council votes and weeks of public protest, the Hermantown City Council voted **unanimously on October 30** to approve Google's proposed data center. If you live in the Duluth–Hermantown area, work in construction or trades, or simply rely on Google Cloud services, this decision affects you directly. Below is a practical breakdown of what was actually approved, what happens next, and the concrete steps you should take — whether you're a resident bracing for power and water impacts, a contractor chasing the build-out contracts, or a cloud user planning around regional capacity.

## What Was Actually Approved (And What Wasn't)

The council's approval is a **land-use and rezoning green light** — not a construction permit. Before dirt moves, Google still needs to clear environmental review, utility interconnection agreements, and state-level permits. Here's the practical timeline breakdown:

| Milestone | Status | What It Means for You |
|---|---|---|
| City council approval | ✅ Passed (Oct 30) | Zoning path is clear; no more public hearings required at city level |
| Environmental review | ⏳ Pending | Watch for the public comment window — this is your last formal input point |
| Utility interconnection (power/water) | ⏳ Pending | Minnesota Power and local water authority must sign off |
| Site prep & construction | Expected 2026–2027 | Trades hiring typically ramps 6–12 months before groundbreaking |
| Live operations | Multi-year horizon | Cloud region capacity impact is a 2027+ story |

**Key takeaway:** The protests delayed the vote twice but didn't stop it. If you opposed the project, your leverage has shifted from the council chamber to the **permitting and environmental review process** — that's where to direct comments now.

## Step-by-Step: What Residents, Workers, and Cloud Users Should Do Next

**If you're a Hermantown-area resident:**
1. **Sign up for the city's permit notification list** (Hermantown city website → Planning & Zoning) so you get alerts on environmental review comment periods.
2. **Document your baseline now** — photograph well levels, note noise levels, and log utility bills. If impacts emerge during construction, you'll have comparison data.
3. **Attend the next planning commission meeting** — environmental review scoping is where water draw and noise mitigation conditions get set.
4. **Request the water use agreement** once filed; data centers typically negotiate long-term draw commitments that are public record.

**If you're a contractor or job seeker:**
1. **Register with local trade unions** (Duluth Building Trades) — data center builds are union-heavy and hire electricians, HVAC techs, and concrete crews first.
2. **Watch Google's data center careers page** for "Hermantown" listings; site management roles post 9–12 months before operations.
3. **Get familiar with liquid cooling and high-density power distribution** — modern Google facilities lean heavily on these, and certified techs command premiums.

**If you're a Google Cloud user:**
1. **Don't expect a new region announcement soon** — Google typically confirms cloud regions only after construction is well underway.
2. **Track latency from the Upper Midwest** using `gcloud` or a simple ping test to `us-central1`; a future Minnesota region would primarily reduce latency for Duluth/Minneapolis/Chicago corridor users.

## Common Concerns and What You Should Know

- **Water usage:** Data centers use evaporative cooling, and Minnesota's cold climate actually reduces annual water draw versus Sun Belt facilities — but summer peak draw is the number to watch in permit filings.
- **Power grid impact:** Minnesota Power's interconnection study will reveal whether residential rates are affected; ask for it at the next public meeting.
- **Property values:** Research on data center proximity is mixed — industrial tax revenue often offsets concerns, but nearby parcels can see noise complaints during construction.
- **The "done deal" myth:** Approval ≠ permits. Environmental review is a genuine chokepoint, and organized comments there have delayed projects elsewhere.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Did the Hermantown data center get final approval?
No — the October 30 council vote approved the land use and zoning path. Environmental review, utility agreements, and state permits are still required before construction begins.

### Why was the Hermantown council vote delayed twice?
Public protests and resident concerns over water usage, noise, and power grid strain pushed the council to postpone the vote twice before the unanimous October 30 approval.

### Will the Hermantown data center create local jobs?
Yes, primarily during construction (trades, electrical, HVAC) and a smaller permanent staff for operations. Most permanent roles are technical and posted via Google's careers site.

### Does this mean Google Cloud is opening a Minnesota region?
Not confirmed. Google announces cloud regions separately and typically only after construction is advanced. Treat any region rumors as speculative until Google's official regions page lists it.
