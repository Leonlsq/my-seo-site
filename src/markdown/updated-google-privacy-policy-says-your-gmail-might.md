## Google's Updated Privacy Policy and Your Gmail: What Actually Changed

Google quietly updated its privacy policy to clarify that data from Gmail and other Workspace-adjacent services can feed its advertising systems when **Personal Intelligence** is enabled. The short version: turning on Personal Intelligence gives Google more of your personal data — including signals derived from email content — for ad targeting and AI personalization. The good news is this is a **setting you control**, not a mandatory data grab. The bad news is it's buried, and the default behavior varies by account type and region. This guide shows you exactly where the toggle lives, how to audit what's already shared, and how to lock your account down in about five minutes.

If you're a personal Gmail user in the US or EU, here's the direct takeaway: **check your Personal Intelligence toggle today**, review your Web & App Activity history, and decide whether the trade-off (better AI answers vs. more ad signal) is worth it to you.

## Step-by-Step: Check and Disable Personal Intelligence

Personal Intelligence is Google's umbrella term for features that use your personal context (Gmail, Calendar, Photos, Search history) to personalize AI responses and — per the updated policy — inform advertising. Here's how to audit it.

### Step 1: Find the Personal Intelligence toggle
1. Go to **myaccount.google.com** and sign in.
2. Navigate to **Data & privacy** in the left sidebar.
3. Scroll to **Personalized results & recommendations** (or **Personal Intelligence**, depending on rollout).
4. Toggle **off** any option labeled "Use your data to personalize AI and ads."

### Step 2: Audit Web & App Activity
1. In **Data & privacy**, open **Web & App Activity**.
2. Check whether **"Include Chrome history and activity from sites, apps, and devices that use Google services"** is on.
3. Turn off **sub-activity** toggles for Gmail, Search, and YouTube if you don't want those signals used.
4. Set **auto-delete** to 3 months (the shortest option) to shrink your historical footprint.

### Step 3: Review Ad Personalization
1. Visit **adssettings.google.com**.
2. Toggle **Personalized ads** off to stop ad targeting based on your profile.
3. Under **"How your ads are personalized,"** review and remove inferred interests you don't want associated with your account.

### Step 4: Check Gmail-specific smart features
1. In Gmail, open **Settings → See all settings → General**.
2. Scroll to **Smart features and personalization**.
3. Uncheck **"Turn on smart features and personalization"** to disable Gmail's data-driven features (Smart Compose, Smart Reply, and related personalization).

| Setting | Location | What It Controls | Recommended |
|---|---|---|---|
| Personal Intelligence | myaccount.google.com → Data & privacy | AI + ad personalization from personal context | Off if privacy-first |
| Web & App Activity | Data & privacy | Search, Chrome, app activity history | Auto-delete 3 months |
| Ad Personalization | adssettings.google.com | Profile-based ad targeting | Off |
| Gmail Smart Features | Gmail → Settings → General | Email-derived personalization | Off if strict |

## What You Should Know Before You Toggle

**There's a trade-off.** Disabling Personal Intelligence degrades Gemini's ability to answer questions using your email and calendar context. If you rely on AI summaries of your inbox, you'll lose that convenience.

**Workspace accounts are different.** If your Gmail is a **Google Workspace** (business/school) account, your admin controls these settings — the policy update primarily targets consumer accounts, and Workspace data is generally excluded from ad targeting. Check with your IT admin.

**Turning it off doesn't delete history.** Disabling the toggle stops *future* use but doesn't retroactively purge past data. Use **auto-delete** and **"Delete activity"** in Web & App Activity to clear history manually.

**Ads don't disappear — they get less personal.** You'll still see ads in Gmail's Promotions and Social tabs; they just won't be targeted from your personal context.

**EU/UK users get extra controls.** Under GDPR and the DMA, you have additional consent screens and the right to object to ad profiling — look for the consent banner at **myaccount.google.com**.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Does Google actually read my Gmail to serve ads?
Google states it does **not** scan email content for ad targeting in the traditional sense, but the updated policy allows *signals derived from* personal context (when Personal Intelligence is on) to inform personalization and ads. Disabling the toggle removes that pathway.

### How do I stop Google from using my Gmail for advertising?
Turn off **Personal Intelligence**, disable **Ad Personalization** at adssettings.google.com, and turn off **Gmail smart features** in Gmail settings. Set Web & App Activity to auto-delete after 3 months.

### Is Personal Intelligence on by default?
Rollout varies by region and account. Don't assume — verify the toggle at **myaccount.google.com → Data & privacy** and set it explicitly.

### Will turning this off break Gemini or Smart Compose?
It can reduce AI personalization (contextual answers, Smart Compose suggestions). Core Gmail still works fine.

## Final Verdict

Treat this policy update as a **prompt to audit, not panic**. Spend five minutes: disable Personal Intelligence if you don't actively use Gemini's personal-context features, set Web & App Activity to 3-month auto-delete, and turn off ad personalization. If you *do* rely on AI inbox summaries, keep Personal Intelligence on but tighten your auto-delete window and review inferred ad interests quarterly. Either way, make the choice deliberately — the default is not your friend here.
