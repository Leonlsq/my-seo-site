## What Austin Wintory's Mass Effect 5 Draft Leak Actually Contains

Composer Austin Wintory—the Journey and Assassin's Creed Syndicate maestro—confirmed he was in early conversations with EA and BioWare about scoring the next Mass Effect. Then he did something unusual: he released roughly 20 minutes of his own draft material for the project publicly. If you've seen the headline and want to actually *understand and listen to* what dropped, this breakdown covers what the drafts are, how to find them, how to listen properly, and what they tell us about the game's direction.

The short version: these are **unfinished sketches**, not a finished OST. Treat them as a mood board, not a soundtrack.

## How to Listen to the Wintory Mass Effect Drafts (Step-by-Step)

Wintory typically posts his draft material through his own channels (his website, Bandcamp, and social accounts), not through EA. Here's how to track it down and listen without confusion:

1. **Start with his official channels.** Check Austin Wintory's Bandcamp page and his personal site first—he has a history of releasing "sketchbook" material there. Reddit's r/masseffect megathread (where the original post surfaced) will link the canonical source.
2. **Avoid reupload channels.** YouTube reuploads of the drafts often have altered audio compression. If you want to judge the mix, use the original upload.
3. **Use headphones, not laptop speakers.** These are orchestral sketches with quiet string beds and low synth drones—laptop speakers will cut off everything under ~200Hz and you'll miss the bass motifs entirely.
4. **Disable audio "enhancements."** On Windows: Settings → System → Sound → your output device → turn off Spatial Sound and any Realtek/Realtek-based "enhancements." On mobile, disable Dolby Atmos auto-processing.
5. **Listen in order, not shuffled.** Draft sets are usually sequenced to show a progression of ideas; shuffling destroys the intended mood arc.

### What the 20 Minutes Actually Sound Like

Based on the released material and Wintory's own commentary, expect:

- **Sparse, textural openings** — solo strings and pads rather than the bombastic brass of the original trilogy.
- **Repeated motif fragments** — short melodic cells that read as "seeds" for a full theme, not finished cues.
- **Hybrid synth/orchestral layering** — consistent with modern sci-fi scoring (think Hildur Guðnadóttir's *Chernobyl* more than Jack Wall's *Mass Effect 2*).
- **No final mix** — levels are rough; don't judge loudness or mastering.

## What This Means for Mass Effect 5 (and What It Doesn't)

**What it confirms:** Wintory was genuinely in talks with EA and BioWare. The drafts are real composer work, not fan fiction.

**What it does NOT confirm:** That Wintory is scoring the game. "Conversations" are not a contract. He may have been one of several composers pitched, or the deal may have stalled. Do not treat these drafts as the official Mass Effect 5 soundtrack.

**Why composers release drafts:** Wintory has a documented habit of sharing "sketch" material as a creative archive. Releasing it also sidesteps the awkwardness of work that might otherwise never see daylight. From a fan perspective, it's a rare window into how game scores are built before the marketing machine takes over.

### The Practical Takeaway for Fans

- **Don't pre-order or hype-build around this.** It's a creative artifact, not a product announcement.
- **Do use it to calibrate expectations.** If the final game leans into this textural, motif-driven style, the drafts are your early preview.
