## Mailed It! Announce Trailer Explained: What You Actually Do as the Night-Shift Intern

The **Mailed It! announcement trailer** just dropped, and if you watched it expecting a cozy mail-sorting sim, you got something stranger and more interesting: a physics-driven chaos engine disguised as a job simulator. Here's the core loop the trailer establishes — you play a night-shift intern who starts so underequipped you **can't even run**. You haul letters across the hall by hand, one at a time, at a walking pace. Every letter you sort earns back a basic ability the job should have given you on day one: sprinting, a crate, a vacuum.

That inverted progression is the hook. Instead of unlocking power-ups, you're unlocking *basic dignity*. This guide breaks down exactly what the trailer shows, how the unlock order likely works, and whether the physics chaos is a selling point or a red flag.

## Mailed It! Progression & Unlock Order: What the Trailer Confirms

Based on the developer's own description and trailer footage, here's the confirmed and implied unlock structure:

| Stage | Unlock | What It Fixes |
|-------|--------|---------------|
| Start | Nothing | Walk speed only, one letter per trip |
| Early | Sprint | Cuts traversal time on long hauls |
| Early | Crate | Carry multiple letters per trip |
| Early-Mid | Vacuum | Cleans up spilled piles fast |
| Mid | Fans | Redirect airborne mail |
| Mid | Air Platforms | Vertical routing for falling letters |
| Late | Lifts | Move bulk volume between floors |
| Late | Sorting Machines | Automated sorting begins |
| Endgame | Self-Sorting Mail | System runs with minimal input |

The key detail: **every letter is a real physics object**. That means mail doesn't teleport into slots — it piles up in heaps, rolls off surfaces, and occasionally comes pouring out of a ceiling pipe all at once. The developer explicitly stated most of the dev time went into *balancing that chaos* so it feels fun rather than just messy.

### Why the Unlock Order Matters

If you're planning to play on day one, understanding the intended progression saves you frustration:

1. **Don't fight the mess early.** Before you have a vacuum, spilled letters are a resource drain you can't fix. Prioritize sorting over tidying.
2. **Sprint is the highest-value early unlock.** Traversal time is your real bottleneck when you're carrying one letter at a time.
3. **The crate changes the math.** Once you can carry multiples, your throughput roughly doubles or triples — this is the inflection point where the game stops feeling like a chore.
4. **Automation is the endgame, not the goal.** Fans, lifts, and sorting machines are meant to let the system run itself, which mirrors the fantasy of "the job finally working the way it should."
