## Game Ownership Bill 2026: What It Actually Changes for Your Library

New York has joined Pennsylvania in pushing a game ownership bill that would force publishers to sell you a **license you actually own** — not a revocable rental — and require an offline mode plus the right to transfer games. If you're here, you don't need the press release; you need to know what to do with your existing library and how to position yourself before (and if) this becomes law. The short version: nothing changes on your console or PC today, but the bill creates a roadmap you can prepare for now — and a few settings you should change regardless. This guide breaks down the requirements, gives you a checklist to audit your library, and explains the gaps publishers will likely exploit.

## What the Bill Requires (and What It Doesn't)

Before you plan around it, understand the three pillars the legislation targets. These are the recurring requirements across the New York and Pennsylvania proposals:

| Requirement | What It Means for You | Current Industry Reality |
|---|---|---|
| **True ownership, not license** | You buy a copy, not a revocable right to access | Most storefronts sell "licenses" (Steam Subscriber Agreement, PSN ToS) |
| **Offline mode mandate** | Games must be playable without server authentication | Many single-player titles still require a one-time or periodic online check |
| **Transfer rights** | You can resell, gift, or transfer a purchased game | Effectively impossible on Steam, PSN, Xbox, eShop today |

**What it doesn't do:** It won't retroactively convert every license in your library, it won't force publishers to keep servers online forever, and it won't apply outside New York (or Pennsylvania) unless other states copy it. Treat it as a consumer-rights floor, not a magic wand.

## Step-by-Step: Audit and Future-Proof Your Game Library

Do this now so you're ready if ownership rules land — and so you're protected if a publisher pulls a title you "bought."

1. **Export your library lists.** On Steam, go to **Help → Steam Support → My Games** or use the account data export. On PlayStation and Xbox, screenshot or export your purchase history from account settings. You want a dated record of what you paid for.
2. **Flag always-online single-player games.** These are your highest risk. Check whether a title boots in airplane mode. If it doesn't, note it — these are the games the offline-mode mandate is designed to protect.
3. **Prioritize DRM-free where possible.** GOG remains the safest storefront for offline permanence. For Steam titles, check [PCGamingWiki](https://www.pcgamingwiki.com) for DRM and offline status before buying.
4. **Back up installers and saves locally.** Keep offline installers for DRM-free games and export save files to a local drive or NAS. Cloud saves are convenient but tied to the same account you might one day lose access to.
5. **Document multiplayer dependencies.** Ownership bills generally protect the *game*, not the *servers*. Assume online-only multiplayer can still be shut down.
6. **Watch your state's legislature.** If you're in NY or PA, track the bill's committee status; if you're elsewhere, this is your cue to contact representatives — copycat bills are likely.

## Common Issues, Loopholes, and What Publishers Will Argue

**The "offline mode" loophole.** Publishers can satisfy an offline mandate with a one-time activation that still requires a server ping. Watch for language requiring *permanent* offline playability, not just "offline after first launch."

**Transfer rights are the hard part.** Resale requires a technical mechanism to revoke a license from one account and issue it to another. Expect publishers to argue this is technically infeasible or to propose "transfer fees" that eat the resale value. A realistic outcome is limited, publisher-controlled transfers — not an open marketplace.

**Subscription services are a separate category.** Game Pass and PS Plus are rentals by design and likely fall outside ownership rules. Don't expect your subscription library to become transferable.

**Physical media still wins on ownership.** A disc or cartridge is the closest thing to true ownership you can buy today. If the bill stalls, physical copies of single-player games remain your best hedge — just verify they don't require a day-one download to function.

**Cost reality check:** Resale rights, if enacted, could raise new-game prices as publishers offset lost digital margin. Budget for that possibility rather than assuming ownership rights are free.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Will the New York game ownership bill affect my existing Steam or PlayStation library?
Not automatically. Most proposals target *future* sales and would need explicit retroactive language to touch games you already own. Assume your current library stays a license unless the final law says otherwise.

### Does the bill guarantee I can resell my digital games?
It establishes a *right* to transfer, but the mechanism is the battleground. Expect publisher-controlled transfer systems with possible fees rather than an open resale market like physical discs.

### What does "offline mode" mean in the game ownership bill?
It means a purchased game should be playable without an internet connection or server authentication. The key detail to watch is whether it requires *permanent* offline play or just a one-time online check.

### Is this law in effect right now?
No. As of this writing it's a proposed bill in New York, following Pennsylvania's lead. It must pass the legislature and be signed before it becomes enforceable.

## Final Verdict

Don't wait for legislation to protect your library — start the audit today. **Export your purchase history, test your single-player games in airplane mode, and buy DRM-free or physical when permanence matters.** The New York bill is a genuine step toward consumer ownership, but its real-world impact hinges on how "offline mode" and "transfer" are defined in the final text. Treat it as a signal to become a smarter digital buyer now, and a reason to contact your representatives if you want it to actually pass.
