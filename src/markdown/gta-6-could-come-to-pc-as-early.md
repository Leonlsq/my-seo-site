## Step-by-Step: How to Prep Your PC for GTA 6

### Step 1: Benchmark Your Current Build
Before buying anything, find your real bottleneck. Run these free tools and record the scores:

1. **3DMark Time Spy** – GPU/CPU gaming baseline.
2. **Cinebench R24** – single-core (matters for Rockstar's engine) and multi-core.
3. **CapFrameX or MSI Afterburner** – log 1% lows in a modern open-world title like Cyberpunk 2077.

If your 1% lows dip below 45 FPS at 1080p in Cyberpunk, you'll struggle with GTA 6's dense city streaming.

### Step 3: Prioritize the Right Upgrades
- **Storage first.** Open-world streaming is I/O bound. A Gen4 NVMe (7,000 MB/s) can cut load times by 40%+ versus SATA.
- **RAM second.** 16 GB is the floor; 32 GB is the safe bet for 1440p and above.
- **GPU last.** Wait for the post-console-launch GPU price drop — historically, GPU prices dip 10–15% in the quarter after a major console release.

## Common Pitfalls and What You Should Know

- **Don't buy a GPU today for a 2027 game.** Next-gen cards (RTX 50-series refresh, RDNA 4) will be cheaper and faster by then.
- **Watch for a "next-gen patch."** Rockstar often ships a performance patch 2–4 weeks post-launch. Don't panic-tune on day one.
- **Denuvo and anti-cheat.** Expect Denuvo DRM, which can cost 5–10% CPU overhead. A strong single-core CPU matters more than core count.
- **Console-first optimization.** The PC port will likely launch with upscaling (DLSS 4 / FSR 4) enabled by default. Test native vs. upscaled before judging performance.
