## Step-by-Step: How to Make More Useful Connections (The Networking Playbook)

The Summit's pitch is "meaningful networking," but meaningful connections don't happen by accident. Use this pre-event, on-site, and post-event system:

### Before the Event (Do This in the Next 4 Days)
1. **Download the attendee list / app** as soon as it's available and filter for three categories: investors matching your stage, potential design partners, and operators who've solved your exact problem.
2. **Build a target list of 10 people** — not 50. Quality beats volume at a one-day event.
3. **Draft a 15-second intro** that states your company, traction metric, and one specific ask ("I'm building X, we just hit Y, and I'd love your take on Z").
4. **Book travel to arrive the night before** — SoWa Power Station is in Boston's South End; arriving fresh beats a 6 AM flight.

### On-Site at SoWa Power Station (Nov 4)
1. **Skip the main-stage keynote if it's a topic you can read later.** Hallway conversations during talks are where the real intros happen.
2. **Use the "two-question" method:** ask what they're building, then what their biggest bottleneck is. Founders remember people who listen.
3. **Collect contacts with context** — note *where* you met and *what* you discussed in your phone immediately. A name alone is worthless in a week.
4. **Target the coffee lines and lunch tables**, not the bar at the end of the day. Sober, specific conversations convert better.

### After the Event (Within 48 Hours)
1. **Send personalized follow-ups** referencing your specific conversation — not a generic LinkedIn request.
2. **Deliver something of value** (a relevant article, an intro to someone in your network) to move from "met once" to "useful contact."
3. **Log everyone in a simple CRM** (even a spreadsheet) with a follow-up date.

## Common Issues, Alternatives, and What You Should Know

- **"I can't afford the ticket."** Compare the ticket cost against the price of a single warm intro. One investor meeting that leads to a check dwarfs the ticket price — but only if you execute the playbook above.
- **"I'm not fundraising."** Then the ROI shifts to design partners and hiring. The Summit is still useful if you have a specific "ask" — otherwise skip it and attend a free meetup.
- **Alternative events:** If you're purely optimizing for investor access, smaller invite-only founder dinners often yield higher conversion per hour than large summits. Use the Summit as your anchor and layer 1–2 side events around it.
- **Hidden cost:** A full day out of building. Budget for it — block your calendar the day after for follow-ups, not deep work.
