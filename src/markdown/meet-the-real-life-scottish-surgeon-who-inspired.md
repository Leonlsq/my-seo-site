### How Bell's Deduction Method Actually Worked

Bell trained his students to observe before they spoke. His approach followed a repeatable sequence:

1. **Observe the physical evidence first** — gait, posture, calluses, clothing wear, and accent.
2. **Correlate observations with known patterns** — a specific callus suggested a specific trade.
3. **Test the hypothesis out loud** — Bell famously guessed a stranger's occupation before the person confirmed it.
4. **Refine with questions** — he treated every patient as a puzzle to be diagnosed, not just treated.

This is the same loop Holmes runs in every story: observe, correlate, hypothesize, verify. Doyle openly credited Bell, writing that the character was "a dramatization of Dr. Bell's methods."

### Did Arthur Conan Doyle admit Holmes was based on Joseph Bell?
Yes. Doyle openly credited Bell, describing Holmes as a dramatization of Bell's observational methods. Doyle served as Bell's clerk at the Royal Infirmary of Edinburgh in the 1870s.

### What was Dr. Joseph Bell famous for?
Bell was famous for diagnosing patients' trades, habits, and backgrounds through close observation — a skill he applied to both medicine and criminal cases, making him an early pioneer of forensic science.
