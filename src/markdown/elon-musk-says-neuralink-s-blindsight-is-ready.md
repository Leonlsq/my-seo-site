## Neuralink Blindsight Explained: What It Actually Does (and Doesn't Do)

Elon Musk announced that Neuralink's second product, **Blindsight**, is "ready" and "just waiting on regulatory approval." Here's the part the headlines bury: Blindsight is a **cortical visual prosthesis** — it bypasses the eye and optic nerve entirely and stimulates the visual cortex directly. That means it is designed for people whose blindness comes from eye or optic nerve damage (retinitis pigmentosa, glaucoma, globe injury), **not** for people with damage to the visual cortex itself. It will not deliver 20/20 color vision on day one. Early cortical implants historically produce phosphenes — points of light — that the brain must learn to interpret. If you or a family member are searching for real answers on eligibility, cost, and timelines, this guide breaks down what is confirmed, what is speculative, and what you should actually do next.

## Blindsight vs. Existing Visual Prosthetics: Key Specs Comparison

Neuralink has not published a full datasheet, so treat non-confirmed figures as estimates. The comparison below uses publicly stated claims plus published data from competing devices.

| Feature | Neuralink Blindsight | Second Sight Orion (legacy) | Pixium PRIMA (subretinal) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Target | Visual cortex (bypasses eye) | Visual cortex | Retina (photoreceptor layer) |
| Eligible blindness types | Eye/optic nerve damage, intact cortex | Same | Dry AMD (advanced) |
| Electrode count | ~1,000+ per implant (claimed, unconfirmed) | 60 | 378 photovoltaic pixels |
| Wireless | Yes (claimed) | Yes (RF coil) | Yes (light-powered) |
| Regulatory status | Pre-approval / pending (Musk claim) | FDA Breakthrough Device (program paused) | CE-marked (EU), clinical trials in US |
| External hardware | Wearable camera + processor (expected) | Glasses-mounted camera | Special augmented-reality glasses |
| Published human vision data | None yet | Phosphene perception, limited acuity | Letter/shape recognition in trials |

**Practical takeaway:** Blindsight's cortical approach is the right architecture for optic-nerve blindness, but it has **zero published peer-reviewed human efficacy data** as of this writing. PRIMA has the strongest published clinical results; Orion has the longest safety track record but its commercial program was discontinued.

## How the Blindsight Process Would Work: Step-by-Step

If Blindsight reaches approval, the patient pathway will look roughly like this:

1. **Neurological screening** — MRI/fMRI to confirm an intact visual cortex and rule out cortical blindness. This is the single biggest disqualifier.
2. **Candidacy assessment** — ophthalmology + neurology review of blindness etiology (retina, optic nerve, or cortex).
3. **Pre-surgical imaging** — mapping the cortical surface to plan electrode placement (likely occipital pole targeting).
4. **Craniotomy & implantation** — surgical placement of the thin-film electrode array; this is brain surgery, with standard risks (infection, bleeding, edema).
5. **Device pairing** — linking the implant to the external camera/processor unit.
6. **Phosphene calibration** — mapping which electrodes produce which perceived light points; this takes weeks of sessions.
7. **Rehabilitation training** — learning to interpret artificial visual patterns; expect months, not days.

**Bottom line:** This is not a same-day fix. Budget 3–6 months from surgery to functional use, based on prior cortical implant trials.

## What You Should Know: Costs, Risks, and Alternatives

- **Cost estimate:** Neuralink has not announced pricing. Comparable neurostimulation implants (cochlear, deep brain) run **$30,000–$100,000+** including surgery and rehab. Assume Blindsight lands in that range or higher initially.
- **Insurance:** Unlikely to be covered at launch; expect out-of-pocket or trial participation.
- **Key risks:** Surgical complications, electrode degradation over time, and cortical stimulation side effects (seizure risk is a known concern in cortical implants — regulatory review will scrutinize this heavily).
- **Regulatory reality:** "Waiting on approval" is Musk's framing. FDA review for a novel Class III brain implant typically takes **months to years**, and Neuralink's first product (Telepathy) is still in early human trials. Treat a near-term launch date skeptically.
- **Alternatives to watch:** PRIMA (retinal, for AMD), optogenetic approaches (e.g., Nanoscope), and stem-cell retinal therapies. If the blindness is retinal, a retinal prosthesis may be lower-risk than brain surgery.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Who is eligible for Neuralink Blindsight?
Likely patients with blindness caused by eye or optic nerve damage who have an intact visual cortex. People with cortical blindness (damage to the visual cortex itself) would not benefit. Final eligibility will be set by trial protocols and regulators.

### How much will Neuralink Blindsight cost?
No official price exists. Based on comparable brain implants, expect **$30,000–$100,000+** for device, surgery, and rehabilitation, with limited insurance coverage at launch.

### Will Blindsight restore normal 20/20 vision?
No. Cortical prosthetics historically produce low-resolution phosphene patterns — enough for light/shape/motion detection and navigation, not reading fine print or color vision, at least in early generations.

### When will Blindsight be available to patients?
Unknown. Musk says it is "waiting on regulatory approval," but no FDA approval or trial enrollment date has been confirmed. Realistic timelines for broad availability are years, not months.

## Final Verdict

**Don't plan around Blindsight yet — but do get on the radar.** The technology is genuinely promising for optic-nerve blindness, and the cortical approach is scientifically sound. However, with no published human data, no confirmed price, and no approval date, it is not a "buy now" product — it is a "register interest and talk to a neuro-ophthalmologist" product. If you have retinal disease like advanced AMD, ask your specialist about **PRIMA trials** today. If your blindness is optic-nerve related, monitor Neuralink's official trial registry listings and clinicaltrials.gov rather than headlines. The honest verdict: **high potential, zero consumer readiness — wait for peer-reviewed data before making any medical or financial decisions.**
