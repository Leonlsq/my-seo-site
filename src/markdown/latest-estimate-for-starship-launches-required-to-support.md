## Why 22 Starship Launches Is the Number That Actually Matters

The latest estimate puts the Starship lunar landing architecture at roughly **22 launches** to support a single crewed Moon landing — and that number is the single most important figure in the entire Artemis program. Here's the direct takeaway: **most of those flights aren't landers at all.** They're tankers. The 22-launch figure is dominated by orbital propellant transfer, not by building ships. If you want to understand whether Artemis III lands on schedule, you need to understand the refueling math below — because "we need to keep our eyes on numerous required fuelings for the lander" is the entire ballgame.

This guide breaks down where 22 comes from, how the count could shrink or balloon, and the specific technical milestones to watch.

## The 22-Launch Breakdown: Where the Numbers Come From

A crewed Starship HLS (Human Landing System) mission requires a fully fueled lander in low Earth orbit. Starship can't reach orbit with enough propellant to go to the Moon and back, so it must be topped up in orbit via cryogenic propellant transfer from tanker Starships. Each tanker delivers a partial load; the lander's tanks are large.

| Mission Element | Est. Launches | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| HLS lander to orbit | 1 | The crewed vehicle itself |
| Tanker flights for lander | ~14–16 | Depends on per-tanker payload to LEO |
| Depot / accumulation support | 1–2 | Long-duration propellant depot |
| Lunar surface demo / uncrewed test | 1–2 | Required before crew |
| Contingency / reflight margin | 2–3 | Losses, scrubs, reflights |
| **Total** | **~22** | Public estimate range |

The critical variable is **usable propellant delivered per tanker flight**. If a tanker can offload ~150–200 tonnes of usable propellant to the depot, the count drops. If boil-off, transfer losses, and return propellant eat into that, the count climbs fast. Every tonne of margin lost per flight adds flights.

### The Refueling Chain, Step by Step

1. **Launch the depot** — a modified Starship that stays in orbit holding cryogenic propellant.
2. **Launch tanker Starships** — each docks and transfers propellant to the depot.
3. **Manage boil-off** — propellant must be kept cold; transfer windows and insulation dictate cadence.
4. **Launch and fill the HLS lander** — the crewed vehicle docks and receives its full load.
5. **Trans-lunar injection** — the lander departs for NRHO (Near-Rectilinear Halo Orbit) to meet Orion.
6. **Land, ascend, return** — the lander performs the surface mission and returns to Orion.

Each of those tanker flights is a full Starship launch, a full booster recovery, and a full ship recovery or disposal. That's the operational load behind "22."

## What Could Shrink — or Blow Up — the 22-Launch Estimate

The estimate is not fixed. It moves with three levers:

- **Per-flight propellant delivery.** Higher usable payload = fewer tankers. This is the biggest lever and depends on demonstrated orbital transfer efficiency.
- **Launch cadence and reuse.** Rapid booster and ship reuse lowers cost per flight but doesn't change the count — it changes whether 22 launches is feasible in a launch window.
- **Boil-off and transfer losses.** Cryogenic propellant in orbit is leaky. Better insulation and active cooling reduce top-up flights.
- **Mission profile.** A minimal lander (no long surface stay, small crew) needs less propellant than a full Artemis surface mission with extended stays.

If transfer efficiency underperforms early estimates, you should expect the count to rise toward the high 20s. If it overperforms, it could drop toward the high teens. Watch the first **orbital propellant transfer demo** — that single test will reset every public estimate.

## Common Questions, Risks, and What to Watch

- **Is 22 launches a hard requirement?** No. It's an estimate for a specific mission profile and transfer efficiency. Treat it as a planning figure, not a spec.
- **Does this delay Artemis III?** The refueling campaign is the pacing item. If tanker cadence and transfer demos slip, the landing slips with them.
- **Cost implication:** At current internal cost targets per launch, 22 flights is a meaningful budget line — but still far cheaper than expendable alternatives per tonne to orbit.
- **Alternative architectures:** Blue Origin's National Team lander uses a different refueling approach with fewer, larger transfers. It's a genuine comparison point if the 22-flight model proves operationally painful.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### How many Starship launches are needed for a Moon landing?
The latest public estimate is roughly **22 launches** per crewed lunar landing, with the large majority being tanker flights to refuel the HLS lander in low Earth orbit.

### Why does Starship need so many refueling launches?
Starship's propellant tanks are large and it must carry enough propellant to travel to the Moon, land, and return to lunar orbit. Since it can't launch fully fueled to those requirements, it must be topped up in orbit by multiple tanker flights.

### Will the 22-launch number change?
Yes. It depends on usable propellant delivered per tanker, boil-off and transfer losses, and the specific mission profile. Early orbital propellant transfer results will determine whether the real number lands in the high teens or the high 20s.

## Final Verdict

Treat **22 launches** as the current planning baseline, not a fixed law of physics. The number you should actually track is **usable propellant delivered per tanker flight** — that single metric drives everything else. If you're following Artemis, watch the first orbital propellant transfer demo and the tanker cadence: those two data points will tell you more about the real launch count than any press release. Until then, plan around 22, but budget your attention for the refueling campaign, because that's where the schedule will be won or lost.
