## Step-by-Step: How to Build the Replica for Free

Follow this workflow to go from zero to a finished model without overpaying.

1. **Download the instructions first.** Search "ASML High-NA EUV" on Rebrickable and grab the free PDF/IO file. Verify the piece count matches **810** before buying anything.
2. **Export the parts list to a Bricklink wanted list.** Most Rebrickable models have a one-click "Add to Wanted List" button. This is the single biggest cost-saver—it lets sellers compete on your order.
3. **Sort by "cheapest across stores."** Use Bricklink's **Auto-Select** or Rebrickable's **Buy Parts** tool. Expect 2–4 stores to cover the full list; consolidating to one store usually adds 20–30% to the total.
4. **Substitute rare colors.** The replica likely uses a few scarce elements (dark blue Technic panels, trans-clear tiles). Swap to the nearest common color—nobody will notice on a display model, and it can cut $15–$25.
5. **Buy used where it's safe.** Structural bricks (Technic beams, plates) are fine used. Avoid used **trans-clear** and **printed** parts, which yellow and scuff.
6. **Print custom stickers locally.** The replica's "ASML" branding uses stickers. Order from a print shop or use a home inkjet on vinyl sticker paper—costs under $5 vs. sourcing rare printed tiles.
7. **Build in sub-assemblies.** The machine breaks into base frame → wafer stage → optical column → housing. Assemble each, then join. This mirrors how the real tool is modularized and makes troubleshooting easier.

## Common Issues, Alternatives & What You Should Know

**Issue 1: Parts list shows "unavailable" elements.** Some colors get retired. Use Rebrickable's **"Find alternative colors"** toggle—it auto-suggests in-production substitutes.

**Issue 2: Instruction file won't open.** The model may be shared as a **.io (Studio)** file. Download Bricklink Studio (free) to view and rotate it before committing to a build.

**Issue 3: Scale mismatch with your shelf.** At 810 pieces the model is roughly **desk-sized (~30–40 cm)**. Measure your display space first—this is not a minifigure-scale toy.

**Alternatives worth comparing:**

- **Buy the official ASML kit secondhand:** Rarely listed, often **$400+** on resale forums, and authenticity is hard to verify. Not worth it unless you're a serious collector.
- **Generic "semiconductor machine" MOCs:** Cheaper but lack the High-NA EUV accuracy and the ASML backstory.
- **3D-printed display model:** More accurate geometry, but no building satisfaction and higher upfront cost.

**Information-gain tip:** The fan replica's real value is the **open customization layer**—you can add working LED lighting to the optical column for ~$8 using a coin-cell LED strip. The official kit can't be modified without devaluing it.
