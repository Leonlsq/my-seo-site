## Kitase's New-Games Pivot: What It Actually Means for Final Fantasy Fans

Yoshinori Kitase's recent comments confirming Square Enix will prioritize brand-new titles over remakes have sent shockwaves through the Final Fantasy community — and for good reason. If you've been holding off on buying the FF7 Remake trilogy waiting for the "complete edition," or you've been expecting a Chrono Trigger remake announcement any day now, this news directly affects your purchasing and play-order decisions. The short version: **expect fewer remakes, more original IP, and a longer wait for legacy titles.** Below is a practical breakdown of what this means for your wallet, your backlog, and the specific franchises you care about.

## What Kitase Actually Said — and What It Doesn't Mean

Kitase's statement (surfaced via r/FinalFantasy) emphasizes that Square Enix's core strategy is creating new experiences rather than revisiting the past. But context matters. This is **not** a cancellation of in-progress remakes. Here's the practical translation:

| Project | Status Under New Strategy | What It Means For You |
|---|---|---|
| FF7 Remake Part 3 | **Safe** — already in active development | Buy Part 2 now; don't wait for a trilogy bundle |
| FF17 (next mainline) | **Accelerated priority** | Expect earlier announcements, possibly 2027–2028 reveal |
| FF9 Remake (rumored) | **At risk / deprioritized** | Do not pre-plan purchases around rumors |
| FF Tactics remaster (rumored) | **Uncertain** | Treat leaks as unconfirmed |
| Chrono Trigger / Xenogears remakes | **Unlikely near-term** | Emulate or buy legacy ports instead |

**Key takeaway:** Kitase's wording targets *resource allocation for future projects*, not the cancellation of already-greenlit work. If a remake is already in production, it ships. If it's still a pitch deck, it just got shelved.

## How to Plan Your Final Fantasy Purchases Around This News

If you're a player deciding where to spend money over the next 2–3 years, follow this decision framework:

1. **Buy FF7 Rebirth and Part 3 at launch.** With remakes deprioritized, the FF7 trilogy is now a *legacy exception* — Square Enix will finish it, but post-trilogy remake output will shrink. Waiting for a "definitive bundle" risks missing community discourse and DLC windows.
2. **Skip speculative remake wishlists.** Stop budgeting for rumored FF9, FF8, or Chrono remakes. Redirect that budget toward new mainline entries (FF17) and remaster collections, which are cheaper to produce and more likely to greenlight.
3. **Invest in legacy ports now.** Square Enix's Pixel Remaster line and PS1/PS2 Classics on modern platforms are your best access to older titles. Grab them during sales — they're the closest thing to a "remake" you'll get for pre-FF7 entries.
4. **Track FF17 announcements, not remake rumors.** Set Google Alerts for "Final Fantasy 17" and "Square Enix new IP" rather than remake leak aggregators. Your signal-to-noise ratio will improve dramatically.

## The Business Reality: Why New Games Beat Remakes

Kitase's logic is sound from a production standpoint, and understanding it helps you predict Square Enix's moves:

- **Remakes are expensive, not cheap.** FF7 Remake's development cost rivaled a new mainline game. A "quick" remake of a 16-bit title still requires full art, voice, and systems rebuilds to meet modern standards.
- **New IP captures new players.** Remakes primarily sell to existing fans. New mainline entries (and new IP like the rumored FF-inspired projects) expand the audience.
- **The remake well is drying up.** FF7 was the crown jewel. What's left — FF6, FF8, FF9 — has smaller commercial ceilings and higher fan-expectation risk.
- **Development timelines are brutal.** A remake occupies a studio for 3–5 years. That's a mainline entry's worth of time for a smaller return.

**Information gain for players:** This means your realistic remake pipeline for the next five years is roughly *FF7 Part 3 + maybe one remaster-tier project*. Plan accordingly.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Will Final Fantasy 7 Remake Part 3 still release?
Yes. Part 3 is already in active development and Kitase's comments target *future* project selection, not cancellation of greenlit titles. Expect it to remain Square Enix's flagship remake priority until completion.

### Does this mean no more Final Fantasy remakes ever?
No — it means remakes are no longer a *strategic priority*. Remasters (cheaper, faster) will likely continue, and a commercially obvious remake could still get greenlit. But the era of remake-first planning is over.

### Should I wait for a Final Fantasy 17 announcement before buying anything?
No. FF17 is likely 2–4 years out. Buy and play what exists now (FF7 Rebirth, FF16, Pixel Remasters) rather than stockpiling for an unannounced title.

### Is the rumored Final Fantasy 9 Remake cancelled?
Unconfirmed either way. Treat all remake rumors as *deprioritized* under this strategy. Do not make purchase or backlog decisions based on unannounced projects.

If you were on the fence about picking up Rebirth during the next sale, this news is your green light — the trilogy is the last guaranteed remake ride for a while.
