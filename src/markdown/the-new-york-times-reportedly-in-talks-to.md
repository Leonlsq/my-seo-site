## The NYT–Letterboxd Deal: What Actually Changes for Your Account

The New York Times is reportedly in talks to acquire Letterboxd for roughly $330 million. If you're a Letterboxd user, your first question isn't about valuation multiples—it's **"what happens to my reviews, lists, and Pro subscription?"** Here's the direct takeaway: based on how the NYT has handled prior acquisitions (The Athletic in 2022, Wordle in 2022), expect the core product to stay intact short-term, but expect account-linking, data-policy, and monetization changes within 12–18 months. This guide breaks down exactly what to do now to protect your film diary and get ahead of the changes.

## What Changes (and What Doesn't) — A Practical Breakdown

Based on the reported deal structure and NYT's acquisition playbook, here's the realistic impact map:

| Feature | Short-Term (0–6 mo) | Likely Long-Term (12–24 mo) |
|---|---|---|
| Reviews & diary | No change | No change (core product) |
| Pro/Patron pricing | No change | Possible price hike or NYT bundle |
| Data sharing | No change | Cross-promotion with NYT/Wirecutter |
| Account login | No change | Possible NYT account linking |
| API access | No change | TOS review likely |
| Ads | Minimal | Higher probability |

**Your action items right now:**

1. **Export your data immediately.** Go to Letterboxd → Settings → Data → "Export Your Data." You'll get a ZIP with `reviews.csv`, `watched.csv`, `diary.csv`, `watchlist.csv`, and `lists/`. Do this monthly from now on.
2. **Screenshot your custom lists.** CSV exports don't preserve list descriptions, cover art, or ordering nuances. Screenshot anything you've spent hours curating.
3. **Document your Pro/Patron status.** Note your renewal date and payment method. If pricing changes, you'll want a record of your grandfathered rate.
4. **Check your linked accounts.** If you log in via Google/Facebook/Apple, verify you have a standalone email/password set as a backup in case SSO changes.
5. **Archive your top reviews.** Use the Wayback Machine's "Save Page Now" on your profile URL and your 5–10 most-liked reviews.

### How to Export Your Letterboxd Data (Step-by-Step)

1. Log in on desktop (the export button is unreliable on mobile web).
2. Click your avatar → **Settings**.
3. Scroll to the **Data** section → click **Export Your Data**.
4. Wait for the email/ZIP (usually 1–5 minutes for large libraries).
5. Unzip and open `reviews.csv` in Excel/Sheets to confirm it's complete.
6. Store a copy in cloud storage **and** a local drive.

## What This Means for Pro Users, Patrons, and API Developers

Letterboxd's revenue model leans on **Pro ($19/year)** and **Patron ($49/year)** tiers plus a small ad layer. The NYT paid $330M—roughly 10–15x Letterboxd's estimated annual revenue—which means they'll want a return. Realistic scenarios:

- **Bundle play:** NYT could fold Letterboxd Pro into an All Access bundle (currently ~$25/mo for News + Games + Cooking + The Athletic + Wirecutter). This could be a *win* if you already subscribe.
- **Ad expansion:** Expect more prominent sponsored placements. Ad-blocker users may see paywall-style prompts.
- **API tightening:** Third-party apps (like some stats dashboards) rely on the Letterboxd API. A TOS review could break unofficial tools—back up any stats you care about.
- **Editorial crossover:** NYT's culture desk + Letterboxd's user reviews = likely co-branded "best films" features. Your public reviews may get surfaced more widely.

**Alternatives to have on your radar** if terms shift: **Serializd** (TV tracking), **Trakt** (film + TV, strong API), **IMDb** (Amazon-owned, ad-heavy), and **Criticker** (niche, recommendation-focused). None match Letterboxd's social polish, but Trakt is the closest functional backup for diary-style logging.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Will the NYT shut down Letterboxd or delete my reviews?
Highly unlikely. The NYT acquires products to grow them (Wordle, The Athletic), not to sunset them. Your reviews and diary are the platform's core value—deleting them would destroy the asset they just bought. The realistic risk is *policy* changes, not deletion.

### Is my Letterboxd data safe if the ownership changes?
Your data remains yours under current terms, but privacy policies can be revised post-acquisition. The safest move is to **export your data now** and set a recurring monthly reminder. Under GDPR/CCPA you retain portability rights regardless of owner.

### Will Letterboxd Pro get more expensive?
No confirmation yet, but acquisitions typically precede monetization changes within 12–18 months. If you're on the fence, **renew an annual Pro plan now** to lock in the current $19/year rate for another cycle.

### Does the NYT deal affect the Letterboxd app or API?
No immediate changes reported. Watch the official Letterboxd blog and their API changelog for TOS updates. Third-party stats tools are the most at-risk category.

## Final Verdict

Don't panic-sell your film diary, but **do act like an owner, not a renter**. Spend 10 minutes today: export your data, screenshot your best lists, and confirm your login method has an email backup. The NYT–Letterboxd deal is likely good for the platform's longevity—more resources, better infrastructure, wider reach—but ownership transitions are exactly when users should control their own data. If you're a heavy user, lock in annual Pro now; if you're API-dependent, start testing Trakt as a fallback. The films aren't going anywhere—make sure your reviews aren't either.
