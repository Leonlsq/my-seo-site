## UNM Health Ransomware Attack: What Patients and Students Need to Do Now

The University of New Mexico Health Sciences Center confirmed a ransomware attack that knocked out clinical and academic systems, forced surgery postponements, and disrupted patient care across its hospitals and clinics. If you're a patient, student, or employee searching for answers, here's the direct takeaway: **assume your records may be exposed until UNM confirms otherwise, and take the protective steps below immediately** — don't wait for an official notification letter to start locking down your identity and accounts.

This guide is not a news recap. It's a practical checklist for anyone affected by the UNM Health outage — patients with appointments, students locked out of academic portals, and staff navigating downtime procedures.

## Step-by-Step: How to Check If You Were Affected and Protect Yourself

### Step 1 — Verify the Current Outage Status
Before panicking, confirm what's actually down. UNM Health typically posts service updates on its main patient portal and official social channels.

- Check the **UNM Health patient portal (myUNMHealth)** login page for a banner notice.
- Call the **main hospital line** rather than showing up in person — phone trees often carry recorded outage messages.
- Monitor the **UNM Health Sciences Center newsroom** for official breach disclosures.

### Step 2 — Confirm Whether Your Data Was Exposed
Under U.S. law, HIPAA requires covered entities to notify affected individuals within **60 days** of discovering a breach involving unsecured protected health information (PHI). Watch for:

| Notification Type | What It Means | Action Required |
|---|---|---|
| Official mailed letter | Confirmed PHI exposure | Enroll in offered credit monitoring |
| Portal/email notice | Possible exposure | Change passwords, monitor accounts |
| No notice after 60 days | Likely not in breach scope | Stay vigilant, keep monitoring |

### Step 3 — Lock Down Your Accounts
- **Change your myUNMHealth password** and any password reused elsewhere (use a password manager).
- **Enable multi-factor authentication (MFA)** on email, banking, and health portals.
- **Freeze your credit** with Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion — free and reversible.
- **Pull your free credit reports** at AnnualCreditReport.com and watch for new accounts.

### Step 4 — Protect Medical Identity Specifically
Medical identity theft is nastier than financial fraud because it corrupts your health records. Request a **copy of your medical records** from UNM Health and review for procedures, prescriptions, or diagnoses that aren't yours. Dispute errors in writing.

### Step 5 — Handle Appointment and Prescription Disruption
- **Reschedule postponed surgeries** through the clinical line, not the portal (which may be down).
- **Request paper prescriptions** or pharmacy call-ins if e-prescribing is offline.
- **Keep a paper log** of medications and dosages in case records are temporarily inaccessible.

## What You Should Know: Ransomware Realities and Alternatives

### Why Healthcare Is a Prime Target
Healthcare ransomware attacks have surged because hospitals can't afford downtime — making them more likely to pay. The FBI and CISA **discourage paying ransoms**, as payment funds criminal operations and doesn't guarantee data deletion.

### The "Downtime Protocol" Reality
When EHR systems go down, hospitals revert to **paper-based downtime procedures**. This means:
- Slower registration and longer wait times.
- Manual charting that can introduce record errors.
- Delayed lab and imaging results.

If you're a patient, **bring printed medication lists and insurance cards** to appointments during the outage.

### Red Flags of a Follow-Up Phishing Scam
Attackers often exploit breaches with **fake "breach notification" emails** demanding you "verify your identity" via a link. Legitimate notices:
- Never ask for your **Social Security number via email**.
- Come from a verifiable **official domain**, not lookalikes.
- Offer credit monitoring you enroll in **directly**, not through an emailed link.

### Cost and Legal Context
Breach remediation — credit monitoring, legal fees, system rebuilds — routinely runs into **millions of dollars**. For individuals, the practical cost is time: freezing credit, disputing fraudulent charges, and correcting medical records. Budget a few hours now to save weeks later.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Was my personal data stolen in the UNM Health ransomware attack?
UNM Health has not publicly confirmed the full scope of data exposure. Treat any notification letter as confirmation, and proactively monitor accounts and credit regardless. HIPAA mandates notification within 60 days if PHI was compromised.

### Are UNM Health surgeries and appointments still canceled?
Disruptions vary by location and system recovery progress. Call the specific clinic or hospital line directly — do not rely solely on the patient portal, which may still be offline. Ask whether to arrive in person or reschedule.

### How do I freeze my credit for free after a breach?
Contact Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion individually (or via their online portals) to place a free security freeze. It blocks new credit inquiries and is reversible anytime with a PIN.

### Is it safe to use the UNM Health patient portal right now?
Only access the portal through the **official URL** you've used before. Avoid links from emails or texts claiming to be UNM Health, as phishing campaigns commonly follow breaches.
