## Is 'You Can See Everything' Worth Watching? A No-Nonsense Review Breakdown

The new documentary *You Can See Everything* claims to pull back the curtain on Elizabeth Holmes and Theranos—but here's the problem: it doesn't. Instead of exposing the mechanics of one of Silicon Valley's most infamous frauds, the film blurs the deceit by humanizing Holmes and letting her narrative breathe without sufficient counterweight. If you're searching for whether this documentary is worth your time, where to stream it, or how it stacks up against *The Dropout* and *Bad Blood*, this breakdown gives you the direct answer first: **skip it unless you're a completionist, and watch *The Inventor* instead.**

## Common Issues, Alternatives, and What You Should Know

The documentary's biggest flaw is a **missing accountability framework**. It presents Holmes' claims without consistently pairing them against the documented evidence—the Edison device's limited capabilities, the voided test results, the ~$9 billion valuation collapse. For viewers, this creates a "both sides" illusion where none exists.

**Better alternatives, ranked:**

- **Watch *The Inventor: Out for Blood in Silicon Valley*** if you want the whistleblower (Tyler Shultz, Erika Cheung) perspective and the actual science failures.
- **Read *Bad Blood* by John Carreyrou** for the definitive, sourced account—the investigative journalism that broke the story.
- **Watch *The Dropout*** if you prefer a dramatized but well-researched narrative arc.

**Red flags to watch for while viewing:** uncritical use of Holmes' voiceover, absence of patient testimony, and framing fraud as "visionary ambition." If you spot these, you're watching a narrative, not journalism.
