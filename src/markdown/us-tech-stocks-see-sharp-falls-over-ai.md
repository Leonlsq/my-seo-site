## AI Cost Fears Are Crashing Tech Stocks: What You Should Actually Do

The BBC reports that US tech stocks have seen sharp falls over fears that AI infrastructure costs are outpacing returns. If you hold shares in hyperscalers, chipmakers, or AI-adjacent software companies, the headline is scary—but the practical question is what to do with your portfolio *this week*. This guide gives you a concrete audit checklist, a hedging framework, and the specific metrics to watch so you can separate noise from genuine risk.

The core issue is simple: companies are spending hundreds of billions on data centers, GPUs, and power contracts, and investors are now demanding proof of revenue. When capex guidance rises faster than operating income, the market reprices the stock. Your job as a holder is to check whether each position in your portfolio can survive a "capex winter" scenario.

## Step-by-Step: Audit Your AI Exposure in 30 Minutes

Follow these steps in order. You don't need a Bloomberg terminal—company filings and free screeners are enough.

1. **List every position with AI revenue or AI capex exposure.** Include obvious names (Nvidia, Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet, Meta) but also "picks and shovels" plays: power utilities, cooling suppliers, networking, and data-center REITs.
2. **Pull the latest 10-Q or earnings call transcript.** Search for the words "capital expenditures," "capex," and "depreciation." Note the year-over-year change in capex guidance.
3. **Calculate a simple capex-to-operating-cash-flow ratio** for each company:

| Ratio (Capex ÷ Operating Cash Flow) | Interpretation | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Below 25% | Healthy, self-funding | Hold |
| 25%–50% | Aggressive but manageable | Hold, monitor quarterly |
| 50%–75% | Stretched; sensitive to rate/earnings shocks | Trim 10–20% |
| Above 75% | High risk if AI revenue disappoints | Trim or hedge |

4. **Check depreciation schedules.** Companies extending GPU useful life from 3 to 5–6 years flatter near-term earnings. If a firm recently changed its depreciation policy, treat reported margins with skepticism.
5. **Set a stop-loss or collar on your two largest AI positions.** A 15% trailing stop or a protective put 10% out of the money limits downside without forcing a full exit.
6. **Rebalance toward cash-flow-positive software and dividend payers.** Shift 10–15% of your AI-heavy allocation into companies whose revenue doesn't depend on AI capex continuing to grow.

## Common Traps, Hedging Alternatives, and What to Watch

**The "buy the dip" trap.** Sharp falls invite reflex buying, but capex-driven selloffs often unfold over multiple quarters as guidance gets cut. Wait for at least one earnings cycle confirming capex growth is decelerating before adding aggressively.

**Depreciation is the hidden risk.** If hyperscalers shorten useful-life assumptions or write down GPU fleets, earnings estimates will fall even if revenue holds. Watch for disclosures about asset impairment in quarterly filings.

**Hedging alternatives to consider:**

- **Broad-market index funds** to dilute single-name AI risk.
- **Equal-weight tech ETFs** instead of cap-weighted ones, which are dominated by the largest AI spenders.
- **Power and utility exposure** as a partial hedge—AI data centers need electricity regardless of which chip vendor wins.
- **Cash or short-duration Treasuries** yielding 4%+ while you wait for clarity.

**What to watch next:** capex guidance updates from the next earnings round, GPU rental pricing trends (falling rental rates signal oversupply), and any commentary on AI product monetization timelines. If rental prices keep dropping while capex rises, the bear case strengthens.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Why are US tech stocks falling over AI cost fears?
Investors are worried that massive spending on data centers, GPUs, and power—often tens of billions per company per year—won't generate matching revenue soon enough. When capex guidance rises faster than earnings, valuations get repriced downward.

### Should I sell my AI stocks right now?
Not necessarily. First check each company's capex-to-operating-cash-flow ratio. Companies below 50% are generally self-funding and can weather a slowdown. Trim positions above 75% and hedge your largest holdings rather than panic-selling everything.

### How do I hedge against AI capex risk?
Use protective puts or collars on your biggest AI positions, diversify into equal-weight tech ETFs, add power/utility exposure, and hold some cash or short-duration bonds. Avoid concentrating more than 20–25% of your portfolio in AI-capex-dependent names.

### Is this AI selloff a buying opportunity?
Possibly, but timing matters. Capex-driven selloffs often span multiple quarters. Wait for one earnings cycle showing capex growth decelerating or AI revenue accelerating before adding to positions.

## Final Verdict

Don't react to the headline—react to the filings. Run the 30-minute audit above, trim any position where capex exceeds 75% of operating cash flow, and hedge your two largest AI holdings with stops or puts. Keep 10–15% in cash or short-duration bonds so you can buy selectively after the next earnings cycle confirms whether this is a correction or the start of a longer capex reset. The AI buildout isn't over, but the era of rewarding spending without proof of returns is.
