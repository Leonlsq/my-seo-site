## How to Keep Your Tires Alive at Interlagos in F1 25 (Wet & Mixed Conditions)

The Brazilian GP at Interlagos is the single most punishing tire-management race on the F1 25 calendar. A topsy-turvy grid, intermittent rain, and a track that eats rear tires on every uphill exit turned this year's race into a survival test — and the winning line was simple: **you've got to keep your tires alive.** If you're loading up the Brazilian GP in Career, My Team, or online lobbies and bleeding lap time after lap 8, this guide gives you the exact setup, stint plan, and crossover rules to fix it. The short version: run lower rear pressures than you think, short-shift the traction zones, and treat the drying line as a trap, not an invitation.

## Step-by-Step: The Interlagos Tire Survival Setup

Interlagos has three tire-killers: the Senna S downhill braking zone, the Ferradura long left, and the Juncao uphill traction exit. Your setup must protect the rears first.

### 1. Baseline dry/wet hybrid setup (F1 25)

| Parameter | Dry Setup | Wet/Mixed Setup | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Front wing | 4 | 6 | Adds front grip for the Senna S braking stability |
| Rear wing | 3 | 5 | Reduces wheelspin on Juncao exit |
| Rear tire pressure | 22.0 psi | 20.5 psi | Larger contact patch = less overheating |
| Front tire pressure | 23.5 psi | 22.0 psi | Prevents graining in the Ferradura |
| Rear suspension (soft) | 4 | 2 | Mechanical grip over the curbs at Turn 2 |
| Diff on-throttle | 55% | 40% | The single biggest rear-tire saver |
| Brake bias | 56% | 54% | Moves load off the rear under braking |
| Ride height | 3 | 5 | Avoids plank damage over the Turn 8 curbs |

### 2. Stint strategy for intermittent rain

1. **Start on intermediates if radar shows rain within 10 laps.** Full wets overheat in under 6 laps on a damp-but-drying track.
2. **Plan a 12–14 lap first stint on inters.** The rear-left is your limiting tire — monitor it in the telemetry overlay.
3. **Crossover to slicks when the racing line is dry for two consecutive sectors.** Do NOT switch on a single dry lap; Interlagos' microclimates fool you.
4. **Undercut is king here.** Pit one lap before the driver ahead when rain is forecast — the out-lap on fresh inters is worth 2.5–3 seconds.

### 3. Driving technique that saves tires

- **Short-shift at 85% revs** out of Juncao and Turn 12. Wheelspin costs 0.4s per lap and adds 3°C of tire temp.
- **Lift-and-coast into Turn 1** instead of trail-braking deep — the Senna S descent loads the fronts brutally.
- **Use the wet line through the Ferradura** (outside, off the rubber) when it's damp; the dry line becomes ice when rain returns.
- **Avoid the curbs at Turn 8** in the wet — they hold water and unsettle the rear on exit.

## Common Issues and What You Should Know

**Tires overheating after lap 6 in the dry?** You're running too much diff and too high rear pressure. Drop on-throttle diff to 40% and rear pressure to 21.5 psi. This alone can extend a stint by 4–5 laps.

**AI opponents diving into the pits early?** In F1 25's Brazilian GP, the AI over-commits to wets. Stay out on inters one extra lap — you'll gain track position every time the rain pauses.

**Rain returning mid-stint?** Don't panic-pit. Inters handle light drizzle fine for 2–3 laps. Only switch to full wets if standing water appears at Turn 3 or the Juncao exit.

**Rear-left graining in the Ferradura?** This is a setup symptom, not a driving one. Soften rear suspension to 2 and reduce front wing by 1 click to rebalance load.

**Career mode note:** Interlagos tire wear scales with your car's upgrade tier. If you're in a midfield car, budget for a 3-stop in mixed conditions — the tire life simply isn't there.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### How many pit stops should you plan for at the F1 Brazil GP in the rain?
Two stops is optimal in intermittent rain: inters to inters around lap 14, then a late slick gamble if the track dries. Plan three only if standing water persists past lap 30.

### What tire pressures stop rear overheating at Interlagos in F1 25?
Run 20.5 psi rear in wet conditions and 21.5 psi in the dry. Anything above 22.5 psi causes the rear-left to grain within 8 laps through the Ferradura.

### Is it better to start on wets or intermediates in Brazil?
Intermediates, unless the radar shows heavy rain for the first 10 laps. Full wets overheat in 5–6 laps on a damp Interlagos surface and force an early stop.

### How do you handle the drying line at Interlagos without crashing?
Stay off the polished dry line until it's fully dry for two sectors. The rubbered line becomes slicker than the wet line when drizzle returns, especially through Turns 4–6.

## Final Verdict

Interlagos rewards patience over aggression. Build the wet-hybrid setup above, cap your on-throttle diff at 40%, and commit to a two-stop inters-first strategy — that combination will keep your tires alive through the chaos and put you in position when the rain inevitably returns. If you're struggling in Career mode with a lower-tier car, drop the aggression slider to 85% for this race specifically; the tire life gain outweighs the lost pace every single time.
