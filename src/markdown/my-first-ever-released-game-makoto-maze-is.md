## Makoto Maze: What the Hand-Drawn Art Style Actually Means for Horror Fans

Makoto Maze is a 2D psychological horror game from a solo developer, set in an "almost mono-chromatic" world inspired by old Japanese folklore. The hook is Miyu, a girl searching for her sister — with the unsettling twist that finding her may be the worst possible outcome. The art style is deliberately "almost childish," hand-drawn, and stripped of color. If you saw the Steam announcement and wondered whether that aesthetic is a gimmick or a genuine design choice, here's the practical breakdown before you wishlist or wait for the demo.

The short answer: the childish, monochrome art is doing real work. In psychological horror, contrast between innocent visuals and disturbing themes is a proven tension engine — think *Yume Nikki* or *OMORI*. Makoto Maze leans into that same dissonance, and the Japanese folklore framing gives it a specific identity rather than generic indie-horror pastiche.

### Why the Art Style Is a Feature, Not a Bug
- **Dissonance = dread.** Soft, childlike linework makes the horror moments hit harder because your brain isn't braced for them.
- **Monochrome focus.** Removing color forces attention onto shape, shadow, and framing — cheap jump scares become harder to hide behind.
- **Folklore grounding.** Japanese folk-horror imagery (spirits, rural isolation, family obligation) pairs naturally with muted palettes and hand-drawn texture.
