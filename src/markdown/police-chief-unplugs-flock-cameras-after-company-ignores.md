## How to Force a Flock Camera Removal When the Company Ignores Your Cancellation

When a police chief in a small city physically unplugged Flock Safety cameras after the company allegedly ignored a contract cancellation, it exposed a problem every municipality, HOA, and business faces: **what do you actually do when a surveillance vendor won't let go?** This guide walks you through the practical, legal, and technical steps to terminate a Flock Safety ALPR (Automated License Plate Reader) contract, get the hardware removed, and force deletion of your data—even when the vendor goes silent.

The core takeaway: cancellation is not one action. It's a documented sequence of written notice, hardware de-energization, data deletion requests, and escalation. Skip any step and you leave leverage on the table.

## Step-by-Step: Forcing a Flock Camera Contract Cancellation

### Step 1: Send Written Notice via Certified Mail (Not Email Alone)
Verbal or email-only cancellation is easy for a vendor to dispute. Send a **certified letter with return receipt** to Flock's registered agent and your account manager. Include:
- Contract number and account ID
- Effective termination date (reference your notice period clause)
- Explicit demand to remove hardware within 30 days
- Explicit demand for **certified data deletion** of all ALPR reads, hotlists, and audit logs

Keep the green return-receipt card. This is your proof of delivery if the dispute escalates.

### Step 2: Document the Hardware State
Before touching anything, photograph and log:
- Camera serial numbers and mounting locations
- Power and network cable runs
- Any city/agency-owned mounting hardware vs. Flock-owned equipment

This protects you if the vendor later claims equipment was damaged or "lost."

### Step 3: De-Energize (Don't Destroy)
The police chief's move—**unplugging**—is legally safer than removing or destroying hardware. De-energizing stops data collection immediately while preserving the vendor's property. If the contract gives you ownership of the hardware, removal is fine; if it's leased, unplug and store it securely.

### Step 4: Demand Data Deletion in Writing
Request a **Certificate of Destruction** confirming deletion of:
- License plate reads and timestamps
- Vehicle images and metadata
- Hotlist and watchlist data
- Shared data with third parties (Flock shares with partner agencies)

Under many state privacy laws and public-records statutes, you're entitled to confirmation.

### Step 5: Escalate Through the Right Channels
| Escalation Path | When to Use | Expected Timeline |
|---|---|---|
| Account manager + certified letter | First 30 days | 1–2 weeks |
| Vendor legal department | No response after notice period | 2–4 weeks |
| State attorney general (privacy complaint) | Data deletion refused | 30–90 days |
| City attorney / contract counsel | Breach of contract | Immediate |
| Public records request | To confirm data handling | 10–30 days |

### Step 6: Involve Legal Counsel for Breach
If the vendor continues billing or refuses removal, a formal **breach-of-contract notice** from your city attorney or general counsel typically resolves it faster than customer service tickets.

## Common Issues, Alternatives, and What You Should Know

**"They kept billing after we cancelled."** Dispute charges in writing with your card issuer or procurement office and attach the certified-mail receipt. Billing after valid termination is often a contract breach.

**"The camera is still transmitting."** Unplugging the power and PoE cable stops transmission. Confirm via your network admin that no traffic hits the vendor's endpoints.

**Alternatives to Flock Safety** if you still want ALPR coverage:
- **Motorola Solutions / Vigilant** — enterprise-grade, agency-focused
- **Genetec AutoVu** — strong on-premises data control
- **Local/self-hosted ALPR** — maximum data sovereignty, higher maintenance

**Cost consideration:** Early termination fees vary by contract; review the termination clause before unplugging to avoid penalty exposure. Many municipal contracts include a 30–90 day notice window.

**Privacy angle:** The controversy driving these cancellations centers on data-sharing with outside agencies and immigration enforcement. If data sovereignty is your concern, negotiate a **no-third-party-sharing clause** before signing any renewal.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Can I legally unplug a Flock camera if the company won't cancel my contract?
In most cases, yes—if the equipment is on your property and you've given valid written notice. De-energizing is generally safer than removing or destroying leased hardware. Consult your contract's termination and equipment-ownership clauses and your legal counsel first.

### How do I get Flock Safety to delete my ALPR data?
Submit a written deletion request citing your contract and applicable state privacy law, and demand a Certificate of Destruction. If refused, escalate to your state attorney general or file a public records request to confirm data handling.

### What happens if I just stop paying Flock Safety?
Non-payment can trigger collections or breach claims. The safer path is formal written termination, documented delivery, and a dispute of post-termination charges—not silent non-payment.

### Does unplugging a Flock camera stop data collection immediately?
Yes. Cutting power and network connectivity halts transmission. Verify with your network administrator that no camera traffic reaches vendor endpoints.

## Final Verdict

Don't rely on a support ticket to end a surveillance contract. **Send certified written notice, de-energize the hardware, demand certified data deletion, and escalate through legal channels** if the vendor stalls. The police chief's unplug was the visible step—but the paperwork and documentation behind it are what actually protect you. If you're evaluating ALPR vendors, prioritize contracts with clear termination clauses and strict no-third-party-sharing terms before you sign.
