## How to Get Into the Founder Summit 2026 Roundtables (and Actually Get Value)

TechCrunch Founder Summit 2026 lands at SoWa Power Station in Boston on November 4, and the main-stage talks are the easy part. The real leverage is in the **roundtables** — smaller, moderated small-group sessions where founders dig into the specific problems they're solving right now (hiring, burn, GTM, AI costs, fundraising in a tight market). The catch: roundtables are capacity-limited, often curated, and most attendees waste their slot by treating them like a panel. This guide is the practical prep playbook — how to get selected, what to bring, and how to walk out with answers you can act on within 48 hours.

If you're attending the Summit, treat the roundtables as your primary ROI and the keynote as background noise. Here's the exact approach.

## Step-by-Step: Prep for a Founder Summit Roundtable

### 1. Pick the right table (not the biggest name)
Roundtables are themed. Match the table to a **live problem you're solving this quarter**, not to the moderator's brand. A "Series A fundraising" table is useless if your real blocker is churn. Prioritize tables where you're 6–18 months from the problem — close enough to have specifics, far enough to change course.

### 2. Write a one-line problem statement
Before you register interest, draft this sentence:

> "We're a [stage] [category] company doing [metric], and we're stuck on [specific blocker] because [root cause]."

Example: *"We're a seed-stage dev-tools company at $40K MRR, and we're stuck on enterprise pilots stalling because our security review takes 6 weeks."* Moderators use statements like this to seat the right mix. Vague "looking to network" requests get deprioritized.

### 3. Bring three artifacts, not a deck
- **A one-page metrics snapshot** (MRR, burn, runway, growth rate, key cohort).
- **Your specific question, written down** — one question, not five.
- **A "give"** — one thing you can offer the table (a benchmark, a tool, an intro). Roundtables reward reciprocity.

### 4. Prepare a 30-second intro
You'll likely go around the table. Structure it as: **who you are → what you're building → the exact problem you want help with → what you can offer.** Practice it out loud. Ramblers lose the room's attention and their follow-up time.

### 5. Arrive with a capture system
Bring a notebook or a notes app with a template: *Idea / Who said it / Next action / Owner / Deadline.* The value of a roundtable evaporates if you can't recall who to follow up with. Tag each takeaway with a name so you can DM them on LinkedIn within 24 hours.

### 6. Ask the second question
The highest-value move in any roundtable is the follow-up. When someone gives a generic answer ("just focus on retention"), ask: *"What did that look like in month one — what did you actually change?"* Specifics are where the information gain lives.

## Roundtable vs. Main Stage: Where to Spend Your Day

| Session type | Group size | Best for | ROI if you're pre-seed/seed | ROI if you're Series A+ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Keynote / main stage | 500+ | Market signal, brand | Low | Low–medium |
| Panel | 100+ | Frameworks, trends | Medium | Low |
| **Roundtable** | **8–20** | **Specific blockers, peer answers** | **High** | **High** |
| Live pitch / networking | Varies | Intros, visibility | Medium | Medium |

The math is simple: a 15-person roundtable gives you roughly 3–5 minutes of direct airtime plus follow-ups. A keynote gives you zero. Budget your day around two roundtables max — more than that and you'll retain nothing.

## Common Mistakes (and What to Do Instead)

- **Treating it as a pitch session.** Don't demo. Ask. Founders who pitch get tuned out; founders who ask sharp questions get remembered.
- **Bringing a co-founder to the same table.** Split up. Two people at two tables doubles your coverage and avoids the "we already know each other" clique effect.
- **Skipping the follow-up.** The roundtable ends; the relationship doesn't. Send a one-line note referencing the specific thing you discussed within 24 hours.
- **Overbooking.** Two roundtables + one networking block is a full day. Three roundtables is a blur.
- **Ignoring the "give."** The founders who get the most are the ones who arrive with a benchmark or intro to offer, not just a question.

### What if you don't get a roundtable slot?
Roundtables are capacity-limited and often curated, so have a backup plan: the hallway track and the networking blocks are where the real conversations happen anyway. Approach a moderator after their session and ask for the one takeaway they'd give a founder in your position — that's a mini-roundtable with a warm intro built in.
