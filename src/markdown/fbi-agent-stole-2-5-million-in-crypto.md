## FBI Agent Stole $2.5M in Crypto: How to Audit Your Wallet Security Today

A former FBI agent has pleaded guilty to stealing nearly $2.5 million in cryptocurrency from the wallets of fraud victims and nation-state hackers under investigation. The uncomfortable takeaway: even the people trusted to seize and safeguard digital assets can become the threat. If you hold crypto—or you're a victim whose seized funds were mishandled—you need a concrete wallet security audit you can run in under an hour. This guide gives you a step-by-step checklist to detect unauthorized access, lock down your holdings, and document everything so a single insider can't drain your wallet.

## Step-by-Step Wallet Security Audit

Run this checklist in order. Do not skip steps—each one closes a different attack path.

1. **Review every wallet's transaction history.** Export CSVs from your exchange accounts and check on-chain history for any transfer you didn't initiate. Flag transactions with timestamps you can't account for.
2. **Check active session and API keys.** On exchanges, revoke all API keys you don't actively use. Disable withdrawal permissions on any key that only needs read access. Kill all active login sessions except your current device.
3. **Rotate seed phrases for high-value wallets.** If a seed phrase was ever typed into a browser, photographed, or stored digitally, treat it as compromised. Generate a fresh wallet and move funds to it.
4. **Move long-term holdings to cold storage.** Hardware wallets keep private keys offline. Transfer anything you don't trade weekly to a device like a Ledger or Trezor.
5. **Enable withdrawal whitelists.** Most major exchanges let you restrict withdrawals to pre-approved addresses. Turn this on so a stolen login can't send funds to an unknown wallet.
6. **Set up address monitoring.** Use a free block explorer alert or a portfolio tracker to notify you of any outgoing transaction in real time.
7. **Document the audit.** Screenshot balances, export transaction logs, and note the date. If you're a victim of a seizure or fraud case, this record is your evidence trail.

### Quick Reference: Risk vs. Mitigation

| Risk Vector | Warning Sign | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Compromised seed phrase | Unknown outbound transfer | Generate new wallet, migrate funds |
| Leaked API key | Unexpected trades or withdrawals | Revoke key, restrict permissions |
| Exchange insider access | Unexplained balance changes | Whitelist addresses, enable 2FA |
| Phishing site | Seed entry prompt on login | Never enter seed online; use bookmarks |
| Seized-funds mismanagement | No case reference or receipt | Demand written chain-of-custody records |

## Common Issues, Alternatives, and What You Should Know

The FBI case exposes a gap most users never consider: **custodial risk during investigations**. When law enforcement seizes crypto, victims often have no visibility into how funds are stored or moved. If your assets are tied to a case, request a written chain-of-custody record and a case number from the handling agent. Keep your own copies of every transaction hash.

For everyday users, the practical alternative to trusting any third party is **self-custody with layered defenses**:

- **Multi-signature wallets** (e.g., a 2-of-3 setup) require multiple approvals before funds move—an insider with one key can't drain the wallet alone.
- **Separate hot and cold wallets** limit exposure. Keep only spending money in a hot wallet.
- **Hardware wallet + passphrase** adds a second secret layer beyond the seed phrase.

Cost note: hardware wallets run $50–$200, multisig setups are free on most chains but add complexity. For holdings above roughly $5,000, the cost is trivial against the loss risk.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### How do I know if my crypto wallet was accessed without permission?
Check your transaction history for outbound transfers you didn't authorize, review exchange login sessions and API keys, and enable real-time address monitoring. Any transfer you can't explain should be treated as a breach.

### Can seized crypto be stolen by law enforcement?
Yes—this case shows it can happen. A former FBI agent pleaded guilty to stealing nearly $2.5 million from wallets of victims and hackers under investigation. If your funds are seized, demand written chain-of-custody documentation and track the wallet address yourself.

### What is the safest way to store crypto after a security scare?
Move long-term holdings to a hardware wallet, enable withdrawal whitelists on exchanges, revoke unused API keys, and consider a multi-signature setup for large balances.
