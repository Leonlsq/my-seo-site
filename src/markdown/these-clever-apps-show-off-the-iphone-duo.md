## iPhone Duo Foldable Apps: What Actually Works and How to Set Them Up

Apple's first foldable iPhone — the **iPhone Duo** — has finally arrived, and the launch-day conversation isn't about the hinge or the crease. It's about the apps. Developers have rushed to build experiences that exploit the Duo's 7.8-inch inner display, and the results range from genuinely useful to delightfully weird (yes, there's a digital toaster). This guide cuts through the hype: here's which apps actually justify the foldable form factor, how to configure them for the best experience, and whether the Duo itself is worth your money in 2026.

The short answer: **the Duo's value lives or dies on multitasking**, and only a handful of apps currently nail it. Below is the practical breakdown.

### How to Set Up FoldNote for Two-Page Note-Taking

1. Install **FoldNote** from the App Store and open it once unfolded.
2. Go to **Settings → Display → Two-Page Layout** and toggle it on.
3. Enable **Pencil Hover Preview** so your Apple Pencil shows a cursor before contact.
4. In the notebook view, swipe from the center spine to split left/right pages.
5. Pin your most-used notebook to the **outer display widget** for quick access when folded.

### How to Configure SkyTrace's Split Flight View

1. Open **SkyTrace** and tap the **radar icon** in the top-right.
2. Select **Duo Split Mode** — this pins the live map to the left pane and flight data to the right.
3. Long-press any flight to **pin it to the outer display** for glanceable status when the phone is closed.
4. Enable **Background Refresh** in iOS Settings → SkyTrace so tracking stays live.

## Common Issues, Workarounds, and What You Should Know

**The "letterboxing" problem.** Many apps still render as stretched phone apps on the inner display. Fix: check **Settings → Display & Brightness → App Scaling** and enable "Optimize for Inner Display" per app. Apps without the toggle simply haven't been updated.

**Battery drain in split view.** Running two live apps (e.g., SkyTrace + video) can cut battery life by roughly 30% versus single-app use. Workaround: enable **Low Power Mode** and disable background refresh for non-essential apps.

**The digital toaster is a gimmick — and that's fine.** Toastr exists to prove developers can. Don't expect productivity; do expect a fun party trick.

**Cost reality check.** The iPhone Duo starts at **$1,799** for 256GB. If you're buying it *only* for these apps, wait. If you already multitask heavily on iPad, the Duo consolidates two devices into one — that's the real value proposition.

**Alternatives to consider:** the Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 7 (mature app ecosystem, cheaper) and the Pixel Fold 2 (better camera). The Duo wins on ecosystem integration and app polish, not price.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### What are the best apps for the iPhone Duo foldable?
The standout apps are **FoldNote** (two-page notes), **Cassette Deck Pro** (retro audio), **SkyTrace** (flight tracking), and **SplitBoard** (dual clipboard). These best demonstrate the inner display's multitasking potential.

### Is the iPhone Duo worth buying in 2026?
It's worth it if you want a single device that replaces both a phone and a small tablet for heavy multitasking. At $1,799, casual users should wait for a price drop or consider the Galaxy Z Fold 7.

### How do I make apps use the full iPhone Duo screen?
Go to **Settings → Display & Brightness → App Scaling** and enable "Optimize for Inner Display" for each app. Apps that lack the toggle haven't been updated for the foldable yet.

### Does the iPhone Duo run all iPhone apps?
Yes — all App Store apps run, but many display in letterboxed phone mode until developers add foldable optimization. Check the App Store's "Made for Duo" collection for verified apps.

## Final Verdict

The iPhone Duo is a **multitasker's device first and a novelty showcase second**. Buy it if you live in split-screen workflows and want Apple's tightest ecosystem integration — FoldNote and SplitBoard alone justify the experiment. Skip it if you're chasing the cassette-player or toaster gimmicks; those are fun demos, not reasons to spend $1,799. My recommendation: **wait for the second-generation Duo** unless you're a power user who'll genuinely use two apps side-by-side daily. The apps are clever; the price still needs to catch up.
