## Is the $200 Savings Real Value — or a Marketing Nudge?

Here's where most "news" coverage stops and this guide starts. A $200 discount is meaningful **only if the base ticket is already in your budget range**. Run this quick cost model before you buy:

- **Ticket:** discounted Founder/Investor rate
- **Travel to Boston:** $150–$600 round trip (domestic)
- **Hotel near SoWa:** $250–$450/night in early November
- **Meals + incidentals:** ~$100/day
- **Opportunity cost:** one full workday

Realistic all-in cost for an out-of-town attendee: **$1,200–$2,500.** The $200 you save covers roughly one hotel night — not nothing, but not transformative.

### Who Actually Gets ROI

- **Pre-seed / seed founders actively raising:** High ROI. 1,000 founders and investors in one room is dense networking.
- **Investors sourcing deal flow:** High ROI if you work the room deliberately.
- **Bootstrapped founders with no fundraising plans:** Lower ROI. Consider the free expo tier instead.
- **Anyone within driving distance of Boston:** Strong ROI — you cut travel and lodging entirely.

## Common Pitfalls and What You Should Know

- **Don't buy an Investor Pass if you can't verify status.** You may be downgraded at the door.
- **The discount doesn't stack** with most other promotions.
- **November in Boston is cold.** SoWa Power Station is a large industrial venue — dress in layers.
- **"Bring your toughest questions"** isn't marketing fluff. Sessions are built around Q&A, so prepare 2–3 specific questions about your business.
- **Compare against alternatives:** a virtual pass, regional founder meetups, or accelerator demo days may deliver similar value for less.
