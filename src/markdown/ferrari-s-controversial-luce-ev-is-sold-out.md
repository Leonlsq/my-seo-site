## Ferrari Luce Sold Out Through 2027: What Buyers Need to Do Now

Ferrari CEO Benedetto Vigna confirmed that the Luce — Maranello's first fully electric car and arguably its most polarizing model — is **"fully allocated" through 2027**. Translation: every build slot for the next two model years is already spoken for. If you're hearing this news and wondering whether you can still get one, the short answer is yes — but not through the normal configurator. This guide breaks down exactly how the allocation system works, realistic wait times, deposit mechanics, and the strongest alternatives if you refuse to wait until 2028.

## How Ferrari's Allocation System Actually Works (And How to Get a Slot)

Ferrari does not sell cars like a normal dealership. You cannot simply walk in with a check and order a Luce. Allocation is relationship-driven, and the sold-out status makes that hierarchy stricter than ever. Here's the practical playbook:

1. **Establish a purchase history with an official dealer.** Existing Ferrari owners (especially those with multiple recent purchases or special-series cars like the Daytona SP3) get priority on new allocations. First-time buyers are effectively at the back of the line.
2. **Get on the official interest list now.** Contact your regional dealer and register formal interest. Lists are time-stamped, and when 2028 slots open, registered prospects are contacted first.
3. **Expect a substantial deposit.** Ferrari typically requires a deposit of roughly 10% of the vehicle's price to lock an allocation once offered. On a car expected to start north of $500,000, budget $50,000+ in committed capital.
4. **Consider the pre-owned flip market — carefully.** Some 2027 allocations will surface via brokers and resale, but expect significant markups, and verify the allocation is transferable with Ferrari before paying anything.
5. **Lease or subscription routes.** Ferrari's financial services occasionally offer lease structures that include future allocation priority — ask specifically about this.

## Key Specs, Positioning, and What You Should Know Before Committing

The Luce is a four-door, four-seat grand tourer — a deliberate departure from Ferrari's two-seat heritage, which is exactly why it's polarizing. What we know from confirmed reporting:

- **Powertrain:** Fully electric, developed with heavy in-house engineering; Ferrari has emphasized sound and driving character as differentiators versus Tesla and Porsche.
- **Body style:** Four-door fastback/GT, positioned as a daily-usable Ferrari.
- **Price:** Expected to start well above $500,000, placing it against the Rolls-Royce Spectre rather than the Taycan.
- **Production:** Limited-volume by design; the 2027 sellout reflects constrained output, not just demand.

**The honest caveats:** First-generation flagship EVs historically suffer from rapid depreciation and software teething issues. Ferrari's resale strength is legendary, but an EV with controversial styling is an unproven asset class. If you're buying purely as an investment, the Luce carries more risk than a limited-run V12.

## Alternatives to Buy Now Instead of Waiting Until 2028

If you want an electric luxury GT in your garage this year, these are the realistic substitutes:

- **Rolls-Royce Spectre** — the closest philosophical rival; ultra-luxury EV coupe, available with far shorter waits.
- **Porsche Taycan Turbo GT** — a fraction of the price, track-capable, and instantly available.
- **Lucid Air Sapphire** — the performance and range benchmark among luxury EVs.
- **Ferrari Roma or Purosangue** — if the badge matters more than the powertrain, these deliver Ferrari ownership without the EV waitlist.
- **Certified pre-owned Ferrari SF90** — hybrid performance with immediate availability and established depreciation curves.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Can I still order a Ferrari Luce if it's sold out for 2027?
Not for 2027 production. You can register on dealer interest lists for 2028+ allocation, or pursue transferable allocations through brokers at a markup. Confirm transferability in writing before paying.

### How much deposit does a Ferrari Luce require?
Expect roughly 10% of the purchase price — approximately $50,000+ on a $500,000+ car — once an allocation is formally offered.

### Will Ferrari increase Luce production to meet demand?
Ferrari has historically protected exclusivity over volume. No production increase has been announced, and the company frames the sellout as intentional scarcity.

### Is the Ferrari Luce a good investment?
Uncertain. Limited volume supports values, but first-gen EVs and polarizing design add risk. A limited-run combustion Ferrari remains the safer collector bet.

## Final Verdict

**Don't chase a marked-up 2027 allocation unless you're a committed collector.** Register on the official 2028 waitlist today, keep your dealer relationship warm, and if you need an electric luxury GT now, buy a Spectre or Taycan Turbo GT and revisit the Luce when the hype premium deflates. The car will still be there in 2028 — likely at a better price.
