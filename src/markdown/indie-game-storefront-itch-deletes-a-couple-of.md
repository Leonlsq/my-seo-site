## Itch.io Just Nuked Terabytes of AI Slop — Here's How to Use the New 'No AI' Filter

Itch.io, the beloved open marketplace for indie games, made a decisive move against generative AI content: it deleted "a couple of terabytes" of AI-generated submissions and rolled out a searchable **"No AI" filter**. If you're a player tired of scrolling past asset-flipped, prompt-generated garbage — or a creator worried about visibility — this guide shows you exactly how to use the filter, what it actually blocks, and what it means for the platform's future.

The short version: the filter is live in search results, it's opt-in per project, and it changes how you should browse the storefront starting today. Here's the full breakdown.

## How the New 'No AI' Filter Works (Step-by-Step)

Itch.io's approach is **creator-declared**, not automated detection. That distinction matters — here's how to actually use it and what to expect.

### For Buyers: Filtering Search Results

1. **Go to the itch.io browse/search page** (e.g., `itch.io/games` or a tag page like `itch.io/games/tag-horror`).
2. **Locate the filter sidebar** on the left side of the results grid.
3. **Toggle the "No AI" option** under the content/accessibility filters. This restricts results to projects whose creators have declared no generative AI was used in assets, code, or writing.
4. **Combine with existing filters** — genre, price (free/paid), platform (Windows/Mac/Linux/Web), and input method — to narrow results further.
5. **Bookmark the filtered URL.** Filter states are reflected in the URL query string, so you can save a "No AI + Free + Horror" link for repeat browsing.

### For Creators: Declaring Your Project

1. Open your **project's edit page** from your dashboard.
2. Navigate to the **metadata / classification section**.
3. **Set the AI disclosure field** to indicate no generative AI was used.
4. **Save and republish.** Your project now appears in filtered "No AI" searches.

> ⚠️ **Important caveat:** Because the filter relies on self-declaration, bad actors can mislabel projects. The purge removed bulk-uploaded slop, but the filter is a trust signal, not a guarantee. Treat it as a strong starting point, not a forensic audit.

## Common Issues, Workarounds & What You Should Know

**"The filter isn't showing up for me."** Filter visibility can vary by page type. Try the main `/games` browse page first, then apply genre tags. Clear your browser cache if the sidebar looks stale.

**"A game I know uses AI still appears under the filter."** Self-declaration is the weak link. Report mislabeled projects via the project page's report button — itch.io moderators act on flagged listings.

**"Will this hurt small creators who use AI tools for, say, code assistance?"** Itch.io's disclosure language generally targets generative content in shipped assets (art, music, writing). Creators should read the current disclosure policy carefully and declare accurately — false "No AI" tags risk account penalties.

**Alternatives if you want stricter guarantees:**
- **Curated bundles and jams** — game jam entries are community-vetted and often AI-free by rule.
- **Follow specific creators** rather than browsing broadly; your feed becomes a trusted filter.
- **Third-party curation lists** on social platforms that manually verify human-made projects.

**For creators losing visibility:** The purge is a net positive for genuine indies. Update your project page with clear "human-made" messaging, tag accurately, and lean into devlogs — buyers are actively seeking authenticity signals right now.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### How do I filter out AI games on itch.io?
Open any browse or search results page, find the **"No AI" toggle** in the left-hand filter sidebar, and enable it. Combine it with genre, price, and platform filters, then bookmark the resulting URL to reuse your custom search.

### Did itch.io really delete terabytes of AI games?
Yes — itch.io confirmed removing roughly **a couple of terabytes** of AI-generated "slop" submissions, primarily bulk-uploaded asset-flips and spam listings, alongside introducing the No AI search filter.

### Is the itch.io No AI filter reliable?
It's based on **creator self-declaration**, so it's a strong signal but not a guarantee. Report any mislabeled projects you find; moderators review flagged listings.

### Does the No AI filter cost anything or require an account?
No. The filter is free and available to all visitors — no login required to browse filtered results.
