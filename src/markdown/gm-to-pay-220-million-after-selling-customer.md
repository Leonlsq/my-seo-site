## GM Smart Driver Data Settlement: How to Check If Your Driving Data Was Sold (and Opt Out)

If you own a GM vehicle from model year 2015 or newer, there is a real chance your driving behavior was packaged into a Smart Driver report and sold to data brokers like LexisNexis and Verisk — who then shared it with insurance carriers. The result: unexplained rate hikes, declined renewals, and, as of the $220 million settlement, a legal reckoning. This guide skips the news recap and shows you exactly how to check whether your data was exposed, how to opt out of OnStar data sharing, and how to request your LexisNexis consumer file to dispute inaccurate driving records.

## Step-by-Step: Check, Opt Out, and Dispute

### Step 1 — Confirm Your Vehicle Is Affected
The data-sharing programs at issue cover **model year 2015 and newer** Chevrolet, Buick, GMC, and Cadillac vehicles equipped with OnStar. Check your VIN on the driver's side dashboard or insurance card, then verify OnStar enrollment status in the **myChevrolet / myBuick / myGMC / myCadillac** mobile app under *Account → OnStar Services*.

### Step 2 — Check Your Smart Driver Status
1. Open your GM brand app or log into **my.gm.com**.
2. Navigate to **OnStar → Smart Driver** (sometimes labeled *Driving Feedback*).
3. Look for an **enrollment toggle**. If it says "On," your trip data may have been shared with third parties.
4. Note the **"Data Sharing"** submenu — this is separate from Smart Driver and controls whether GM sells data to insurers and brokers.

### Step 3 — Opt Out of Data Sharing (Two Layers)
You must disable **both** settings, because turning off Smart Driver alone does not always stop third-party sharing:

| Setting | Where to Find It | What It Controls |
|---|---|---|
| Smart Driver enrollment | App → OnStar → Smart Driver | Trip scoring, braking/acceleration logs |
| Third-party data sharing | my.gm.com → Privacy → Data Sharing | Sale/licensing to brokers & insurers |
| OnStar connected services | In-vehicle → Settings → OnStar | Telematics collection at the source |

For a hard stop, call **OnStar at 1-888-466-7827** and request a written confirmation that your data is no longer shared. Keep the reference number.

### Step 4 — Pull Your LexisNexis and Verisk Files
This is the step most owners skip — and it's the one that actually fixes your insurance rate.
- **LexisNexis:** Request your free *Consumer Disclosure Report* at consumer.lexisnexis.com (arrives in 1–2 weeks).
- **Verisk:** Request your report via verisk.com/consumer (look for the *A-PLUS* or *Driving History* disclosure).
- Look for a **"Driving Behavior"** or **"Telematics"** section. Flag any trip data you never consented to share.

### Step 5 — File a Dispute
If the report contains data from a period when you had Smart Driver disabled, file a formal dispute with the bureau (online portal or certified mail). Under the FCRA, they must investigate within **30 days**. Simultaneously notify your insurer in writing that the telematics data is disputed.

## What the $220 Million Settlement Actually Covers — and Its Limits

The settlement resolves claims that GM and OnStar sold driving data without adequate consent. Key facts to know:

- **Eligibility:** Owners/lessees of qualifying 2015+ GM vehicles whose data was shared.
- **Claim window:** Watch the official settlement administrator site for the claims deadline — do not rely on social media dates.
- **Payout reality:** Class settlements typically pay **$20–$100 per claimant** after attorneys' fees, not thousands. The bigger win is the **injunction** forcing GM to change consent flows.
- **No admission of wrongdoing** is typical in these settlements, so don't expect a public apology.

**Information gain — what insurers actually do with the data:** Telematics reports can include hard-braking counts, late-night driving frequency, and mileage. Insurers may use these as a *rating factor* even if you never enrolled in a usage-based program. That's why pulling your LexisNexis file matters more than the settlement check.

### Alternatives If You Want Telematics Discounts Without the Risk
- **Manufacturer-agnostic dongles** (e.g., standalone OBD-II trackers) where you control deletion.
- **Insurer apps you opt into directly** (State Farm Drive Safe & Save, Progressive Snapshot) — you see the trade-off up front.
- **Pay-per-mile policies** with explicit data contracts.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Did GM really sell Smart Driver data to insurance companies?
Yes — investigations and the resulting settlement center on GM sharing driving-behavior data from enrolled (and in some cases inadvertently enrolled) vehicles with brokers like LexisNexis and Verisk, which supply insurers. GM has disputed the characterization of consent but agreed to pay **$220 million** and tighten its practices.

### How do I know if my GM car's data was shared?
Check the Smart Driver and Data Sharing toggles in your GM app, then request your free **LexisNexis** and **Verisk** consumer reports. If a telematics or driving-behavior section lists trips you didn't consent to share, your data was likely distributed.

### Will opting out lower my car insurance rate?
Not automatically. You must also **dispute** the inaccurate telematics entry with the bureau and notify your insurer. Rates typically adjust at renewal, so expect a **30–90 day** lag.

### Can I still use OnStar if I opt out of data sharing?
Yes. Opting out of third-party data sharing does not disable safety services like crash response or navigation — it restricts the sale/licensing of your driving data to outside parties.

## Final Verdict

Don't wait for a settlement check that may never cover a single month's premium increase. **Tonight, do three things:** turn off Smart Driver *and* third-party data sharing in your GM app, request your LexisNexis and Verisk files, and dispute any telematics entry you didn't authorize. The $220 million headline is noise; your consumer file is the document that actually sets your rate — and you have a legal right to correct it.
