## Rocket Lab Neutron Delay: How to Replan Your Payload Manifest Without Losing a Year

Rocket Lab has pushed the first flight of its medium-lift Neutron rocket yet again, and if you're a satellite operator, university lab, or rideshare broker, the headline matters less than the calendar math. Neutron was announced in 2021 with a target first flight of 2024. That target has slipped repeatedly, and the latest delay pushes operational commercial flights further into the late 2020s. The practical takeaway: **do not build a 2027 manifest that assumes Neutron is your primary vehicle.** Treat it as a bonus capacity option, not a baseline. This guide walks you through replanning your launch strategy, comparing real alternatives, and locking in slots before they disappear.

## Step-by-Step: Rebuilding Your Launch Plan After the Neutron Delay

### Step 1: Classify your payload by mass and orbit
Neutron's advertised niche is ~13,000 kg to low Earth orbit (LEO) with a reusable first stage, and roughly 1,500 kg to Mars or Venus on an expendable configuration. If your payload is under ~300 kg to LEO, you were never Neutron's core customer — you're a rideshare passenger. Sort yourself first:

| Payload Class | Mass to LEO | Best Current Option | Neutron Dependency |
|---|---|---|---|
| CubeSat / SmallSat | 1–300 kg | Rideshare (Transporter/Bandwagon, Vega, Electron) | None |
| Mid-class | 300–2,000 kg | Dedicated small launchers, Falcon 9 rideshare ESPA | Low |
| Heavy smallsat | 2,000–8,000 kg | Falcon 9 dedicated, Ariane 6, Vulcan | Medium |
| Constellation batch | 8,000–13,000 kg | Falcon 9, New Glenn, Starship | High |

If you land in the bottom two rows, the delay is a real problem and you need a contingency contract now.

### Step 2: Audit your launch contract clauses
Pull your launch services agreement and check three things:
1. **Force majeure vs. delay language** — does a vehicle development slip trigger a refund, a rebooking right, or nothing?
2. **Reassignment rights** — can Rocket Lab move you to Electron or a rideshare if Neutron slips?
3. **Milestone payment triggers** — are you paying on integration milestones or calendar dates? Push for milestone-based payments if you haven't signed yet.

### Step 3: Lock a backup slot 18–24 months out
Rideshare and dedicated slots sell out 12–24 months ahead. If your launch window is H2 2027, you should be booking a Falcon 9 rideshare or an Ariane 6 rideshare **now** as a hedge, even if you keep your Neutron reservation.

### Step 4: Re-baseline your schedule with a 12-month buffer
Every medium-lift vehicle in history — Falcon 9, Electron, Ariane 6, Vulcan — took longer than its first announced date. Build a 12-month slip buffer into your critical path and re-run your funding and revenue model against the later date.

## Alternatives and What You Should Know

**What Neutron still offers:** A reusable first stage, a large 5-meter fairing, and a dedicated-ride price point below Falcon 9 for mid-class payloads. If it flies, it's a strong option. The problem is purely schedule risk.

**Realistic alternatives right now:**

- **Falcon 9 rideshare (Transporter/Bandwagon):** Cheapest per-kg for smallsats, high cadence, but you accept a fixed orbit and shared schedule.
- **Electron:** Rocket Lab's own small launcher, ~300 kg to LEO, high cadence, but too small for Neutron-class payloads.
- **Ariane 6 / Vega-C:** European institutional priority; commercial slots are tighter.
- **New Glenn / Vulcan:** Medium-to-heavy capacity, but also ramping and competing for the same manifest slots.
- **Starship:** Massive capacity, but rideshare economics and schedule are still maturing.

**Cost reality check:** A dedicated Falcon 9 runs roughly $67M list; rideshare seats run from ~$1M for a 200 kg ESPA-class port. Neutron's promised pricing was positioned to undercut dedicated Falcon 9 — but a delayed cheaper rocket is more expensive than an available one, because your satellite depreciates on the ground.

**Hidden risk:** Insurance. Launch delay can void or reprice your launch insurance policy. Notify your underwriter the moment your primary vehicle slips.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### When will Rocket Lab's Neutron rocket actually fly?
Rocket Lab has not committed to a firm operational date after the latest delay. Based on the pattern since the 2021 announcement (original target 2024), treat any announced date as a development milestone, not a commercial commitment, and plan for a slip.

### Is Neutron still worth booking for a 2027 launch?
Only as a secondary option. Keep the reservation if it's low-cost, but secure a backup rideshare or dedicated slot 18–24 months out. Never make Neutron your sole path to orbit for a revenue-generating payload.

### What is the cheapest alternative to Neutron for a 500 kg satellite?
A Falcon 9 rideshare port is typically the cheapest per-kilogram option for a 500 kg payload to a standard rideshare orbit. If you need a custom orbit, a dedicated small launcher or an ESPA-class dedicated rideshare is the next step up.

### Does the Neutron delay affect Electron launches?
No. Electron is a separate, operational vehicle with its own cadence. The delay is specific to Neutron's development and first flight.

## Final Verdict

**Do not bet your mission timeline on Neutron.** Treat it as upside capacity, not a plan. Reclassify your payload, audit your contract for delay and reassignment clauses, and lock a backup rideshare or dedicated slot 18–24 months before your target window. If you're under 300 kg, ignore the Neutron news entirely and book a rideshare today. If you're flying 2,000–13,000 kg, dual-source your manifest across Falcon 9, Ariane 6, or New Glenn while keeping Neutron as a cost-down option for a later batch. The operators who win the next two years are the ones who plan for the rocket that exists, not the one on the roadmap.
