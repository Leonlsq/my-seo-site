## Did Flock Safety Cancel Police Contracts? What the CEO Actually Said

If you searched for "Flock Safety fired police departments" or "did Flock cancel contracts," you landed in the middle of a genuine information gap. Flock Safety CEO Garrett Langley publicly stated that the surveillance company **has not canceled contracts with any police department or law enforcement agency**, directly pushing back on reports that agencies were being dropped as customers over data-abuse allegations. The nuance matters: Flock says it terminates *individual user access* and *agency misuse*, not entire departmental contracts.

That distinction is exactly where the confusion lives — and where you, as a resident, journalist, council member, or privacy-conscious user, need actionable steps rather than headlines. This guide walks you through **how to verify your own city's Flock contract status, audit deployed ALPR (Automated License Plate Reader) cameras, and file a public records request** to get the real answer for your jurisdiction.

## How to Verify Your Local Flock Safety Contract (Step-by-Step)

Flock Safety contracts are typically held at the **city, county, or agency level**, not federally — which means "Flock fired the police" is almost never a blanket national event. Here's how to check your specific situation.

### Step 1: Identify the Contracting Entity
- Search your **city council agenda packets** (usually a searchable PDF portal) for "Flock Safety," "ALPR," or "license plate reader."
- Check the **police department's annual budget** line items — Flock subscriptions often appear as "technology services" or "surveillance software."
- Look for **grant funding** (e.g., Byrne JAG, state homeland security grants), since many agencies buy Flock with grant money rather than general funds.

### Step 2: File a Public Records Request
Use a template like this:

> "Pursuant to [your state's public records act], I request all contracts, purchase orders, invoices, and memoranda of understanding between [Agency Name] and Flock Safety (or Flock Group Inc.) from January 2020 to present, including any termination, suspension, or non-renewal notices."

Agencies typically respond in **5–30 days** depending on state law. If Flock "fired" your department, a termination notice will exist in writing — if it doesn't, the contract is likely still active.

### Step 3: Cross-Check Flock's Transparency Portal
Flock operates a **Transparency Portal** where agencies can publish their audit logs and sharing settings. Not all agencies opt in, but when they do, you can see:
- Which neighboring agencies your local cameras share data with
- How many searches were run and by whom
- Whether "national lookup" or "federated search" is enabled

### Step 4: Confirm the Termination Type
This is the key troubleshooting step. Flock distinguishes between:

| Action | What It Means | Contract Status |
|---|---|---|
| User access revoked | Individual officer misused the system | Contract **active** |
| Agency audit triggered | Internal investigation opened | Contract **active** |
| Contract non-renewal | Agency chose not to renew | Contract **ends at term** |
| Contract termination | Flock or agency ends early | Contract **void** |

Langley's statement addresses the bottom two rows: he claims Flock has not terminated *agency-level* contracts. Reports of "firing" usually refer to the top rows.

## Common Issues, Alternatives, and What You Should Know

### Why the Confusion Exists
- **"Fired" is ambiguous.** Media and advocates use it to mean "cut off access"; Flock uses it to mean "canceled the contract." Both can be technically true at different scopes.
- **Individual bans ≠ agency bans.** Flock has acknowledged revoking individual user credentials after misuse investigations, which is not the same as dropping a department.
- **State-level bans are separate.** Some states (and Canada) have restricted Flock or ALPR use via legislation, not Flock's own decisions.

### Alternatives to Flock Safety
If your city is evaluating options, compare:

- **Motorola Solutions (formerly Vigilant)** — larger footprint, higher cost, deeper integration with dispatch.
- **Axon Fusus** — bundles with body cams and evidence management.
- **Leonardo/Genetec** — enterprise-grade, common in larger metros.
- **Open-source ALPR (e.g., OpenALPR/Rekor)** — cheaper but weaker support and accuracy at scale.

### How to Opt Out or Push Back Locally
1. **Attend city council meetings** during budget season (usually spring).
2. **Request the MOU** to see data-sharing scope.
3. **Cite the audit logs** if misuse is documented.
4. **Propose a moratorium** — several cities have paused Flock deployments pending review.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Did Flock Safety actually fire any police departments?
According to CEO Garrett Langley, **no** — Flock has not canceled contracts with any police department or law enforcement agency. The company says it revokes *individual user access* for misuse, which is different from terminating an agency-wide contract.

### How do I find out if my city still has a Flock Safety contract?
File a public records request with your local police department or city clerk asking for all Flock Safety contracts and termination notices. Cross-check the Flock Transparency Portal if your agency participates.

### Can a police department lose Flock access without losing its contract?
Yes. Flock can suspend or revoke individual officer credentials while the agency's subscription remains active. This is the most common source of "Flock fired the police" confusion.

### Is Flock Safety being banned anywhere?
Some states, cities, and Canadian provinces have restricted or paused ALPR deployments through legislation or local ordinance — but these are government actions, not Flock terminating contracts.

## Final Verdict

The "Flock fired police departments" narrative is **mostly a scope problem, not a factual one**. Langley's claim is narrow and specific: no *agency-level* contracts have been canceled. If you want the truth for your town, don't rely on national headlines — **file the records request, check the Transparency Portal, and read the MOU**. That's the only way to know whether your local Flock cameras are still watching, still sharing, and still under contract. If privacy is your priority, push for a council review before the next renewal date, because that's the moment your city actually has leverage.
