## Delaware's "No Human at the Helm" AI Business Plan: What It Actually Means for Founders

Delaware is considering a legal framework that would let AI agents run an entire business entity — no human director, no human officer, no human "at the helm." The headline sounds like science fiction, but the practical question most founders and operators are actually searching for is simpler: **can I register a business that an AI operates, and what liability do I take on if I do?** Here's the direct takeaway: the proposal is not yet law, and if it passes, it will likely create a new category of entity with mandatory disclosure, a human "supervisory" contact, and — per critics like US Rep. James Comer — potential liability shields for owners whose AI acts "recklessly." That last point is the one that should shape your filing strategy today.

This guide breaks down what the plan would change, how to prepare your entity structure, and the concrete steps to take before (and if) the law passes.

## What the Delaware AI Agent Business Proposal Would Change

Delaware dominates US corporate registrations because of its Court of Chancery and flexible LLC/corporation statutes. The proposal extends that framework to "autonomous" entities. Based on the reported details and the surrounding debate, here's the practical breakdown:

| Feature | Current Delaware Law | Proposed AI-Agent Framework |
|---|---|---|
| Human director/officer required | Yes | Potentially waived for AI-run entities |
| Decision-maker | Human fiduciary | AI agent (with defined authority) |
| Liability for AI "reckless" acts | Owner/manager exposed | Possible shield for owners (per Comer's criticism) |
| Disclosure requirement | Minimal | Likely mandatory AI-operation disclosure |
| Human oversight contact | N/A | Expected "responsible party" on record |

**Key context:** Rep. Comer's objection is that owners of AI-run firms "would evade liability for reckless AI." That is the central tension. A liability shield is attractive to founders, but it also means courts and regulators will demand *something* in exchange — most likely strict disclosure, insurance, and a named human accountable for the AI's actions.

### Why This Matters to You Right Now
- If you're building an AI-operated service business (e.g., an autonomous e-commerce store, an AI-run SaaS, an agentic trading or content firm), your entity choice today affects how you migrate later.
- If you're an investor or co-founder, the liability question determines whether you want your name on the filing at all.
- If you're a developer deploying agentic workflows, the "reckless AI" standard is the phrase to watch — it will define your logging and audit requirements.

## Step-by-Step: How to Prepare Your Business for an AI-Agent Entity

You cannot file under a law that doesn't exist yet. But you *can* structure now so you're ready on day one. Here's the sequence.

1. **Keep your current human-managed entity as the parent.** Form a standard Delaware LLC or C-Corp today with a human manager. When the AI framework passes, you spin up the AI-run subsidiary under it. This preserves your liability firewall and avoids re-filing from scratch.
2. **Document the AI's decision authority in writing.** Draft an operating agreement or bylaws addendum that explicitly scopes what the AI agent can and cannot do (spending limits, contract ceilings, hiring, data access). Courts will look for this.
3. **Build an audit log from day one.** Every AI decision should be timestamped, versioned, and stored. If "reckless AI" becomes a legal standard, your logs are your defense. Use append-only storage.
4. **Name a human "responsible party."** Even if the law allows no human at the helm, regulators will want a contact. Designate a CTO or compliance officer internally.
5. **Line up insurance early.** Ask brokers about AI liability / tech E&O riders that cover autonomous agent actions. This will likely be a filing prerequisite.
6. **Track the bill's status.** Monitor the Delaware General Assembly and Secretary of State filings. Do not file or market yourself as an "AI-run company" until the statute is enacted — misrepresenting entity status creates its own liability.
7. **Consult corporate counsel licensed in Delaware.** This is non-negotiable for a novel entity type.

## Common Issues, Risks, and What You Should Know

**The liability shield is not a free pass.** Comer's criticism signals political pushback. If the shield survives, expect conditions: mandatory disclosure, minimum capital or insurance, and a defined "reckless" standard that could still pierce protection for gross negligence.

**"No human at the helm" ≠ no human responsibility.** Every jurisdiction that has touched autonomous entities (DAOs, AI agents) has ultimately required a human anchor for service of process and enforcement.

**Migration is messy.** If you operate an AI-run business today under a standard LLC, converting to a new AI-entity class may trigger tax events, contract re-assignments, and re-registration. Plan for a 60–90 day transition window.

**Alternatives if the law stalls:**
- **Wyoming LLC** — strong privacy, no state corporate income tax, human manager required.
- **Standard Delaware LLC with a "technology committee"** — a human board that formally delegates operational authority to an AI system, documented in the operating agreement.
- **Foreign entity (e.g., certain EU or Caribbean structures)** — only if you have genuine operations there; otherwise it invites scrutiny.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Can I register an AI-run business in Delaware right now?
No. The proposal is not enacted law. You can form a standard Delaware LLC or corporation with a human manager today, but there is currently no filing category for an entity with no human at the helm.

### Would owners really escape liability if their AI acts recklessly?
That is the core criticism from Rep. Comer, not a settled outcome. Any liability shield would almost certainly come with conditions — disclosure, insurance, and a defined "reckless" standard — so owners should assume some exposure remains.

### What is the "reckless AI" standard and why does it matter?
It's the likely legal threshold for when an AI-run entity's protection could be challenged. For operators, it means your audit logs, decision limits, and human oversight records become your primary legal defense.

### Do I need a human officer if the law passes?
Likely yes, in some form — most proposals still require a named "responsible party" for service of process and regulatory contact, even if operational control sits with the AI.

## Final Verdict

**Don't file anything speculative — but start structuring now.** The Delaware AI-agent business plan is a signal, not a green light. The smart move for founders is to keep a human-managed Delaware entity as your parent, document your AI's decision authority, and build audit logs immediately so you're ready to migrate the moment the statute passes. If you're weighing the risk, treat the "liability shield" as a negotiation point, not a guarantee — Comer's pushback proves the politics are unsettled. Consult Delaware counsel before any filing, and revisit your insurance and governance docs every quarter until the law's final shape is clear.
