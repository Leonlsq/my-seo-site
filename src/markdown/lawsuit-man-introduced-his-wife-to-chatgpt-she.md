## The ChatGPT Poisoning Lawsuit: What It Means for Your Account Security

A lawsuit alleging that a man introduced his wife to ChatGPT — and that she then used it to plan his murder via bitter energy drinks — has pushed AI safety from an abstract debate into a household concern. Whatever the eventual legal outcome, the practical question thousands of users are now typing into Google is simple: **how do I stop ChatGPT from being misused in my own home?**

This guide skips the news recap. Instead, it gives you a concrete, step-by-step hardening checklist for ChatGPT and similar AI assistants — covering account controls, shared-device hygiene, prompt-level red flags, and what to do if you suspect misuse. The direct takeaway: **ChatGPT is not a surveillance tool and will not "snitch" on a family member, but you can materially reduce risk with the settings and habits below.**

## Step-by-Step: 7 ChatGPT Safety Settings and Habits to Enable Now

### 1. Turn on Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)
- Open ChatGPT → click your profile icon → **Settings** → **Security** → enable **Multi-Factor Authentication**.
- Use an authenticator app (TOTP) rather than SMS where possible.
- Why it matters: shared or compromised accounts are the most common way third parties see someone else's chat history.

### 2. Audit "Shared Links" and Data Controls
- Go to **Settings** → **Data Controls**.
- Disable **Improve the model for everyone** if you want conversations excluded from training.
- Review **Shared Links** regularly — any conversation you've publicly shared stays accessible until you delete it.

### 3. Use Temporary Chat for Sensitive Sessions
- Start a **Temporary Chat** (available in the sidebar) for anything you don't want stored in history.
- Temporary chats don't appear in your history and aren't used to train models.
- Caveat: this protects *your* privacy — it does not flag dangerous content to anyone.

### 4. Separate Accounts per Household Member
- Never share one login across a family. Each adult should have their own account with their own MFA.
- For teens, use **parental controls / linked supervision** where available in your region.
- On shared devices, always **log out** or use a separate browser profile.

### 5. Know the Red-Flag Prompt Patterns
AI safety filters block explicit requests for violence, but users should recognize escalation patterns in a household member's behavior:
- Repeated questions about **undetectable substances, dosages, or symptoms**
- Queries about **covering tracks, alibis, or evidence removal**
- Sudden interest in **victim-specific routines** ("what does X eat/drink daily")
- Attempts to **rephrase blocked prompts** ("hypothetically," "for a novel")

If you see these, treat them as a real-world safety concern, not a tech problem.

### 6. Enable Device-Level Monitoring (Legally)
- On family devices you own and administer, use OS-level screen time and activity logs (iOS Screen Time, Google Family Link, Windows Family Safety).
- These are the *only* reliable way to see what an at-risk household member is doing — ChatGPT itself will not alert you.

### 7. Set Up an Exit Plan for Suspicion
- If you suspect poisoning or intent to harm: **do not confront via chat logs**. Contact local emergency services or a poison control center immediately.
- Preserve evidence (screenshots, timestamps) and consult law enforcement before deleting anything.

| Setting | Where to Find It | Risk Reduced |
|---|---|---|
| MFA | Settings → Security | Account takeover |
| Training opt-out | Settings → Data Controls | Data exposure |
| Temporary Chat | Sidebar | History retention |
| Separate accounts | Account creation | Cross-user visibility |

## Common Issues, Legal Realities, and What You Should Know

**ChatGPT will not warn you about a family member.** OpenAI's systems flag policy-violating content internally, but there is no consumer feature that notifies a spouse or parent that someone asked about poisons. Expecting the AI to act as a watchdog is a dangerous misconception.

**"Bitter drinks" is a detection clue, not a tech fix.** In the alleged case, the tampering was reportedly masked by strong flavors. Practical takeaway: if a drink tastes unexpectedly bitter or metallic, **do not finish it** — seal it, note the time, and contact poison control (US: 1-800-222-1222).

**Prompt injection and jailbreaks are not your main threat.** For households, the realistic risks are shared logins, unmonitored teen accounts, and normalizing violent "hypothetical" queries. Focus your effort there.

**Alternatives with stronger guardrails:** Claude and Gemini apply similar safety filters; none offer spouse-alert features. Enterprise tiers (ChatGPT Team/Enterprise) add admin logging — useful for businesses, not homes.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Can ChatGPT report someone to the police for planning a crime?
No. ChatGPT has no mechanism to contact law enforcement about an individual user. OpenAI may act on policy violations internally, but there is no consumer alert system for family members.

### How do I see what someone asked ChatGPT on a shared computer?
Only through device-level tools you administer (Screen Time, Family Link, browser history) or by accessing the account directly. ChatGPT does not provide a "monitor another user" feature.

### Does deleting a ChatGPT conversation remove it permanently?
Deleted conversations are removed from your history and typically purged from OpenAI's systems within about 30 days, though abuse-monitoring logs may be retained longer under OpenAI's data policies.

### Is the ChatGPT poisoning lawsuit real or a hoax?
Multiple outlets have reported the lawsuit filing; treat the allegations as unproven until adjudicated. Regardless of outcome, the safety steps above are valid for any household.

## Final Verdict

Don't panic-uninstall ChatGPT — but do stop treating it as a private diary on a shared device. **Spend 10 minutes today**: enable MFA, split household accounts, turn off training data where you prefer, and set up device-level family monitoring. Those four moves close the realistic risk gaps. If you ever see genuine red flags around poisoning or intent to harm, skip the tech troubleshooting entirely and call emergency services or poison control first.
