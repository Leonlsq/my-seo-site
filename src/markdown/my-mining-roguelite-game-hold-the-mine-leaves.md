## Hold The Mine 1.0: What Actually Changes and How to Prepare

Hold The Mine launches out of Early Access on **December 10**, and if you have been sitting on the fence, the 1.0 patch is the version to jump into. The pitch is simple but specific: a turn-based mining phase by day, an auto-battler defense phase by night, wrapped in hand-drawn 2D animation. Think *Dome Keeper* meets a deckbuilder's resource economy, but with dwarves, relics, and 60+ buildings that actually change how your run plays out.

The problem most new players hit is not difficulty — it is **decision paralysis**. With 14 heroes, 65+ relics, 60+ buildings, and 20+ random events, your first three runs can collapse by night four simply because you mined the wrong tiles or over-invested in a hero that does not scale. This guide gives you a concrete opening build, a mining priority order, and the 1.0 content breakdown so you can hit the ground running on launch day.

## Step-by-Step: The Best Opening Build for Your First Runs

Follow this sequence for your first 5–8 runs. It is designed to be forgiving while still teaching the core economy.

1. **Start in Classic Mode, not Conquest.** Classic has the gentlest curve and lets you learn relic synergies without modifier stacking. Conquest adds win-condition pressure that punishes slow economies. Endless is for after you have cleared Classic at least once.
2. **Mine vertical before horizontal.** Vertical shafts reveal resource-dense blocks faster and give your dwarves shorter pathing. Horizontal sprawl early means your defenders spend combat turns repositioning instead of fighting.
3. **Prioritize ore over gems in the first two days.** Ore feeds building construction; gems feed relic purchases. Buildings compound earlier than relics do, so ore-first wins the first two nights almost every time.
4. **Build one production structure and one defensive structure per day cycle.** Do not stack three defenses on night one — you will starve your economy by night three.
5. **Unleash your first hero before the second night.** Heroes are your damage floor. A run with zero heroes active by night two is a run you are actively losing.
6. **Bank one relic purchase for the first elite wave.** Elite waves around night 5–7 are where unprepared groups wipe. Holding a relic slot open lets you counter-pick based on what the wave throws at you.

### Mining Priority Cheat Sheet

| Priority | Tile Type | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ore clusters | Funds buildings, the core of your economy |
| 2 | Gem veins | Relic purchases, mid-game power spikes |
| 3 | Block-dense paths | Opens map, reveals events, shortens dwarf travel |
| 4 | Isolated resources | Only after your main shaft is 6+ tiles deep |

## Hero, Building, and Relic Synergy: What Actually Matters in 1.0

The 1.0 numbers are not marketing fluff — they change how you draft. Here is how to read them practically.

- **14 Heroes:** You will not see all 14 in a single run. Draft heroes that match your existing buildings, not heroes that look strong in isolation. A hero with no supporting structure is a wasted unleash.
- **60+ Buildings:** Buildings are the multiplier layer. Two buildings that share a resource type will outperform three that do not.
- **65+ Relics:** Relics are your answer to bad wave RNG. Keep at least one relic slot flexible for counter-picks.
- **20+ Blocks and 20+ Random Events:** These add variance run-to-run. If you get a bad event early, do not panic-restart — the event pool is designed so that a bad event on day one is often offset by a strong relic offer on day three.

### Common Mistakes That Kill Runs Before Night 5

- **Over-mining depth without defense.** Depth is worthless if your dwarves die defending a corridor they cannot reach.
- **Ignoring the auto-battler phase.** Combat runs itself, but positioning and hero placement are still your job. Check your formation every night.
- **Buying relics before buildings.** Relics amplify; buildings generate. Amplifying zero is still zero.
- **Skipping random events.** Events are free value more often than they are traps.

## Hold The Mine vs Dome Keeper: Which Should You Play?

If you are coming from *Dome Keeper*, the comparison is fair but the games diverge fast.

| Feature | Hold The Mine | Dome Keeper |
|---|---|---|
| Mining style | Turn-based | Real-time |
| Combat | Auto-battler | Real-time defense |
| Unit control | Dwarf group + heroes | Single defender |
| Build depth | 60+ buildings | Limited upgrades |
| Visual style | Hand-drawn 2D, frame-by-frame | Pixel art |
| Strategic layer | Resource + relic economy | Upgrade tree |

**Verdict on the comparison:** Hold The Mine is the more strategic, more colorful option. Dome Keeper is tighter and faster. If you want a run you can finish in 20 minutes, stay with Dome Keeper. If you want a run you can think about between sessions, Hold The Mine 1.0 is the pick.
