## Sony's USB-C Walkman Headphones: Nostalgia Tax or Genuine Upgrade?

Sony has revived the original Walkman-era headphone design — the iconic flat-folding, orange-cushioned silhouette — and quietly modernized it with a USB-C port. That single port is the whole story: it means the new model is a **wired USB-C digital headphone**, not a Bluetooth device. If you found this page after seeing the announcement and you're wondering whether to buy, how to use them with your phone, or why they won't work with your laptop, you're in the right place. Short answer up front: they're a stylish, low-latency wired option for USB-C phones and handhelds, but they are **not** a replacement for wireless earbuds, and there are three compatibility gotchas you need to check before checkout.

## What You're Actually Buying: Specs and Compatibility Breakdown

The revival keeps the retro shell — including the signature **orange ear cushions** — but swaps the old 3.5mm analog cable for a USB-C digital connection with an onboard DAC. Here's how it stacks up against the obvious alternatives:

**Key parameters to verify before buying:**
- **DAC sample rate:** Confirmed up to 24-bit/96kHz over USB-C on supported Android and Windows devices.
- **Cable:** Detachable USB-C to USB-C, roughly 1.2m.
- **Weight:** ~180g (lighter than most over-ears due to the compact folding design).
- **Inline controls:** Play/pause, volume, and a mic for calls — but note that **inline volume control does not work on iPhones**.

### Step-by-Step: Setting Them Up on Android, iPhone, Windows, and Switch

1. **Android (best experience):** Plug in via USB-C. A system prompt asks whether to route audio to the USB device — tap **Allow**. Then open **Settings > Sound > Audio output** and select the Walkman headset. For hi-res, enable **Developer Options > USB audio** if your phone exposes it.
2. **iPhone 15/16 (USB-C):** Plug in and they work immediately for audio and calls. However, the inline buttons are limited to play/pause and mic — **volume must be adjusted on the phone**. No hi-res mode.
3. **Windows 11:** Plug in, then go to **Settings > System > Sound > Output** and pick the USB headset. If it doesn't appear, open **Device Manager**, find **Audio inputs and outputs**, right-click the device, and select **Update driver**. Windows treats it as a generic USB Audio Class 2 device — no Sony app required.
4. **Nintendo Switch / Steam Deck:** Plug into the USB-C port directly. On Switch, audio routes automatically in handheld mode; on the dock, use the front USB-C if available. On Steam Deck, go to **Quick Access Menu > Audio > Output** and select the USB device.
5. **Older gear with 3.5mm only:** You'll need a **USB-C to 3.5mm adapter with a built-in DAC** (e.g., Apple's or a Ugreen model). The headphones cannot output analog audio themselves.

## Common Issues, Workarounds, and What You Should Know

**"No sound when I plug them in."** This is the #1 complaint. It's almost always one of three things:
- Your phone's USB-C port is **charge-only** (common on budget phones) — check the spec sheet for "USB audio" support.
- The USB-C port is **wet or dirty** — clean it and retry; the headphones draw power from the host.
- On Windows, the device defaulted to the wrong output — reselect it in the Sound panel.

**"The volume buttons do nothing."** Expected on iPhone. Inline volume is handled by the host device on iOS; Android and Windows support the full control set.

**"They drain my phone battery."** They're bus-powered, drawing roughly 40–60mA — modest, but noticeable on long sessions. There's no internal battery to charge, which is actually a durability plus.

**Alternatives worth weighing:**
- If you want **wireless convenience**, the WH-CH720N or AirPods 4 are better daily drivers.
- If you want **retro style plus zero latency** for gaming or music production, the revival is genuinely compelling at ~$99–$129.
- If you mostly use **3.5mm gear**, skip these — the adapter tax and control limitations aren't worth it.

**Cost analysis:** At ~$110 street price, you're paying roughly a $30–$40 "nostalgia premium" over comparable wired USB-C headphones. The orange cushions and folding design are the differentiator; the audio hardware is solid but not audiophile-grade.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Are the revived Sony Walkman headphones wireless or wired?
They are **wired USB-C headphones** with a built-in DAC. There is no Bluetooth radio and no battery — they draw power from the connected device. The USB-C port is for audio, not charging.

### Do the Sony Walkman USB-C headphones work with iPhone?
Yes, on **iPhone 15 and later** (USB-C models), for audio and calls. Inline volume buttons do not function on iOS, and hi-res playback is not supported. Older Lightning iPhones require a Lightning-to-USB-C adapter and offer limited functionality.

### Can I use them on a plane or with a 3.5mm seat jack?
Not directly. You'll need a **USB-C to 3.5mm adapter with a DAC**, or a USB-C power bank if the seat only provides analog. This is the biggest practical drawback for frequent flyers.

### Do the orange ear cushions come included or are they sold separately?
The **orange ear cushions are included** as the default finish on the revival model, matching the original Walkman aesthetic. Replacement cushions are expected to be sold separately later.

## Final Verdict

Buy the Sony Walkman revival **if** you have a USB-C phone or handheld, you value near-zero latency and retro styling, and you don't mind a cable. At ~$99–$129, the orange-cushion design and bus-powered simplicity make them a fun, durable secondary pair — especially for Switch or Steam Deck sessions. **Skip them** if you need wireless freedom, fly often with 3.5mm seat jacks, or expect inline volume on iPhone. For everyone else, the nostalgia is earned, not just marketed — but check your device's USB-C audio support before you click buy.
