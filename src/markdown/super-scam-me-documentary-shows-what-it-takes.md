## How to Catch a Romance Scammer: The Super Scam Me Playbook

The *Super Scam Me* documentary makes one thing clear: catching a romance scammer isn't about dramatic confrontations — it's about **methodical evidence gathering**. If you or someone you know is dealing with a suspected romance scammer, this guide breaks down the exact investigative workflow shown in the film (and used by real fraud investigators) so you can document, verify, and report safely. The direct takeaway: **collect evidence first, never confront, and report through official channels** — the victim in *Super Scam Me* only got results because she built a paper trail before acting.

## Step-by-Step: How to Investigate and Report a Romance Scammer

Follow these steps in order. Each one mirrors what the *Super Scam Me* victim did when she tracked down her scammer.

### Step 1: Preserve the Evidence Before Anything Else
Scammers delete or block accounts the moment they suspect exposure. Screenshot everything **now**:

- Full chat history (including timestamps and usernames)
- Profile photos, bio text, and follower lists
- Payment requests, wallet addresses, or bank details shared
- Call logs and voicemail recordings
- Emails with full headers (in Gmail: **More → Show original**)

Store copies in a folder synced to cloud storage so nothing is lost if your device is compromised.

### Step 2: Run a Reverse Image Search
This is the single highest-yield step. Romance scammers almost always steal photos.

| Tool | Best For | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Google Lens | General reverse image search | Free |
| TinEye | Finding the *oldest* instance of an image | Free tier |
| PimEyes | Facial recognition across the web | Paid |
| Yandex Images | Strong on Eastern European sources | Free |

Upload the suspect's profile photo to Google Lens and TinEye. If the same face appears under a different name on LinkedIn, a modeling site, or a military forum — you have your proof.

### Step 3: Verify the Identity Claims
Cross-check every claim against public records:

1. **Military claims** — request the name, rank, and unit; verify via official channels. Scammers frequently pose as deployed soldiers to explain why they can't video call.
2. **Job claims** — search the person on LinkedIn and company directories.
3. **Video call refusal** — a legitimate partner will video call. Repeated excuses ("camera broken," "base Wi-Fi blocked") are a red flag.
4. **Domain check** — if they email from a company address, run it through **MXToolbox** or **whois** to see when the domain was registered. Domains registered weeks ago are fake.

### Step 4: Trace the Money Request Pattern
Document every request. Common patterns include:

- Emergency medical bills
- Customs fees for a "gift" or "inheritance"
- Crypto investment "opportunities" (pig-butchering scams)
- Gift cards as payment (a near-certain scam indicator)

Log amounts, dates, and payment methods in a spreadsheet — this becomes your evidence timeline.

### Step 5: Report Through the Right Channels
| Channel | Use It For |
|---|---|
| Platform report tools (Meta, Tinder, WhatsApp) | Getting the account removed |
| FBI IC3 (ic3.gov) | US financial fraud and romance scams |
| FTC ReportFraud.ftc.gov | Consumer fraud complaints |
| Action Fraud (UK) / Scamwatch (AU) | Regional reporting |
| Local police | Only if money was lost or threats made |

Never send money to "recover" lost funds — recovery scams target victims twice.

## Common Issues and What You Should Know

**Don't confront the scammer directly.** The *Super Scam Me* narrative shows confrontation can escalate to blackmail, especially if intimate images were shared. Report and block instead.

**Beware of "recovery expert" scams.** After a scam, victims are often contacted by fake recovery agents. Legitimate agencies never demand upfront crypto payments.

**Platform reports often fail silently.** If a report is rejected, escalate by reporting the account for a different violation (impersonation often works when romance fraud doesn't) and submit evidence to IC3 regardless.

**Evidence retention matters.** Keep your evidence folder for at least 12 months — investigations and chargebacks can take months to process.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### How do I know if someone is a romance scammer?
The strongest signals are: refusal to video call, requests for money or gift cards, love bombing within days, stolen profile photos found via reverse image search, and inconsistent details about their life. Two or more of these together is a near-certain scam.

### Can you actually catch a romance scammer like in Super Scam Me?
Full identification is rare because scammers often operate from abroad through stolen identities. What victims *can* achieve — as the documentary shows — is documenting the fraud, getting accounts removed, and helping authorities build cases through IC3 reports.

### Should I confront a romance scammer?
No. Confrontation tips them off, lets them delete evidence, and can trigger blackmail. Preserve screenshots, report to the platform and IC3, then block.

## Final Verdict

If you suspect a romance scam, act like the *Super Scam Me* investigator: **screenshot everything, reverse-search the photos, verify claims, log the money trail, and report to IC3 and the platform — in that order.** Don't pay for "recovery services," and don't confront the scammer. Evidence, not emotion, is what catches these criminals.
