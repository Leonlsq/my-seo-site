## Shohei Ohtani Is Back and the NLCS Just Got Scary

The baseball world held its breath when Shohei Ohtani exited Game 2 of the National League Championship Series with what looked like a nagging lower-body issue. For 48 hours, Dodgers fans doom-scrolled every injury report, every beat writer's tweet, and every cryptic press conference quote. Then came the news that flipped the entire series on its head: manager Dave Roberts told reporters that Shohei Ohtani will be in the Dodgers' Game 3 NLCS lineup. No limitations. No bench role. The two-way superstar is back in the heart of the order.

This is not just a lineup card update. It is a psychological reset for a Dodgers team that suddenly looked mortal, and a nightmare scenario for an opposing pitching staff that had just started to believe it could neutralize the most dangerous hitter on the planet. When Ohtani is locked in, the entire geometry of a game changes. Pitchers nibble. Defenses shift. Crowds rise to their feet the moment he steps into the box.

Roberts' announcement also quiets the loudest storyline of the series: whether LA could survive a deep October run without its $700 million centerpiece. The answer, at least for Game 3, is that they will not have to find out.

## What Dave Roberts Actually Said and Why It Matters

Roberts addressed the media with his usual calm, but the substance of his comments carried enormous weight. Here is the breakdown of what his confirmation means in practical terms.

- **Lineup protection:** With Ohtani back in the two-hole or three-hole, Mookie Betts and Freddie Freeman see better pitches. Pitchers cannot pitch around the entire middle of the order.
- **Pitch-count pressure:** Ohtani's presence forces starters to work deeper counts, which accelerates bullpen usage for the opponent and plays directly into LA's late-inning advantage.
- **Defensive positioning chaos:** Opposing managers must now decide whether to shift, pitch around, or challenge him, and every option carries risk.
- **Clubhouse energy:** Roberts noted the mood in the room shifted instantly. Veterans described it as a weight lifted.
- **Series momentum:** Game 3 is the swing game. Going up 2-1 versus falling behind 1-2 is the difference between controlling a series and chasing it.

### The Injury Context Nobody Should Ignore

Ohtani's exit in Game 2 was precautionary in nature, but that does not mean he is at one hundred percent. Roberts has been careful with his language all postseason, and the fact that he confirmed the lineup spot rather than saying he is fully healthy suggests a managed workload. Expect to see Ohtani in the DH slot rather than the field, which preserves his bat while reducing physical stress. This is the smart play for a team that needs his offense far more than his glove right now.
