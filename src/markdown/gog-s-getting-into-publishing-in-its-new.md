## GOG Is Now a Publisher: What Actually Changes for Your Library?

GOG announced it's moving into game publishing as an independent company following its separation from CD Projekt, and it already has titles in the pipeline. If you're a GOG user, the practical question isn't "what's the news" — it's **what happens to your existing library, your DRM-free guarantees, and whether these new published games are worth buying on GOG versus Steam**. Here's the direct answer: your existing purchases are unaffected, GOG's DRM-free policy remains its core selling point, and the new published titles will launch DRM-free on GOG first — but you should verify offline installers and regional pricing before committing, because publisher-backed releases sometimes ship with heavier launcher dependencies than classic GOG titles.

This guide breaks down what the publishing pivot means for you in concrete terms: library safety, DRM implications, how to prepare your account, and how to evaluate the first wave of GOG-published games against Steam alternatives.

## What the GOG Publishing Shift Means for You: A Practical Breakdown

GOG's transition from storefront to publisher is a structural change, not a change to your account terms. Here's how to think about it and what to do:

1. **Confirm your library is safe.** GOG account purchases are tied to your GOG account, not to CD Projekt corporate structure. The separation doesn't touch your entitlements. Log in at gog.com, go to your library, and verify your games still appear. Download offline installers for your most important titles as a backup — this is the single best hedge against any future store changes.
2. **Re-check DRM status on new releases.** GOG's brand promise is DRM-free. When GOG publishes a game, confirm on the store page that it lists "DRM-Free" and offers offline installers. If a new published title only offers a Galaxy client download, that's a red flag worth noting before purchase.
3. **Enable GOG Galaxy 2.0 integrations.** If you use Galaxy, connect your Steam and Epic accounts so your full library is visible in one place. This matters more now: as GOG publishes its own titles, you'll want a single dashboard to compare what you own across stores.
4. **Set up price alerts.** Use GOG's wishlist and price-tracking tools (or third-party trackers like IsThereAnyDeal) to monitor the first GOG-published titles. Publisher-backed launches often get launch discounts, and GOG frequently runs sales shortly after release.
5. **Check regional availability and currency.** GOG pricing varies by region. Before buying a new GOG-published title, verify the price in your store region and confirm whether a GOG Wallet balance or regional payment method applies.

### GOG vs. Steam for New Published Titles: Quick Comparison

| Factor | GOG (published titles) | Steam |
|---|---|---|
| DRM | DRM-free, offline installers | Steam DRM (varies by game) |
| Offline play | Yes, after installer download | Requires Steam client (offline mode limited) |
| Refund window | 30 days (GOG policy) | 2 hours playtime / 14 days |
| Library portability | High (installers are yours) | Tied to Steam account |
| Community features | Galaxy 2.0, basic | Extensive (workshop, reviews, forums) |
| Regional pricing | Varies, often competitive | Varies |

The refund window is a genuine GOG advantage: 30 days with no playtime cap is far more forgiving than Steam's 2-hour/14-day rule, which matters if you're unsure about a new publisher's first titles.

## Common Issues, Alternatives, and What to Watch For

- **Launcher dependency creep.** Some newer GOG releases still require the Galaxy client for updates or cloud saves. If you want pure offline installers, check the "offline backup" section on each game's page before buying.
- **Missing features versus Steam.** GOG-published titles may lack Steam Workshop mod support or Steam Deck verification at launch. Check ProtonDB and GOG's own compatibility notes if you play on a Steam Deck.
- **Regional pricing gaps.** Compare the GOG price against Steam and Epic in your region — GOG isn't always cheaper, even for its own published games.
- **Refund edge cases.** GOG's 30-day policy is generous, but digital goods rules can vary; read the refund terms on the specific product page.
- **Alternatives if you want DRM-free elsewhere.** itch.io and Humble Store (DRM-free options) remain viable alternatives, though GOG's curated catalog and installer model are still the most convenient.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Is my existing GOG library affected by the CD Projekt split?
No. Your purchases are tied to your GOG account and remain accessible. Download offline installers for key titles as a precaution, but the corporate separation does not change your entitlements.

### Are GOG-published games DRM-free?
GOG's stated policy is that all games it sells, including its own published titles, are DRM-free with offline installers. Always verify the "DRM-Free" label and offline backup availability on the specific store page before buying.

### Should I buy GOG-published games on GOG or Steam?
Choose GOG if you value DRM-free ownership, offline installers, and the 30-day refund window. Choose Steam if you need Workshop mods, Steam Deck verification, or extensive community features. For most players, GOG is the better ownership model; Steam is the better ecosystem.

## Final Verdict

GOG's move into publishing is good news for players who care about DRM-free ownership — but don't buy on brand loyalty alone. **Verify the DRM-Free label and offline installer availability on each new title, compare regional pricing against Steam, and back up your existing library with offline installers now.** If GOG's first published games deliver DRM-free releases with its 30-day refund policy intact, they're worth buying on GOG over Steam for the ownership model alone. If a title ships with launcher dependencies or missing features, Steam remains the safer ecosystem bet.
