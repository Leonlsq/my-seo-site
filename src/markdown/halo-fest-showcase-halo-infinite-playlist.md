## Halo Fest Showcase Playlist: What You're Actually Loading Into

The Halo Fest Showcase playlist is now live in Halo Infinite matchmaking, and it's not just a victory lap for the Forge community — it's a genuinely useful testing ground for players who want to sharpen their Arena fundamentals on fresh geometry. The playlist bundles all **10 finalist maps** from the Halo Fest Forge Contest across a rotating set of Arena modes, meaning you'll cycle through Slayer, Oddball, Capture the Flag, Strongholds, and King of the Hill variants depending on the map. If you've been grinding the same ranked rotation for months, this is your chance to break stale habits and learn sightlines that no YouTube guide has mapped yet.

Below is how to get the most out of it, which settings to tune before you queue, and what to expect from each mode.

## Step-by-Step: Optimizing Your Setup for Unfamiliar Forge Maps

Forge maps often differ from official maps in lighting, geometry density, and verticality. Before you queue, run this checklist:

1. **Bump your field of view (FOV) to 95–105.** Forge finalists tend to favor tighter corridors and multi-level arenas. A wider FOV helps you catch flankers on unfamiliar vertical routes. Anything above 110 introduces fisheye distortion that hurts tracking at range.
2. **Lower motion blur to 0%.** Community Forge maps sometimes use custom lighting and skyboxes that amplify motion blur artifacts. Disabling it keeps target acquisition clean during strafes.
3. **Set your deadzones deliberately.** If you play controller, drop the move thumbstick deadzone to 0–2 and the look deadzone to 0–3. Forge geometry has more ledges and ramps than standard maps, and tight deadzones give you finer control on uneven surfaces.
4. **Enable "Maintain Sprint" if you use sprint-heavy routes.** Many finalist maps are built with sprint-jump chains in mind. Holding sprint manually on unknown geometry causes missed jumps.
5. **Play 2–3 matches in Bot Bootcamp first.** Not for aim practice — for **map reading**. Load the playlist maps in a custom game with bots to learn spawn logic and power weapon locations before you expose yourself to real opponents.
6. **Check your ping region.** Forge maps with heavy custom geometry can stutter on high-ping connections. If you see rubber-banding, back out and requeue rather than blaming the map.

## What to Expect: Modes, Map Variety, and Information Gain

The playlist rotates Arena modes, so your performance will vary by mode far more than by map. A few practical notes:

- **Slayer** on Forge finalists rewards map control over aggression. Because power positions aren't community-documented yet, the first team to lock down high ground usually wins. Play slow for the first 60 seconds.
- **Oddball and Strongholds** expose spawn-trapping weaknesses fast. Forge authors rarely balance spawns as tightly as 343's official maps, so expect occasional unfair spawns — don't tilt, just note the map and adapt.
- **CTF** is where unfamiliar geometry hurts most. Learn the two fastest flag routes per map during your bot warm-up; that single prep step wins more games than raw aim.

**Hidden value most players miss:** this playlist is a free Forge showcase. If a map impresses you, open its file in Forge mode afterward and study the geometry — the finalists are a masterclass in efficient blockout design, and you can remix layouts for your own custom games.

**What you should know before investing time:** the playlist is a showcase, not a permanent ranked tier. Rewards and progression are tied to standard matchmaking, so don't expect exclusive cosmetics. Treat it as practice with a novelty factor.
