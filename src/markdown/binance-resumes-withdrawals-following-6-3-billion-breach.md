## Binance Withdrawals Are Live Again — Here's What You Should Do First

Binance has resumed withdrawals after a $6.3 billion breach, and the exchange says it has now covered 95% of the stolen assets. If you're reading this because you're staring at a pending withdrawal, a frozen balance, or you just want your coins off the exchange — this guide is for you. The short version: withdrawals are processing again, but you should **not** blindly hit "withdraw all" without verifying your account status, whitelist settings, and network choice first. Follow the steps below to move funds without triggering a security hold or losing money to a wrong-network transfer.

## Step-by-Step: Verify Your Account and Withdraw Safely

Before you touch the withdraw button, work through this checklist in order. Skipping steps is how people end up locked out for 24–72 hours during high-traffic recovery windows.

### Step 1 — Confirm withdrawal service status per asset
Not every coin unlocks at the same time. Check the official Binance status page and the withdrawal page itself, which shows a per-asset status tag.

- **Green "Withdrawal Available"** → proceed.
- **"Maintenance" or "Suspended"** → wait; do not open duplicate support tickets.
- **"Delayed"** → the request queues but may sit for hours. Don't cancel and resubmit repeatedly, as this can flag your account.

### Step 2 — Re-verify KYC and 2FA
Post-incident, exchanges typically re-trigger identity checks. Confirm:

1. Your **identity verification (KYC)** shows "Verified" — not "Pending review."
2. **2FA** is active on an authenticator app (not SMS, which is SIM-swap vulnerable).
3. **Anti-phishing code** is set so you can spot fake emails.

### Step 3 — Check your withdrawal whitelist
If you enabled address whitelisting, new addresses need a **24-hour cooldown** before they become usable. Plan for this: add your destination wallet address *now*, then wait out the timer while you finish the other steps.

### Step 4 — Pick the right network (the #1 costly mistake)
Sending USDT or a token on the wrong chain is the most common way users lose funds during chaotic withdrawal windows.

| Asset | Recommended Network | Typical Fee | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| USDT | TRC-20 or BEP-20 | ~$1 or less | Confirm destination supports the chain |
| BTC | Bitcoin (native) | Variable | Avoid BEP-20 wrapped BTC unless intended |
| ETH | ERC-20 | Higher gas | Check destination is an Ethereum address |
| USDC | ERC-20 / Solana | Low on Solana | Match exactly to your wallet's chain |

**Rule:** the sending network and the receiving wallet's network must match. A TRC-20 address is not an ERC-20 address.

### Step 5 — Test with a small amount first
Send a **minimum test transaction** (e.g., $5–$10 equivalent), confirm it lands, then send the rest. This single habit prevents 90% of "funds lost to wrong address" disasters.

### Step 6 — Withdraw to self-custody, not another exchange
The whole point of moving off Binance after a breach is to hold your own keys. Use a hardware wallet (Ledger, Trezor) or a reputable self-custody wallet. Sending to another exchange just moves your counterparty risk.

## Common Issues, Alternatives, and What You Should Know

**"My withdrawal is stuck in 'Processing' for hours."** This is normal during recovery windows. Large batches and manual risk reviews slow things down. Give it 24 hours before escalating.

**"My account got restricted after the breach."** Exchanges often freeze accounts flagged by automated risk systems. Submit one clear support ticket with your user ID and transaction hash — don't spam.

**"Should I trust the 95% coverage claim?"** Coverage of stolen assets is a solvency signal, not a guarantee. Treat it as reassurance to *act calmly*, not a reason to leave everything on the exchange. Diversify: split holdings across self-custody and, if you must use exchanges, more than one.

**Cost reality check:** network fees spike during high-volume withdrawal windows. Withdrawing a large batch in one transaction is cheaper than many small ones — but always do the test send first.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Are Binance withdrawals working right now?
Yes — Binance has resumed withdrawals following the $6.3 billion breach, with the exchange stating it has covered 95% of stolen assets. Individual asset availability can still vary, so check the per-asset status on the withdrawal page before initiating a transfer.

### How long do Binance withdrawals take after the breach?
Most withdrawals process within minutes to a few hours, but expect delays of up to 24 hours during the recovery window due to manual risk reviews and higher volume. New whitelisted addresses add a 24-hour cooldown.

### Is it safe to keep funds on Binance after the breach?
No exchange is risk-free. The safest approach is to move the majority of holdings to self-custody (hardware wallet) and keep only active trading funds on any exchange.

## Final Verdict

Withdrawals are back, but the breach is your wake-up call. **Verify your KYC and 2FA, whitelist your destination address early, test with a small transfer, and move the bulk of your holdings to self-custody.** Don't leave your entire portfolio on any single exchange — the 95% coverage figure is reassuring, but the only assets truly safe from an exchange breach are the ones you control the keys to.
