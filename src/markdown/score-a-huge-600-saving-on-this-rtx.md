## Step-by-Step: How to Configure This Laptop Without Overpaying

Follow this order at checkout to maximize value:

1. **Keep the base 16GB RAM if you're comfortable with DIY.** A 32GB DDR5 SO-DIMM kit costs roughly $70–$100 on the open market versus HP's markup. The Omen Max 16 has accessible SO-DIMM slots.
2. **Do NOT upgrade to HP's 1TB/2TB SSD tier.** Buy a second M.2 2280 Gen4 drive (1TB ≈ $60–$80) and add it yourself—the chassis supports dual M.2 slots.
3. **Confirm the display refresh rate** before ordering. Verify you're getting the high-refresh QHD panel, not a 165Hz variant if a faster option exists.
4. **Check the GPU TGP in the spec sheet.** The 5070 Ti Laptop runs best at 130–140W. A lower-wattage variant in a thin chassis loses 10–15% performance.
5. **Add the extended warranty only if you travel**—HP's accidental damage coverage is worth it for a $2,100 machine, otherwise skip it.
6. **Price-match before checkout.** HP frequently runs stacked coupon codes; a chat agent can often apply an additional 5%.

## Real-World Performance & Settings to Hit 100+ FPS

Once you own it, these settings extract maximum frames at 1600p:

- **Enable DLSS 4 with Frame Generation** in supported titles—expect 40–70% frame gains.
- **Set the Omen Gaming Hub to "Performance" mode** and enable "Discrete GPU only" (MUX switch) to bypass Optimus and cut latency.
- **Cap the refresh rate to your panel's native max**, not higher, to avoid tearing without V-Sync.
- **Undervolt via Intel XTU** by -50 to -80mV for lower temps and sustained clocks.

Expected rough performance (1600p, high settings, DLSS Quality): esports titles 200+ FPS, AAA titles 90–130 FPS.
