### Step-by-Step: How to Evaluate Whether to Join

1. **Get the real local price in writing.** "Affordable" is marketing until you see the rate card for your zip code. Ask for the month-to-month rate *and* the annual-commitment rate — the gap is often 20–30%.
2. **Audit the recovery amenities, not the marketing.** Confirm the number of cold plunges and saunas versus peak-hour member count. Two plunges for 3,000 members is a bottleneck, not a perk.
3. **Ask what the blood panel actually covers.** A basic lipid + glucose panel costs $30–$60 out-of-pocket via LabCorp or Quest. If the included panel is that basic and only annual, value it at roughly $3–$5/month — don't let it sway you more than that.
4. **Check the cancellation policy before you sign anything.** Wahlberg's prior gym venture (F45) famously ran into trouble and was delisted from the NYSE — a reminder to read the fine print on contracts and auto-renewals.
5. **Do a trial week at peak hours.** Visit 6–8 PM on a weekday. If you can't get on a plunge or a rack, the price is irrelevant.
6. **Stack the muffin math.** If a shake/muffin bar item runs $5–$8 and you'd buy 12 a month, that's $60–$96 in real value — potentially offsetting a big chunk of dues.

## Common Issues, Hidden Costs & What You Should Know

- **"Affordable" is market-dependent.** Franchise-style rollouts often price coastal cities 40–60% higher than the national average. Budget for sticker shock if you're in LA, NYC, or Miami.
- **Blood panels raise privacy questions.** Ask who stores your lab results, whether they're shared with partners, and how to opt out of marketing based on health data. This is legitimate Information Gain most coverage skips.
- **Recovery tech has a real learning curve.** Cold plunges aren't a "walk in for 30 seconds" gimmick — protocol matters. Start at 50–59°F for 1–3 minutes, 2–3x weekly, and never immediately after heavy strength training if hypertrophy is your goal (cold exposure can blunt the inflammatory response you want post-lift).
- **Compare against à la carte.** A standalone cold plunge studio runs $30–$50/session; a sauna membership $100–$200/month. If you'd use both 8+ times monthly, the bundled model wins on paper.
- **Watch the F45 precedent.** Wahlberg's previous fitness investment had a rocky public-market history. That doesn't predict this venture's fate, but it's a fair reason to favor month-to-month over multi-year contracts until the concept proves stable.
