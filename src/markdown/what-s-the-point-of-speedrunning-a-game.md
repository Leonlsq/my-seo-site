## Why Speedrunners Skip Half the Game: The Real Answer
If you have ever watched a speedrun and thought, 'why would anyone race a game just to skip 50% of it?' — you are not alone. The confusion usually comes from a simple misunderstanding: **speedrunning is not about completing a game the way a developer intended. It is about optimizing a ruleset to its theoretical limit.** When a runner skips half the content, they are not cheating the game — they are playing a *different* game defined by a category. This guide breaks down the category system, explains why skips exist, and shows you exactly how to pick a category and start running in 2026 without wasting months on the wrong route.

## The Category System: Why 'Skipping' Is the Whole Point

Speedrun leaderboards (Speedrun.com, leaderboards on Discord, and marathon events like AGDQ and SGDQ) are organized by **categories**. Each category is a contract: what counts as a valid run, what is banned, and what the timer starts and stops on.

| Category | Goal | Typical Skip Rate | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Any% | Reach credits by any means | 40–70% of content | *Super Mario 64* 16-star |
| Any% No Major Glitches (NMG) | Credits without game-breaking glitches | 20–40% | *Ocarina of Time* NMG |
| Low% | Finish with minimum items | 50–80% | *Hollow Knight* 11% |
| 100% | All required collectibles | 0% (by definition) | *Celeste* 202 berries |
| Glitchless | No exploits allowed | 10–25% | *Elden Ring* Glitchless |

So when someone asks 'why skip 50% of the game,' the honest answer is: **they entered a category where that content is optional.** Any% is the most popular category because it is the fastest to learn, the most competitive, and the most streamable. It is not a lesser version of the game — it is a *speed* version.

### Why Developers Put Optional Content In

Modern games are built with redundancy. Bosses have multiple damage windows, areas have multiple entrances, and items have multiple sources. Speedrunners find the **single shortest dependency chain** through that graph. Skipping is not laziness — it is graph theory.

## How to Start Speedrunning Without Skipping the Wrong Things

If you want to run a game but feel weird about 'missing content,' follow this order:

1. **Pick a game you already love.** You will replay it 200+ times. If you do not enjoy the base game, the grind will kill you.
2. **Choose a category that matches your values.** Want to see everything? Run 100%. Want pure execution? Run Any% NMG. Want chaos? Run Any%.
3. **Download the right tools.** Livesplit (timer), a capture card or OBS for verification, and the community route doc (usually pinned in the game's Discord).
4. **Learn splits, not the whole game.** Memorize the first 5 splits, then add 5 more. Do not try to learn a 90-minute route in one sitting.
5. **Watch the current world record with splits visible.** Pause and note item pickups and route decisions.
6. **Submit to Speedrun.com only after a full run with video proof.** Most boards require video for top placements.

### The 'Skip' Is Usually the Skill Check

Here is the counterintuitive part: the skips are often *harder* than the content they replace. A wrong-angle clip in *Zelda: Ocarina of Time* or a pixel-perfect wall clip in *Super Mario 64* takes hundreds of attempts. Runners are not avoiding difficulty — they are trading **content difficulty for execution difficulty.**

## Common Misconceptions and What You Should Know

- **'Any% is the real run.'** False. Every category is equally legitimate. 100% runs are frequently more impressive to watch.
- **'Skipping means the game is broken.'** Not necessarily. Some skips use intended mechanics (movement tech, damage boosts) that developers left in deliberately.
- **'Runners cheat.'** Verification is strict. Runs need video, splits, and often input display. Cheaters like the infamous *Minecraft* and *Trackmania* cases get banned permanently.
- **'It is all glitches.'** Glitchless categories exist for almost every major game and are often the most technically demanding.

### Cost and Time Reality Check

- **Time:** Expect 20–100 hours to get your first competitive run in a mid-length game.
- **Money:** $0 to start. A $60–$150 capture card only matters if you plan to submit verified runs.
- **Community:** Free. Discord route docs are the single best resource — better than any YouTube tutorial.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Why do speedrunners skip so much of the game?
Because they compete in categories like Any%, where only reaching the credits matters. Optional bosses, cutscenes, and collectibles are not required, so skipping them is the optimal strategy, not a shortcut around the rules.

### Is Any% considered cheating?
No. Any% is a defined category with strict rules. Runners must still reach the credits legitimately within the game's code. Using external tools, save editing, or TAS inputs in a real-time run is cheating and gets you banned from leaderboards.

### What is the difference between Any% and 100%?
Any% finishes the game as fast as possible by any allowed means. 100% requires completing all specified objectives, such as every boss, item, or collectible, making it far longer but more complete.

### Can I speedrun a game without learning glitches?
Yes. Look for the 'Glitchless' or 'No Major Glitches' category on Speedrun.com. These routes rely on pure execution and are ideal for runners who want to play the game close to its intended design.

## Final Verdict
Skipping half a game is not missing the point of speedrunning — **it is the point.** Categories define what 'finished' means, and Any% simply defines it as 'reach the credits.' If that bothers you, run 100% or Glitchless and you will skip almost nothing. If you want the most competitive, most-watched, and fastest-to-learn experience, start with Any% and accept that the skips are the skill. Pick your category, grab a timer, and join the Discord — the route doc is waiting.
