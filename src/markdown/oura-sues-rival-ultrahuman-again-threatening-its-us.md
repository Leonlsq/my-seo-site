## Oura vs Ultrahuman Lawsuit: What It Means for Ring Pro Buyers

If you were waiting for the Ultrahuman Ring Pro to officially land in the US, Oura just threw a wrench in those plans. In its newest patent infringement lawsuit, Oura alleges the Ultrahuman Ring Pro infringes two of its patents — a follow-up to the earlier ITC action that already threatened Ultrahuman's US comeback. The practical takeaway for buyers: **the hardware is real, the US availability is uncertain, and you should buy with your eyes open.** This guide breaks down exactly what the lawsuit covers, what it means for your warranty and import options, and how the Ring Pro stacks up against the Oura Ring 4 so you can decide whether to buy now, wait, or pick a different ring entirely.

## What the Lawsuit Actually Alleges (and What It Doesn't)

Oura's complaint centers on **two patents** related to smart ring sensor architecture and form-factor design. It is a civil patent suit — not a product recall, not a safety issue, and not a ban on the Ring Pro everywhere. Here's the breakdown:

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Plaintiff | Ōura Health Oy (Finland) |
| Defendant | Ultrahuman Healthcare (India) |
| Core claim | Ring Pro infringes 2 Oura patents |
| Prior action | ITC complaint (import/sales exclusion risk) |
| Product affected | Ultrahuman Ring Pro |
| Geographic scope | Primarily US market |
| Status | Filed — no ruling yet |

**What this means in plain English:**

- The Ring Pro is **not** being pulled from shelves in the EU, UK, or India.
- A US launch could be **delayed or blocked** if the ITC or courts side with Oura.
- Existing owners are **not** affected by warranty or functionality — the suit targets sales/import, not users.
- No recall, no firmware lockout, no bricking risk.

## Should You Buy the Ring Pro Now? A Decision Checklist

Use this quick decision tree before you spend $349+ on a smart ring in late 2026:

1. **Are you in the US?** If yes, expect limited official availability and possible import friction. Buy from a retailer with a clear return policy.
2. **Do you need US-based support/warranty?** If yes, the Oura Ring 4 is the safer bet — Oura has a mature US service network.
3. **Are you outside the US?** The lawsuit has minimal impact on you. The Ring Pro remains a strong Oura alternative.
4. **Do you care about subscription fees?** Ultrahuman's no-subscription model is a genuine differentiator (Oura charges ~$5.99/month).
5. **Are you risk-averse about future app support?** A legal loss could affect US app features or updates. Weigh that against the Ring Pro's lower total cost.

## Common Issues and What You Should Know

- **Importing the Ring Pro to the US:** You can technically order from international retailers, but you lose easy warranty service and may face customs delays. Document your purchase carefully.
- **App feature risk:** If a US sales ban lands, Ultrahuman's US app experience could lag. Non-US users are largely unaffected.
- **Firmware updates:** These are delivered via the app and are not tied to the lawsuit. Your ring won't stop working.
- **Resale value:** A pending lawsuit can depress resale prices on US-market Ring Pros. Factor that in if you flip devices.
- **Alternatives if you're nervous:** Samsung Galaxy Ring and RingConn Gen 2 are viable no-subscription or low-risk options.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Is the Ultrahuman Ring Pro banned in the US?
Not yet. Oura has filed a lawsuit and previously pursued an ITC complaint, but no ban is currently in effect. Availability is limited and could change if a ruling goes against Ultrahuman.

### Will my Ultrahuman Ring Pro stop working because of the lawsuit?
No. The lawsuit targets sales and imports, not existing devices. Your ring and app will continue to function normally.

### Is the Oura Ring 4 worth it over the Ring Pro?
If US support, warranty certainty, and proven sleep tracking matter most, yes. If you want to avoid a monthly subscription and you're outside the US, the Ring Pro is the better value.

### Does the lawsuit affect Ultrahuman rings sold in Europe or India?
No. The dispute is primarily US-focused. European and Indian buyers face no direct impact on availability or support.

## Final Verdict

**US buyers:** Wait or choose the Oura Ring 4 unless you're comfortable with import risk and limited warranty coverage. The Ring Pro is a great device, but the legal cloud over its US future is real.

**Everyone else:** The Ultrahuman Ring Pro remains one of the best no-subscription smart rings you can buy — the lawsuit barely touches you. Buy it, enjoy the metabolic insights, and skip the monthly fee. Just keep an eye on US rulings if you plan to travel or resell.
