## How to Verify an RTX 5090 Serial Number Before You Buy (And Avoid Stolen Stock)

A stolen shipment of Nvidia RTX 5090 graphics cards worth roughly $470,000 vanished from a Brazil airport — and at least one of those cards has already appeared for sale online, identified by its serial number. Police have arrested three airport employees linked to the theft. If you're shopping for a flagship GPU right now, this is your warning: **the secondhand market is now contaminated with stolen inventory**, and buying one can leave you with a bricked card, zero warranty, and potential legal exposure. This guide gives you a concrete, step-by-step method to verify any RTX 5090's serial number before money changes hands.

## Step-by-Step: How to Check an RTX 5090 Serial Number

The serial number is your single best defense. Here's exactly how to run the check.

### Step 1 — Locate the serial number on the card
- **Retail box:** The S/N is printed on a white sticker on the side panel and on the anti-static bag seal.
- **The card itself:** Look for a sticker on the backplate (usually near the PCIe power connectors) or on the underside of the shroud. Never trust a box-only serial — scammers swap boxes.
- **Invoice/receipt:** A legitimate seller should have a matching purchase receipt showing the same S/N.

### Step 2 — Cross-check the serial against Nvidia's database
1. Go to Nvidia's official **warranty and RMA portal** (support.nvidia.com).
2. Enter the serial number into the warranty lookup tool.
3. Confirm the card shows **"In Warranty"** with a valid manufacture date. A serial that returns "not found," "void," or a mismatched region is a red flag.

### Step 3 — Verify the AIB partner's serial format
Third-party cards (ASUS, MSI, Gigabyte, Zotac, Palit) each use their own serial formats. Check the manufacturer's warranty page directly:

| Brand | Serial Prefix Example | Where to Verify |
|-------|----------------------|-----------------|
| ASUS | Starts with letter + 12 digits | ASUS RMA portal |
| MSI | 10–14 alphanumeric | MSI warranty check |
| Gigabyte | "SN" + 12 digits | Gigabyte eSupport |
| Zotac | 8–12 digits | Zotac warranty page |

### Step 4 — Request proof of purchase and payment method
Ask the seller for the original invoice. Legitimate sellers paid retail price and can show it. Stolen goods almost never come with a matching receipt, and sellers will push for cash or crypto to avoid a paper trail.

### Step 5 — Meet in person and test before paying
Boot the card in a system, run GPU-Z, and confirm the reported serial and BIOS match the sticker. If the seller refuses an in-person test, walk away.

## Red Flags That Signal Stolen or Fraudulent RTX 5090 Stock

- **Price far below MSRP** (a $2,000+ card listed at $1,200–$1,400 is a trap).
- **No original box or accessories** — stolen shipments are often moved loose.
- **Seller can't explain provenance** ("a friend gave it to me").
- **Serial returns "warranty void" or "not found"** on the manufacturer portal.
- **Pressure to pay via crypto, gift cards, or wire transfer.**
- **Listing photos with serial numbers blurred out** — legitimate sellers show them.

## What Happens If You Buy a Stolen RTX 5090?

Beyond the ethics, the practical fallout is severe:

1. **Warranty is void.** Nvidia and AIB partners flag stolen serials; RMA requests get rejected, leaving you with an expensive paperweight if the card fails.
2. **Potential confiscation.** Law enforcement can seize stolen property, and you'd have no legal claim to a refund from the thief.
3. **Driver/BIOS locks.** Manufacturers can blacklist serials in firmware validation, and some regions require proof of purchase for driver support.
4. **Resale is impossible.** Any buyer who checks the serial will find it flagged.

## How to Report a Suspected Stolen GPU

If a listing looks suspicious, don't confront the seller. Report it to:
- The **platform's fraud team** (eBay, Facebook Marketplace, Mercado Livre).
- **Local police** with the listing URL and serial number.
- **Nvidia's anti-fraud team** via their support portal.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### How do I check if an RTX 5090 is stolen by serial number?
Enter the card's serial number into Nvidia's official warranty/RMA portal and the AIB partner's warranty checker. A valid, in-warranty result with a matching receipt confirms legitimacy; "not found" or "void" results indicate a flagged or stolen unit.

### Can I get a warranty on a used RTX 5090?
Only if the serial is valid and the original purchase receipt is transferable under the manufacturer's policy. Nvidia FE cards generally require the original buyer; some AIB partners (like EVGA historically, and certain ASUS/ MSI programs) allow limited transfer — always verify before buying.

### Is buying a stolen GPU illegal?
Yes — receiving stolen property is a criminal offense in most jurisdictions, including Brazil, the US, and the EU. Even unknowing buyers can lose the card to police seizure with no compensation.

### Why are RTX 5090 GPUs being stolen?
The RTX 5090's high resale value (often 2–3x MSRP due to supply shortages) makes it a prime target for organized cargo theft. The Brazil airport case involved three employees, highlighting insider risk at logistics hubs.

### What should I do if I already bought a suspicious RTX 5090?
Stop using it, contact the seller in writing to demand a refund, report the transaction to the platform, and notify local police. Preserve all messages and payment records as evidence.

## Final Verdict

The Brazil airport heist is a reminder that the RTX 5090's scarcity has created a black market that preys on eager buyers. **Never buy a flagship GPU without verifying the serial number against Nvidia's and the manufacturer's official portals, and always demand a matching receipt.** If a deal looks too good to be true — a 5090 well under MSRP, no box, no receipt — it almost certainly is. Spend the extra 10 minutes on verification; it's the cheapest insurance you'll ever buy for a $2,000+ graphics card.
