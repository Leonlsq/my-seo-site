## Meta Muse AI Avatar Video Chat: How to Set It Up and Use It

Meta has flipped the switch on video calls with Muse, its customizable animated AI avatar, for US users. Instead of typing prompts and waiting for text, you can now place a live video call and talk to Muse face-to-face (well, avatar-to-face) in real time. The feature is rolling out inside the Meta AI experience on Messenger and Instagram, and it's the first public step toward the Tamagotchi-like wearable Meta has teased for bringing Muse into the physical world.

Here's the short version: if you're in the US, have the latest Messenger or Instagram app, and can see the Meta AI button, you can start a Muse video call in under a minute. If you can't see it yet, the fixes below will tell you exactly why and what to do. This guide covers setup, customization, troubleshooting, and the privacy trade-offs you should know before you call.

## Step-by-Step: Start a Video Chat With Your Muse Avatar

Before you begin, confirm your baseline requirements:

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Region | US account (feature is US-only at launch) |
| App | Latest Messenger or Instagram (iOS/Android) |
| Account | Logged into a personal Meta account with Meta AI enabled |
| Permissions | Camera + microphone access granted to the app |
| Age | 13+ per Meta AI terms (18+ recommended for video features) |

**1. Update your app.** Go to the App Store or Google Play and install the latest Messenger and Instagram updates. Meta AI video calling is gated behind recent app builds, so an outdated app is the #1 reason users don't see the option.

**2. Open Meta AI.** In Messenger, tap the Meta AI icon (the blue-purple circle) in your chat list or search bar. In Instagram, tap the Meta AI icon in your DMs inbox.

**3. Start a voice or video call.** Inside the Meta AI chat, tap the phone or video icon in the top corner. If you only see a text prompt box, the feature hasn't reached your account yet — see the troubleshooting section below.

**4. Grant camera and mic permissions.** On first call, your phone will ask for access. Tap "Allow." On iOS, double-check Settings > Messenger > Camera/Microphone if you accidentally tapped "Don't Allow."

**5. Customize your Muse avatar.** Before or during the call, open the avatar settings to pick appearance, voice style, and personality presets. Your choices persist across calls, so you only customize once.

**6. Talk naturally.** Muse responds to spoken conversation in real time with lip-synced animation. You can interrupt, ask follow-ups, and switch topics — it behaves like a voice assistant with a face rather than a scripted chatbot.

**7. End and review.** Calls end with the red hang-up button. Check your Meta AI activity history if you want to review or delete past sessions.

## Troubleshooting: Why You Can't See Muse Video Chat Yet

If the video icon is missing or calls fail, work through these fixes in order:

- **You're outside the US.** The rollout is US-only. VPNs are unreliable here because Meta ties eligibility to account region and payment/device signals, not just IP.
- **Your app is stale.** Force-close and update. On Android, also clear the app cache (Settings > Apps > Messenger > Storage > Clear Cache).
- **Meta AI isn't enabled on your account.** Open Settings > Meta AI in the app and confirm it's turned on. Some accounts have it disabled by default.
- **You're on a minor or restricted account.** Teen supervision settings can block AI calling features. A parent-managed account may need the setting toggled in Family Center.
- **Server-side rollout lag.** Meta staggers feature releases over days or weeks. If everything else checks out, wait 48–72 hours and retry — there's no legitimate "unlock" trick, and any site claiming one is a scam.
- **Call drops or avatar freezes.** Switch from cellular to Wi-Fi, close background apps, and disable battery saver, which can throttle camera and mic access on Android.

## What You Should Know Before You Call

**Privacy:** Video calls with Muse involve camera and microphone data processed by Meta's AI systems. Review your Meta AI activity controls and delete call history if you don't want it retained. Don't share sensitive information — treat Muse like any cloud-connected assistant.

**The wearable angle:** Meta has confirmed a Tamagotchi-like device designed to bring Muse into the physical world. Video chat is the software foundation for that hardware, which means the avatar you customize now will likely carry over to the wearable. Customizing early is low-risk and may pay off later.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Is Muse AI video chat free?
Yes. Meta is offering Muse video calls at no cost inside Messenger and Instagram for US users. Expect potential future premium tiers tied to the wearable, but the current rollout is free.

### Why can't I video chat with Meta AI even though I'm in the US?
The most common causes are an outdated app, Meta AI disabled in settings, or a staggered server-side rollout. Update the app, confirm Meta AI is on, and retry after 48–72 hours.

### Can Muse see me during a video call?
The feature uses your camera and microphone, and Meta processes that data per its AI terms. You control permissions at the OS level and can revoke camera access anytime in phone settings.

### When is the Muse Tamagotchi wearable coming out?
Meta has confirmed the device is in development but hasn't announced a release date or price. Video chat is the first public step toward it.

## Final Verdict

If you're a US user curious about AI companions, Muse video chat is worth five minutes of your time — it's free, built into apps you already have, and genuinely novel. Set it up now, customize your avatar, and keep your app updated so you're ready when the wearable arrives. If you're outside the US or on a restricted account, don't chase sketchy workarounds; wait for the official regional rollout. And regardless of where you live, adjust your Meta AI activity and privacy settings before your first call.
