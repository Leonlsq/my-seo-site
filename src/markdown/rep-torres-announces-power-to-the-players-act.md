## The Power To The Players Act Explained: What It Means for Your Game Library

Rep. Ritchie Torres has introduced the **Power To The Players Act**, a federal bill aimed at forcing game publishers to give buyers real ownership of their digital games instead of revocable licenses. If you have ever lost access to a game you paid for—or watched a publisher shut down a storefront and wipe your library—this legislation targets exactly that problem. The core takeaway: the bill would require clear disclosure of what you actually own, and it would push back on the "license, not a sale" model that lets companies revoke access at will. Here is what the bill does, what it does not do, and how to protect your library right now regardless of whether it passes.

## What the Power To The Players Act Actually Proposes

The bill is a response to years of high-profile delistings, server shutdowns, and license revocations. Below is a breakdown of the practical mechanics as currently framed:

- **Ownership disclosure:** Publishers would have to state plainly, at the point of sale, whether you are buying a game or licensing it.
- **Revocation limits:** Companies would face restrictions on revoking access to a game you purchased without cause.
- **Storefront shutdown protections:** If a digital storefront closes, publishers would need to provide a path for users to retain or download purchased content.
- **Refund and notice requirements:** Advance notice before delisting or server shutdowns that affect paid content.

### What the Bill Does NOT Do

| Claim | Reality |
|---|---|
| "It bans always-online games" | No—online-only titles can still require servers |
| "It forces publishers to run servers forever" | No—it targets access to purchased content, not perpetual multiplayer |
| "It applies retroactively to all past purchases" | Unclear—retroactivity is one of the biggest open questions |
| "It kills live-service games" | No—but it may change how shutdowns are handled |

## How to Protect Your Game Library Right Now (Before Any Law Passes)

You do not need to wait for Congress. These steps reduce your exposure to license revocation today:

1. **Audit your libraries.** Export your Steam, Epic, GOG, and console purchase lists so you have proof of purchase.
2. **Prioritize DRM-free stores.** GOG sells installers you can back up offline—those files cannot be revoked.
3. **Back up installers and saves.** Keep local copies of offline installers and save files on external drives or NAS.
4. **Screenshot receipts.** Store order confirmations for high-value purchases in case of disputes.
5. **Watch for delisting notices.** Publishers often announce removals weeks ahead—buy or download before the deadline.
6. **Prefer physical where it matters.** For single-player titles you replay, discs and cartridges remain the most revocation-proof format.

### Why This Matters More in 2026

The gaming industry has shifted hard toward digital. Physical console releases are increasingly limited-run, and PC is almost entirely digital. That means the "license not a sale" clause buried in every EULA now governs the majority of what you play—making the Power To The Players Act the first serious federal attempt to address it.

## Common Questions and Criticisms of the Bill

- **Enforcement is the hard part.** A US law cannot easily compel a foreign publisher to keep servers online.
- **"Cause" is undefined.** Without a clear definition, revocation limits could be toothless.
- **Retroactivity is contested.** Whether it covers games bought before passage is a major sticking point.
- **Industry pushback is likely.** Publishers argue perpetual access is technically and financially unfeasible for online titles.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### What is the Power To The Players Act?
It is a federal bill introduced by Rep. Ritchie Torres that would require game publishers to disclose ownership terms and limit their ability to revoke access to digitally purchased games.

### Does the Power To The Players Act make digital games permanently mine?
Not automatically. It would strengthen your rights and require disclosure, but the exact scope—especially for online-only games and past purchases—depends on the final text and whether it passes.

### Will this law bring back games removed from Steam or consoles?
Possibly for purchased titles if storefront-shutdown protections pass, but it would not force publishers to restore delisted games you never bought.

### Is the bill law yet?
No. As of 2026 it is proposed legislation and must pass both chambers of Congress and be signed before it takes effect.

### How do I protect my Steam library if a game gets delisted?
Download and install it immediately, back up the game files and saves locally, and keep your purchase receipt. Once installed, most single-player titles remain playable offline.

## Final Verdict

The Power To The Players Act is the most meaningful consumer-ownership push gaming has seen, but it is a proposal—not a guarantee. Treat it as a signal, not a shield. **Do this now:** back up your libraries, favor DRM-free and physical where you can, and keep your receipts. If the bill passes, you will be positioned to benefit; if it stalls, you will still own what you can actually control. Follow the bill's progress, but do not wait on Washington to secure your own collection.
