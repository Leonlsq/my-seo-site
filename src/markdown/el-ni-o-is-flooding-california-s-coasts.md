## How to Track California King Tides and Protect Your Property in 2026

El Niño is already pushing California's coasts past their normal waterlines, and the year's highest tides—the king tides—haven't even arrived yet. If you live, drive, or own property near the coast, the practical question isn't "is this happening?" but "how do I know exactly when my street, garage, or commute will flood?" This guide gives you the exact tools, timing windows, and a home-prep checklist so you can act before the water does. The short version: bookmark NOAA's tide predictor, know your local king tide dates, and prep your lowest entry points now—not the morning of the surge.

## Step-by-Step Guide: Track the Tides and Prep Before the Surge

### Step 1: Find Your Exact King Tide Dates
King tides occur when the sun, moon, and Earth align (new and full moons), amplified by El Niño's warmer, higher Pacific waters. Check these sources in this order:

1. **NOAA Tides & Currents** (tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov) — enter your nearest station ID. California key stations include:
   - **9414290** – San Francisco
   - **9410660** – Los Angeles
   - **9410170** – San Diego
   - **9411340** – Santa Barbara
2. **California King Tides Project** (california.kingtides.net) — publishes projected king tide dates and crowdsourced flood photos.
3. **Your county's public works alert page** — many Bay Area and SoCal counties post street-level flood advisories 24–48 hours out.

### Step 2: Read the Tide Chart Correctly
Most people misread tide charts and get caught out. Here's what actually matters:

| Term | What It Means | Why It Matters |
|------|---------------|----------------|
| **MLLW** | Mean Lower Low Water (chart baseline) | A +7 ft tide is ~7 ft above this line |
| **King tide threshold** | Typically +6.5 to +7 ft MLLW in CA | Coastal roads and lots begin flooding |
| **High tide window** | Peak ± 1–2 hours | Flooding peaks *before* and *after* the printed time |
| **Slack water** | Moment current stops | Safest window to move a car or cross a low road |

**Rule of thumb:** If the forecast shows **+6.5 ft or higher** at your station, assume low-lying roads, beach parking lots, and storm drains will flood—especially if it coincides with rain or onshore wind.

### Step 3: Prep Your Property (Do This 1–2 Weeks Early)
- **Clear storm drains** in front of your property—clogged drains turn a 6-inch puddle into a flooded intersection.
- **Move valuables up:** garage shelves, stored boxes, and electronics should sit at least **12 inches off the floor**.
- **Install a backflow valve** on ground-floor drains if you're in a flood zone (roughly $150–$400 installed).
- **Sandbags:** most cities offer free sand and bags at designated sites—grab them *before* the surge, not during.
- **Photograph everything** for insurance; standard homeowners policies often exclude flood damage (see FAQ below).

### Step 4: Plan Your Commute Around the Tides
Coastal highways (Highway 1, sections of I-5 near the bay, and low-lying beach roads) flood predictably. Cross-reference your route against the tide chart and shift your drive outside the ±2-hour high-tide window. Apps like **MyCoast** and local Waze flood reports help, but the tide table is the reliable source.

## Common Issues, Alternatives, and What You Should Know

**"My tide app and NOAA disagree."** Apps often use a different datum or a nearby station. Always trust the official NOAA station closest to you, and remember local wind and atmospheric pressure can add or subtract **0.5–1 ft** from predictions.

**"The flood receded—am I safe?"** Seawater intrusion can push through storm drains for hours after peak tide. Don't drive through standing water; 6 inches of moving water can stall a car, and saltwater destroys undercarriages and wiring.

**What about renters?** You can't install backflow valves, but you *can* request them from your landlord in writing and buy renter's insurance that explicitly covers flooding (standard renter's policies usually don't).

**Alternatives to sandbags:** reusable water-activated flood barriers (like Quick Dam) cost $30–$60 and store flat—worth it if you flood more than once a year.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### When are the highest king tides in California in 2026?
King tides cluster around new and full moons in winter. The California King Tides Project typically flags windows in **December, January, and February**, with the highest peaks often occurring in **January**. Check california.kingtides.net for your region's exact dates—they shift yearly.

### Does homeowners insurance cover El Niño flood damage?
Usually **no**. Standard homeowners and renters policies exclude flooding. You need a separate **NFIP (National Flood Insurance Program)** policy or private flood insurance—and there's typically a **30-day waiting period**, so buy before the season, not the week of the surge.

### What tide height floods California coastal roads?
Most low-lying coastal roads begin flooding around **+6.5 to +7 ft MLLW**, though this varies by location. Combined with rain or high surf, flooding can start lower. Always check your specific NOAA station's threshold.

### How do I report king tide flooding in my area?
The California King Tides Project accepts photo submissions through its website and app. Your photos help scientists and city planners map vulnerable areas—and can support future flood-mitigation funding for your neighborhood.

## Final Verdict

Don't wait for the headline-grabbing surge—**prep now**. Bookmark your local NOAA station, note the king tide dates on your calendar, clear your drains, and lift anything valuable off the floor. If you're in a flood zone, price out flood insurance *today* given the 30-day waiting period. The tides are predictable; the damage is optional.
